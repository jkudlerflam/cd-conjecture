# C31. Displaced seas and interactions of the central switch

## 1. Verdict

[heuristic, supported numerically at n=2,3,4] The fixed-pattern switch has an open weak-coupling c=n region at each of these three sizes. Two routes work. Arm asymmetry, a neutral-carrier phase and a uniform logical chemical potential remove every non-density quartic resonance in the computed Gaussian reference. Alternatively, with equal arms and zero phase, the interactions actually generated by the cell put pair transfer on the irrelevant side of its one-loop flow. These are infrared conclusions using local elimination and bosonization, not measured central charges.

[proved, limitation] This does not establish c=n for every n. The all-n displaced-center separation and the all-n sign of the relative stiffness remain unproved. Numerical results at three sizes cannot supply either finite-dimensional statement. The selector and mobile wrapper are also outside this calculation. Thus this switch is a stronger candidate, but not an established fixed-alphabet, all-n counterexample.

[proved] There is no finite-rank or scale contradiction. The symmetric leading hopping is definite for every n, with an explicit positive lower bound below. Every fixed-n hierarchy closes if an appropriate infrared target is supplied. The couplings may depend on n and are then fixed before taking the thermodynamic limit. No n-independent lower bound on the allowed coupling is obtained.

[proved] A correction to the proposed zero-field route matters. At the parameters used in the source README, staggered particle-hole symmetry leaves quartic umklapp resonances even when different flavors have different sea centers. A small uniform chemical potential removes this constraint. Distinct centers alone are insufficient.

## 2. Inputs, conventions and exact microscopic facts

[proved, accounting] The required background, assembly Section 7, switch-cell README, C26 result, infrared note and project memory were read. C8/C21/C22/C23 progress was also consulted. `model.py`, `model2.py` and `onebody.py` are local copies of the switch implementation, used for independent occupation-basis checks. All other scripts here are new. No external file, project STATE, selector, network resource or cluster was modified or used.

[proved, convention] Measure energies in units e_T=e_H=e_D=1. Take w_T=w_H=w=0.3, w_D=-w, tau_A=tau_B=tau, and kappa=1. The left-arm hopping multiplier is 1+epsilon and all neutral-carrier forward hops have phase phi. The logical chain through the first nontrivial orders is

\[
 H_{\rm log}=\lambda^2\tau^2\sum_j
 [c_j^\dagger T c_{j+1}+{\rm h.c.}+c_j^\dagger(M+\bar\mu I)c_j]
 +O(\lambda^2\tau^4,\lambda^3\tau^2).
 \tag{1}
\]

T and M in this report are coefficients with lambda and tau removed. The physical uniform data field is mu_c=lambda^2 tau^2 bar_mu. It is included as a small perturbation of the degenerate cell code, not in the unperturbed carrier denominator. The optional field already appears in the source rule table. Changing these three scalar couplings does not enlarge its d=48 frozen-pattern alphabet.

[proved] The carrier graph has S=3n+3 vertices and maximum degree three. Let w_* be its largest hopping magnitude and delta_0=1-3w_*. The hopping quadratic-form bound gives

\[
 H_{\rm cell}\ge(\delta_0-|\tau|)N_{\rm carrier}.
 \tag{2}
\]

Indeed, each edge hopping is bounded below by minus its magnitude times the two endpoint carrier projectors. At a data vertex the conversion acts on two disjoint two-state blocks, each with norm |tau|, and vanishes without a carrier. Summing proves (2). The no-carrier space is precisely the 2^n-dimensional code. For |tau|<delta_0 it is the complete kernel. The same estimate applies with arbitrary logical occupation and arbitrarily many carriers, not just to the numerical truncation. Onsite energy can be allocated to the edge and conversion terms to make each grouped local term positive and code-annihilating.

[proved] Every microscopic term conserves total fermion number. The field does too. Consequently no anomalous pairing operator is generated at any order.

### The additional symmetry at zero field

[proved] On cell j consider the canonical particle-hole transformation

\[
 \beta_x\mapsto(-1)^j\alpha_x^\dagger,\qquad
 \alpha_x\mapsto(-1)^j\beta_x^\dagger,\qquad
 c_a\mapsto-(-1)^j c_a^\dagger.
 \tag{3}
\]

It interchanges H and D carriers and fixes the no-carrier and T sectors. The neutral hopping operator beta_x^dagger alpha_x alpha_y^dagger beta_y is unchanged. Charged hopping is exchanged with a minus sign, compensated by w_D=-w_H. The two conversions are exchanged when tau_A=tau_B. Across a junction the staggered factors compensate the fermionic reordering sign of p_R^dagger p_L. Each centered density changes sign, so their product is invariant. This verifies the symmetry term by term, including nonzero neutral phase and unequal arm hopping.

[proved] Its consequence for the leading, purely Gaussian lambda^2 tau^2 Hamiltonian is

\[
 T=T^*,\qquad M=-M^T,\qquad h(\pi-k)=-h(k)^T,
 \quad h(k)=M+Te^{ik}+T^Te^{-ik}.
 \tag{4}
\]

Thus M is imaginary antisymmetric. With phi=0 it also is real, so M=0 at this order. At tau^4, particle-hole conjugation of normal-ordered quartics also produces quadratic contractions; one must not impose this constraint on the normal-ordered one-body coefficient alone. A root p has a partner pi-p modulo 2pi with the same velocity sign: differentiate the reflected negative eigenvalue to see this. When both right-moving and left-moving pairs occur, their sums are pi modulo 2pi. Their pair-to-pair umklapp is therefore resonant. This is the zero-field resonance seen in the n=2 example with displaced centers. A uniform field breaks (3).

[proved, scope] The symmetry statement is a local bulk statement; it can be implemented on even-cell rings. Boundary twists do not remove the bulk momentum constraint. At higher orders it also constrains longer-range hopping, rather than asserting that all hopping remains nearest-neighbor.

## 3. Finite reduction and the all-n rank proof

### Carrier-pair inversion

[proved] At tau=0, fix the logical occupation and the carrier types X,Y in two adjacent cells. The excited Hamiltonian is

\[
 A_{XY}=H_X\otimes I+I\otimes H_Y.
 \tag{5}
\]

It has dimension S^2. The one-carrier Hamiltonians have diagonal one and hopping -w exp(i phi) for T and +w for H,D, with the specified arm weights. Physical occupation-basis signs are included in the carrier source and conversion maps in `reduced.py`.

[proved] Each graph is a tree. A diagonal gauge makes all three one-carrier Hamiltonians the same real matrix K=I-w A, where A includes the arm weights. If depth is measured from the left port, the gauges are exp(i phi depth) for T and (-1)^depth for H,D. Hence every inverse in (5) is a diagonal-gauge transform of the single positive inverse

\[
 (K\otimes I+I\otimes K)^{-1}.
 \tag{6}
\]

This is the default sparse solve in the final scripts. The earlier spectral sum remains available as `method="spectral"`, but its cancellation error matters at n=4.

[proved] Let B=QVP, R_0=(QH_0(tau=0)Q)^(-1), and C=partial_tau H_0. Define X_0=R_0 B and X_r=R_0 C X_{r-1}. The required coefficients are

\[
 K_{2,2}=-X_0^\dagger C R_0 C X_0,
 \qquad K_{2,4}=-(C X_1)^\dagger R_0(C X_1),
 \tag{7}
\]
\[
 K_{3,4}=\sum_{r=0}^4 X_r^\dagger(QVQ)X_{4-r}.
 \tag{8}
\]

Subscripts denote powers of lambda and tau. Equation (7) is the Neumann expansion of -B^dagger R(tau)B. Equation (8) follows from B^dagger R(tau)V R(tau)B; the total number of minus signs is four. There is no canonical normalization subtraction until order lambda^4. For (8), the endpoint vectors have one carrier per cell. Components of V outside that sector have zero overlap with the other endpoint vector, so its projected use is exact.

[proved] Two distinct bonds do not contribute at order lambda^2 or lambda^3 on the bulk line. An outer cell on a bond used only once has zero code compression of the port operator. This remains true with arbitrary carrier conversions between junction events. Thus the cubic coefficient used here is the whole connected cubic payload interaction.

### Definite hopping for arbitrary n

[proved] At equal arms, zero phase and positive kappa, put d_a=n+1+a for a=1,...,n. This is the distance from either port to data vertex a. Let K_T=I-wA and K_H=I+wA and define

\[
 F_a(t)=\int_0^t
 (e^{-(t-s)K_H})_{pa}(e^{-sK_T})_{ap}\,ds.
 \tag{9}
\]

The two junction source orderings, with port amplitudes 1/sqrt(2) and 1/2, give

\[
 -T_{ab}={\kappa\over4}\int_0^\infty F_a(t)F_b(t)\,dt.
 \tag{10}
\]

The graph is bipartite, so (-1)^d_a F_a is positive for t>0. Its first nonzero Taylor term is

\[
 F_a(t)=(-1)^{d_a}e^{-t}
 {w^{2d_a}t^{2d_a+1}\over(2d_a+1)!}
 [1+O(t^2)].
 \tag{11}
\]

There is a unique shortest path. Expanding each propagator and integrating s^d(t-s)^d gives the factorial in (11). The first nonzero powers differ for every a, so no nontrivial linear combination of the F_a vanishes. Equation (10) is strictly positive definite. This proves full rank at every finite n, without inferring it from tiny numerical eigenvalues.

[numerical, independent integral check] `gram.py` evaluates (9)-(10) at n=1,2,3 by adaptive quadrature and recovers the coefficient 1/4 entry by entry. This is a consistency check of the source normalization, not the rank proof.

### An explicit lower bound

[proved] The following deliberately loose bound makes the n dependence explicit. Write m_a=2d_a+1, d_*=2n+1, and

\[
 Q_{ab}={1\over m_a+m_b+1},\qquad
 q={\det Q\over(\operatorname{tr}Q)^{n-1}}>0.
 \tag{12}
\]

The Cauchy determinant is

\[
 \det Q={\prod_{a<b}(m_b-m_a)^2\over
                 \prod_{a,b}(m_a+m_b+1)},
\]

and the eigenvalue product gives lambda_min(Q)>=q. There are at most 3^l walks of length l. After dividing (11) by its leading monomial, its positive remainder for 3wt<=1/2 is at most

\[
 3^{2d_*}\sum_{r\ge1}(r+1)(3wt)^{2r}
 \le4\,3^{2d_*}(3wt)^2.
 \tag{13}
\]

Choose

\[
 t_0=\min\left(1,{1\over6w},
 {q^{1/4}\over\sqrt8\,n^{1/4}3^{d_*}(3w)}\right),
 \qquad b_a={w^{2d_a}t_0^{m_a}\over m_a!}.
 \tag{14}
\]

On t=t_0 x, 0<=x<=1, the normalized columns differ from x^m_a in L2 operator norm by at most sqrt(q)/2. Their least singular value is therefore at least sqrt(q)/2. Restoring e^(-t), b_a and dt gives

\[
 s_{\min}:=\lambda_{\min}(-T)
 \ge {\kappa t_0 e^{-2t_0}q\over16}\min_a b_a^2>0.
 \tag{15}
\]

For delta=1-3w>0, |F_a(t)|<=t e^(-delta t), so also

\[
 s_{\max}\le{\kappa n\over16\delta^3}.
 \tag{16}
\]

These bounds are much worse than the observed spectrum. They prove positivity and finiteness, not a useful numerical bandwidth.

[proved] Simple, strongly separated eigenvalues can also be arranged for every n by reducing the common hopping w. After removing the diagonal factors w^(2d_a), (10) tends as w->0 to the positive matrix

\[
 G^{(0)}_{ab}={\kappa\over4}
 {(m_a+m_b)!\over m_a!m_b!\,2^{m_a+m_b+1}}.
 \tag{17}
\]

Dominated convergence is valid for w bounded below 1/3. Positive upper and lower bounds on this normalized matrix and the min-max principle sandwich its ordered eigenvalues between constants times w^(4d_a). Consecutive eigenvalue intervals become disjoint as w->0. In particular, at each n one can choose w so consecutive eigenvalue ratios are below 1/8. This is an existence proof with a computable finite-matrix criterion, not a claim that w=0.3 has this property for all n.

[proved] This also controls the centered reference apart from pair transfer. With bar_mu=s_min/16 its roots are +/-k_a, k_a=arccos(bar_mu/(2s_a)). For a nonzero integer combination with sum |m_a|<=4, the largest inverse eigenvalue present dominates the others by the ratio 1/8. Expanding arccos x=pi/2-x+O(x^3), with |O(x^3)|<=x^3 here, bounds every combination that was a multiple of 2pi at zero field away from it by at least bar_mu/(8s_max). Combinations initially at pi/2 or pi stay farther away. Consequently the non-pair quadratic and quartic momentum margin can be chosen at least s_min/(128s_max). Combinations with every m_a=0 include pair transfer and are excluded from this assertion. This argument does not displace the centers.

## 5. The centered alternative, including the generated interactions

### Conversion counting and extraction

[proved] At tau=0 the data are spectators, so every finite-cluster effective Hamiltonian is scalar on them. Conjugating by total data parity sends tau to -tau. Number conservation makes the effective Hamiltonian even under that conjugation. Its traceless part is therefore even in tau and starts at tau^2. Each conversion contains one data creation or annihilation operator. A normal-ordered quartic requires at least four conversions. Hence

\[
 H_{\rm nonGaussian}=O(\lambda^2\tau^4)
                    +O(\lambda^3\tau^4)+\cdots,
 \tag{19}
\]

while the lambda^3 tau^2 term is Gaussian. This is a proof for this carrier cell. It does not import C26's rho^2 statement, whose reflection construction is different. It also does not provide a thermodynamic convergence theorem.

[proved] To extract the quartic vertex, take matrix elements of (7)-(8) on the vacuum, one-particle and two-particle code sectors. Subtract the scalar and the quadratic operator determined by the first two sectors. Four conversions cannot contain a higher normal-ordered monomial, so the remaining two-particle matrix V_ij,kl determines the complete quartic operator

\[
 \sum_{i<j,k<l}V_{ij,kl}d_i^\dagger d_j^\dagger d_l d_k.
 \tag{20}
\]

For continuum modes alpha,beta,gamma,delta with two-cell wavefunctions u, the antisymmetrized amplitude is

\[
 \Gamma_{\alpha\beta;\gamma\delta}=
 \sum_{i<j,k<l}
 (u_{\alpha i}u_{\beta j}-u_{\beta i}u_{\alpha j})^*
 V_{ij,kl}
 (u_{\gamma k}u_{\delta l}-u_{\delta k}u_{\gamma l}).
 \tag{21}
\]

Here u_(a,r)=(U_a,exp(i r k_a)U_a), where U diagonalizes -T. Equation (21) includes the exchange subtraction and both sites of the bond. Dropping either would give incorrect forward couplings.

[numerical, independent check] At n=2, all 11 vacuum/one/two-particle columns were also evaluated in the literal 11,680-state occupation space. The initial reduced spectral extraction agreed with K_(2,4) and K_(3,4) to relative errors 1.01e-13 and 5.45e-14. The final positive-inverse implementation agrees to 9.53e-16 and 5.58e-16 relative, respectively. The n=3,4 extractions use 22 and 37 code columns. Their largest carrier matrix has dimension 225. No many-body central-charge estimator is used.

### Couplings and dimensions

[assumed, bosonization convention] Normalize rho_R,L=(partial_x phi minus/plus partial_x theta)/(2pi), and write the opposite-chirality interaction as sum_ab g_ab rho_aR rho_bL. Let J_ab multiply psi_aR^dagger psi_aL^dagger psi_bL psi_bR plus its adjoint. Equal arms and zero phase give equal left and right speed v_a. All coefficients below refer to that centered reference with bar_mu=0.3*s_min.

[proved within this convention] The Gaussian matrices are V_phi=diag(v)+(G4+G2)/(2pi), V_theta=diag(v)+(G4-G2)/(2pi). The equal-time stiffness solves K V_phi K=V_theta. Expanding this equation gives

\[
 (K-I)_{ab}=-{g_{ab}\over\pi(v_a+v_b)}+O(g^2).
 \tag{22}
\]

For l=e_a-e_b, the pair-transfer dimension is l^T K^(-1)l. Thus

\[
 \Delta_{ab}=2+x_{ab}+O(g^2),\qquad
 x_{ab}={g_{aa}\over2\pi v_a}+{g_{bb}\over2\pi v_b}
       -{2g_{ab}\over\pi(v_a+v_b)}.
 \tag{23}
\]

Positive x means that the corresponding relative-mode stiffness is below one in the equal-velocity limit. Equation (23), rather than a single scalar K, is the appropriate unequal-velocity criterion.

### One-loop flow, rather than only a tree-level dimension test

[heuristic, standard weak-coupling RG] Distinct centered widths leave pair transfer as the only non-density marginal channel once the other momentum mismatches have cut off. With f_ab=g_ab/[pi(v_a+v_b)], y_aa=0 and real symmetric y, the one-loop equations in this convention are

\[
 \dot f_{aa}=-{1\over4}\sum_{c\ne a}y_{ac}^2,\qquad
 \dot f_{ab}={v_av_b\over(v_a+v_b)^2}y_{ab}^2\quad(a\ne b),
 \tag{24}
\]
\[
 \dot y_{ab}=-(f_{aa}+f_{bb}-2f_{ab})y_{ab}
             -{1\over2}\sum_{c\ne a,b}y_{ac}y_{cb}.
 \tag{25}
\]

[heuristic, derivation] A same-flavor Cooper shell has coefficient 1/(2pi v_c); the mixed opposite-chirality shell has coefficient 1/[pi(v_a+v_b)]. The former renormalizes g_aa by -sum_c J_ac^2/(2pi v_c), and the latter renormalizes g_ab by +J_ab^2/[pi(v_a+v_b)]. Dividing by the definitions of f and y gives (24), the intermediate-flavor Cooper ladder gives the last term of (25), and (23) gives its linear term. Same-chirality forward terms change velocities but cancel from the first stiffness correction. Velocity feedback in these equations is beyond the retained order. For two equal-speed flavors these reduce to dot x=-y^2, dot y=-xy, which is the KT consistency check.

[numerical] `rg.py` integrates (24)-(25) with the measured leading vertices. Since f and y start at tau^2, it integrates their coefficients using u=tau^2 log(length). Radau integration to u=3, relative tolerance 2e-10 and absolute tolerance 1e-12, gives

| n | smallest x/tau^2 initially | smallest x/tau^2 finally | largest decrease of any x/tau^2 |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: |
| 2 | 9.08843 | 9.08836 | 0.0000763 |
| 3 | 16.5500 | 16.5492 | 0.001515 |
| 4 | 42.2787 | 42.2783 | 0.01184 |

[numerical] Every pair amplitude decays; the largest final coefficient is below 9e-14, 1e-15, 1e-15, respectively. The strict positive remaining x values put the terminal flow in a neighborhood attracted to the Gaussian fixed manifold. These integrations test the one-loop system, not exact microscopic dynamics.

[numerical] Direct enumeration at these centered targets, excluding only the pair-transfer identities, gives non-pair margins 0.150453, 0.000126249 and 1.95606e-7. There are exactly 1, 3 and 6 resonant pair-transfer comparisons, respectively.

[heuristic] Thus pair tunnelling is irrelevant on an open set within the number-conserving family near these centered targets at n=2,3,4. Choose tau small enough that the large coefficients in the table still give weak dimensionless interactions, then lambda small enough to control the Gaussian and quartic corrections. The other, non-pair quartics are cut off by distinct fillings; tau can be reduced further so their finite pre-cutoff running is small. Irrelevance of the pair terms is more than a positive bare dimension: (24)-(25) check that their feedback does not cross the KT boundary.

[proved, limitation] No positivity theorem for (23), or invariant irrelevant cone containing this cell's couplings for every n, has been established. The matrices in (7)-(8) are negative or mixed quadratic forms before normal ordering. Their operator signs do not determine the projected g_ab signs. Extrapolating the numerical sign pattern is a heuristic, not a proof of the missing all-n finite-dimensional inequality.

## 6. The hierarchy and physical energy scale

[numerical] At w=0.3, equal arms and zero phase the hopping spectrum is

| n | s_max | s_min | s_min/s_max |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: |
| 2 | 9.23759e-10 | 7.12307e-13 | 7.71096e-4 |
| 3 | 1.07876e-11 | 1.12137e-17 | 1.03950e-6 |
| 4 | 1.23813e-13 | 1.90825e-22 | 1.54124e-9 |

[numerical] The ratio drops by factors 0.00135 and 0.00148 in these steps. The largest hopping also falls by about 0.0115 per added flavor, so the absolute slow scale falls much faster than the ratio alone suggests. There is no demonstrated asymptotic law from three sizes. Equations (15)-(16) are the all-n statements.

[proved, units] The physical hopping scale and slow velocity scale are

\[
 W_{\max}=\lambda^2\tau^2\|T\|,
 \qquad v_{\rm slow}=\lambda^2\tau^2\bar v_{\min}.
 \tag{26}
\]

For a single cosine band its bandwidth is four times its hopping amplitude. The displaced reference reaches its fully detuned continuum regime below a conservative energy of order

\[
 E_{\rm osc}=\lambda^2\tau^2\bar v_{\min}
                 \min(\delta_2,\delta_4).
 \tag{27}
\]

This distinction matters when flavors have very different speeds. Reporting only lambda^2 or only the eigenvalue ratio omits both conversion visibility and propagation through the arms.

[proved, finite-cluster estimate] For a two-cell bond, b=||V||<=1+|kappa|/4=5/4. Equation (2) at tau=0 gives ||R_0||<=1/(2delta_0), while ||C||<=2 in the two-carrier sector. The Neumann series therefore converges for |tau|<delta_0. Counting the two conversions in the cubic term gives a traceless bound of order 3b^3 tau^2/(2delta_0^4); the even remainder after the leading quadratic term at order lambda^2 is bounded by a constant times b^2 tau^4/delta_0^5. These bounds deliberately discard the small propagation factors and so are extremely pessimistic.

[assumed, infrared transfer] As in C26 and ir_control, use local massive-sector elimination to obtain quasi-local continuum couplings, with finite matching constants C_n for each fixed cell. A finite isolated-band estimate on an entire ring does not supply this statement. The logical bandwidth is extensive and overlaps excited-cell bands on sufficiently large rings. All parameters must be fixed before the ring length tends to infinity.

[proved, conditional scale closure] For a displaced target with finite nonzero velocity and margins, choose a positive tolerance eta_n below its quadratic root-stability and RG thresholds. The generic requirements have the form

\[
 C_n\tau^2 <\eta_n\bar v_{\min},\qquad
 D_n|\lambda|<\eta_n\bar v_{\min},\qquad
 |\tau|<\delta_0/2,
 \tag{28}
\]

where C_n,D_n are finite. They can always be met at fixed n with nonzero tau and lambda. For the centered route, first require a strict irrelevant one-loop basin, then choose tau to keep all stiffness and pre-cutoff corrections small and lambda to preserve that basin. A vanishing s_min as n grows gives no contradiction at any fixed n, but does not supply the missing basin or momentum design.
