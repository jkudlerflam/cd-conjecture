## Why

c ≤ 1 for every nearest-neighbour qubit chain is derived on every symmetric class. On T and K_I it is conditional on (A_W), which agent C167 is working on in parallel. Do not work on (A_W).

On symmetry-free bonds N the derivation rests on C154's island assumption. (H) says every connected critical component of B_2 has a closure point in the controlled set. Its one-species form (A6) says that outside the controlled set a conformal point has a single boson or at most two Majoranas.

The closure lemma propagates c ≤ 1 inward from the controlled set. So (H) fails only for a c > 1 island disconnected from it. Kinematically, N allows two candidates:
- Ising × floating boson, at codimension 1 and any K;
- two floating bosons, at codimension 0, an open set.

C165 closed Ising × boson near the hidden Heisenberg point (to all orders in weak harmonics; the residual A_S at order-one harmonics is being tested on Typhon, so do not redo it). C160 closed the h_F corner, and C162 and C164 closed two-valley gases where a symmetry protects them, with a null search elsewhere. Sampling finds nothing: 12,000 uniform points plus about 150,000 adversarial evaluations, and every rising reading traced to the XZ Néel cat (REPORT §9.126).

The target is (H) or (A6) on N, derived at the physics standard, or a strictly narrower residual, or an explicit counterexample. Stay on this lemma.
