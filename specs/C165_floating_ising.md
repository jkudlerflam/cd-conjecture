## Why

In the symmetry-free stratum N, take an incommensurate (floating) c = 1 liquid with wavevector q, and let a momentum-π order parameter σ (staggered moment or dimerization) order continuously inside it. Translation protects σ, so the Ising transition costs one tuning. Every coupling of σ or ε to the boson's vertex operators carries momentum π + mq or mq, which is never zero for irrational q/2π, so it is forbidden. The uniform couplings that survive (ε ∂φ, ε(∂φ)², σ² terms) are marginal or irrelevant. The result is a decoupled Ising × boson, c = 3/2, on a hypersurface, at any K. It is C154's pattern P1 and would refute the d = 2 conjecture. What excludes it now:
- near h_F, C160's M2 (a minimizing classical texture has no zero mode at nonzero Bloch momentum, so no continuous period doubling);
- elsewhere, only C163's heuristic that a nearest-neighbour bond has no frustration, so spiral and staggered orders compete first order. Classically the energy of a "staggered cone", n_j = (sinθ cos qj, sinθ sin qj, (−1)^j cosθ), is linear in cos²θ for an XXZ + DM bond, so the two orders switch without coexisting.
Quantum fluctuations at lattice-scale couplings, or terms that curve that interpolation (anisotropies, fields, Γ terms that couple the in-plane spiral to the staggered component at second order), could change this. Nobody has tried to build it.

## Tasks

1. Mechanism. Derive the Landau theory of a coexisting phase (floating spiral plus staggered order) for a general nearest-neighbour bond, classically and with leading quantum corrections (spin-wave, or the large-K bosonization of the floating liquid coupled to a staggered Ising field). Identify which bond terms produce a positive quartic coupling between the two orders, i.e. curvature that allows coexistence rather than a first-order switch, and whether the coexistence region is entered through a continuous Ising transition with the floating liquid surviving on both sides. Check that the floating liquid's boson is not itself the staggered order (in the U(1) class Néel along the axis is cos 2φ of the same boson, so the construction must place the liquid's U(1)-like phase and the staggered order in independent channels). Use C159 Lemma A to make sure the liquid is truly floating (both fields incommensurate) in N, not half-floating.
2. Classical lattice search. On the classical chain, search N for bonds with a stable coexisting state of incommensurate spiral plus staggered component, and for a continuous classical transition into it (Hessian at Bloch momentum π relative to the lattice going soft while the spiral persists). Note that M2 forbids this only for continuum minimizers near h_F, and that a lattice twist map on S² has no Poincaré–Bendixson theorem.
3. Quantum test. For candidates, locate the transition with DMRG (C144's `dmrg.py`, C153's `vumps.py` and `fes.py`, C158's `fesfit.py`, all validated, two-site cells where the staggered order needs them), measure the staggered order parameter, the structure factor (an incommensurate peak plus a peak at π), and c from entanglement, with finite-entanglement scaling. Fix the criteria before looking: a c = 3/2 candidate needs entanglement fits above 1.3 that do not fall from L = 128 to 256, truncation below 1e-7, the staggered order vanishing continuously at the point, the incommensurate peak present on both sides, and an FES slope above 1.3 at χ ≥ 256.
4. Calibrate. Reproduce a known Ising × boson c = 3/2 point outside B_2 (for example a two-leg or two-site-cell model with a decoupled transverse-field Ising chain and an XX chain) with the same pipeline, and a c = 1 floating point inside B_2.
