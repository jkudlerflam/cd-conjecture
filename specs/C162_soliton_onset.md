## Why

C161 found an explicit K-stratum bond, v = (−1, −1 + με²/2, −1 + κε²/2, 0, 0, −ε, −hε², 0, 0) in est.py coordinates with κ = 0.05, μ = 2.3, h ≈ 0.02 to 0.03, small ε. Over a stable Z₂-symmetric uniform background its planar 2π soliton is tilt-unstable, and the isolated soliton has two degenerate minima at crystal momenta P = π ∓ 0.443π (Haldane, P = sΩ). This is the two-minimum mechanism of frustrated J1–J2 chains near saturation, realized by solitons of a nearest-neighbour one-site qubit bond. Near the onset the solitons form a dilute two-component gas. If it is immiscible, one valley wins (one boson plus Z₂ breaking, c = 1, or a first-order onset, as in C161's example where like tilts attract). If it is miscible, the two components are independent Luttinger liquids, and the relative phase carries momentum 2P₀, which is incommensurate, so no umklapp locks it. That would be a stable c = 2 phase, a counterexample to the d = 2 conjecture. C161 argued against miscibility only heuristically (monotone same-sign tails attract).

## Tasks

1. Effective theory of the dilute two-valley soliton gas. Derive it from the blow-up functional: soliton mass (the curvature of E(P) at each valley), and the soliton–soliton interaction for like and unlike valleys, both the classical tail interaction and the quantum two-body scattering data. In 1D at low density all interactions flow to hard core, so the miscibility question is decided by the subleading data (the difference of the 1D scattering lengths, or equivalently the sign of the effective exchange in the pseudospin sector). Use the dilute two-component Bose gas theory of Kolezhuk, Heidrich-Meisner, Greschner and Vekua (PRB 85, 064420 (2012)) and related work, and the statistics the solitons inherit (Berry phase, exchange). Account for the Eisenberg–Lieb type theorem (spin-independent interactions give a ferromagnetic pseudospin ground state for bosons) and say exactly what breaks it here.
