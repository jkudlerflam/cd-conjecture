## 0. Summary

The loophole is the second Majorana species B at XZ. A qubit bond near XZ has c_tot > 1 only if B reaches a phase with c_B > 1/2 while A stays critical (c_B = 7/10 or 3/2 would give 6/5 or 2), or c_B >= 3/2 with A gapped. The analysis below says this does not happen, for a structural reason.

1. [proved] Every P-even direction at XZ is an exact Majorana-ladder term (A hopping, B hopping, the YY plaquette a_j b_j, and A-B bilinears). The only quartic B interaction that second-order Schrieffer-Wolff can produce is the YY-induced one, -beta^2 sum K_jl b_j b_l. Its kernel K is a positive-semidefinite matrix (Lehmann) with K~(0) = 0 for critical A [proved] and its maximum at q = pi [numerical]. In the momentum channels of the B bond density the induced interaction is attractive everywhere and most attractive at q = pi.
2. [derived] Matched to Rahmani-Zhu-Franz-Affleck (RZFA), g_RZFA = 2 beta^2 K(2) > 0. That is the attractive side, whose phases are Ising (c = 1/2), tricritical Ising at g/t of about 250, and a dimerized gapped phase. The c = 3/2 Ising + Luttinger phase sits only on the repulsive side, -2.86 < g/t < -0.285.
3. [derived, mean field; numerical] The Lifshitz route to c = 3/2 needs the third-neighbour hopping generated by the Fock term to satisfy h3/h1 < -1/3 or h3/h1 > 1. The attractive kernel gives 0 < h3/h1 < 3/7 for the pure RZFA term (analytic), and h3/h1 <= 0.94 for the full kernel at every lam = t/beta^2 (hf_bmodel.out). The B chain dimerizes before this is reached (ED, lam = 0 to 0.5).
4. [derived, with C144 numerics] The RZFA tricritical point (the c_B = 7/10 route, c_tot = 6/5) would be at lam of about 6e-4. The log-divergent staggered tail of K drives dimerization at lam of order log(1/beta) instead (C144's wedge), and the A-mediated eps_B eps_B/|x - y| interaction is strongly relevant at a putative B tricritical point. The entry into the wedge is the Ashkin-Teller (XXZ-like) Kosterlitz-Thouless route, c_tot = 1 to 0.
5. [derived] The A/B-mixing directions (XX + ZZ, B_y) gap A at first order. With interactions the zero count of the hybridized band reaches 6 or 8 only if h3/h1 > 1 or < -1/3, which the attractive kernel excludes. The P-odd directions are relevant couplings of the A order and disorder fields (dimension 1/8). There is no controlled second-order B Hamiltonian there. YX/ZY form a period-3 twisted XYZ chain (C154), and the bisector field is analysed in section 4.
6. The bisector field (Kitaev chain in a [110] field, raised by C156). [proved] It is exactly a critical transverse-field Ising chain sigma whose bond signs are the B qubits g, with the field coupling the A order parameter to (1 + g^z) and flipping g only where sigma^z = +1. [numerical] Its slow sector is incommensurate and is not resolved at L <= 48. The RZFA calibration shows that a true c = 3/2 phase reads 1.24 to 1.69 at L = 128. On the bisector line the same estimators read 0.97 to 1.07 at L = 128 (h = 0.4, 0.45; C156's job). So c = 1 there, not above. The L = 192 and 256 runs (C157 job 17595396) are pending as a convergence check.

## 1. The P-even normal space at XZ is an exact Majorana ladder

[proved] With the C144 dictionary (ZX = -i beta_j beta_{j+1}, XZ = i alpha_j alpha_{j+1}, YY = a_j b_j, XX = i alpha_j beta_{j+1}, ZZ = -i beta_j alpha_{j+1}, Y_j = i beta_j alpha_j), the bond XZ + alpha ZX + beta YY + e_xx XX + e_zz ZZ + B_y (Y (x) 1 + 1 (x) Y)/2 gives the ring Hamiltonian

    H = sum_j [ a_j + alpha b_j + beta a_j b_j + i e_xx alpha_j beta_{j+1} - i e_zz beta_j alpha_{j+1} + i B_y beta_j alpha_j ].     (1.1)

This is a two-leg Majorana ladder with one interaction, the plaquette term a_j b_j = -alpha_j alpha_{j+1} beta_j beta_{j+1}. It is exact, not perturbative. So the full P-even neighbourhood of XZ is "two Majorana chains with a single four-fermion coupling and arbitrary single-particle mixing". Everything quartic in B at second order comes from the plaquette. The A/B-mixing bilinears contribute only quadratic B terms at second order (cross terms of a mixing bilinear and the plaquette are odd in alpha and vanish in the A ground state).

## 2. The second-order B Hamiltonian on the tuned hyperplane

### 2.1 A/B-preserving part (ZX, YY)

C144 section 3.1 derived, and validated against the full qubit ring at beta = 0.004,

    H_B = t sum_j b_j - beta^2 sum_{j != l} K_jl b_j b_l + const,      t = alpha + beta <a>_L,
    K_jl = <0| da_j Q (H_A - E_0)^{-1} Q da_l |0>_A,                                                  (2.1)

with the nearest-neighbour pairs cancelling exactly, so that in units of beta^2 (lam = t/beta^2)

    h_B(lam) = lam sum_j b_j - 2 sum_j sum_{r >= 2} K(r) b_j b_{j+r},      b_j b_{j+r} = -beta_j beta_{j+1} beta_{j+r} beta_{j+r+1}.     (2.2)

Couplings, explicit (kernel_q.out, L = 400 ring, critical A): K(0) = 0.18089, K(1) = -0.13583, K(2) = +0.07480, K(3) = -0.05096, K(4) = +0.03844, K(5) = -0.03076, K(6) = +0.02559, K(7) = -0.02187, K(8) = +0.01907, tail K(r) = (-1)^r/(2 pi r) + O(r^{-3}) (C144). So the B chain has no hopping on the tuned hyperplane, an RZFA term (r = 2) of strength 2 K(2) = 0.1496 beta^2, an O'Brien-Fendley term (r = 3, b_j b_{j+3} = Z X X + X X Z in the spin form of C144's bmodel.py) of strength -2 K(3) = +0.1019 beta^2, and an alternating 1/r tail.

Positivity. [proved] By the Lehmann representation K_jl = sum_{n != 0} <0|da_j|n><n|da_l|0>/(E_n - E_0), K is positive semidefinite. Hence its Fourier transform K~(q) = sum_r K(r) e^{iqr} is >= 0. [proved] At the XZ point, sum_l da_l = H_A - E_0 annihilates the A ground state, so sum_l K_jl = 0 and K~(0) = 0. [numerical, kernel_q.out] K~(q) rises monotonically from 0 at q = 0 (0.0041, 0.0171, 0.0406, 0.0784, 0.138, 0.238, 0.434 at q = k pi/8, k = 1 to 7) to its maximum at q = pi (1.85 at L = 400, growing like (1/pi) log L). The minimum eigenvalue of the L = 60 kernel matrix is 1e-17, consistent with positivity.

In terms of the bond-density Fourier modes b_q = sum_j e^{-iqj} b_j / sqrt(L), the quartic part of (2.2) is, up to a constant,

    -beta^2 sum_q K~(q) b_q^dagger b_q,                                                               (2.3)

attractive in every channel and most attractive at q = pi, the channel of the staggered bond density, which is the Ising energy operator eps_B of the B chain.

When A is gapped (dimerized with hoppings 1 +- d, kernel_q.out), positivity and the q = pi maximum survive, the tail becomes exponential, and K(2) stays positive: 0.0559, 0.0247, 0.0047 for d = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5. [numerical]

### 2.2 Matching to RZFA

RZFA (PRB 92, 235123, their Eq. 4) is H = sum_j [ i t g_j g_{j+1} + g g_j g_{j+1} g_{j+2} g_{j+3} ], with g < 0 called repulsive. With b_j = -i g_j g_{j+1}, this is -t sum b_j - g sum b_j b_{j+2}. Comparing with (2.2):

    t_RZFA = -t (a gauge choice: g_j -> (-1)^j g_j flips t and leaves every b_j b_l invariant),
    g_RZFA = 2 beta^2 K(2) = 0.150 beta^2 > 0,       g_RZFA / |t_RZFA| = 0.150 / |lam|.                (2.4)

So the effective chain lives on the attractive half of RZFA's phase diagram for every lam, and the ratio g/t covers (0, infinity) as lam goes from infinity to 0. RZFA's phases there (DMRG): Ising (c = 1/2) for g/t below about 250, tricritical Ising (c = 7/10) at g/t of about 250, a twofold dimerized gapped phase beyond. The c = 3/2 Ising + Luttinger phase (-2.86 < g/t < -0.285) and the fourfold CDW (g/t < -2.86) need g < 0. They are not reachable at second order around XZ. [derived]

The longer-range terms do not reverse this. In the momentum language (2.3), RZFA's repulsive term +|g| sum b_j b_{j+2} = |g| sum_q cos(2q) b_q^dagger b_q is repulsive at q = 0, pi and attractive at q = pi/2. The c = 3/2 phase is driven by that repulsion of the staggered bond density. A positive-semidefinite kernel can only produce -beta^2 K~(q) <= 0, and ours is most negative exactly at q = pi.

### 2.3 Hartree-Fock: the Lifshitz route is closed

For H = t sum b_j + sum_j sum_{r >= 2} U_r b_j b_{j+r} with a translation-invariant state, G_r = <i g_j g_{j+r}>, Wick gives

    <b_j> = -G_1,    <b_j b_{j+r}> = G_1^2 - G_r^2 + G_{r+1} G_{r-1},                                (2.5)

and the mean-field hoppings h_r = de/dG_r (single-particle band eps(k) = 4 sum_r h_r sin(kr)) are

    h_1 = -t + 2 G_1 sum_r U_r + U_2 G_3,    h_r = -2 U_r G_r + U_{r-1} G_{r-2} + U_{r+1} G_{r+2}  (r >= 2, U_1 = 0).      (2.6)

Any uniform Majorana chain has zeros at k = 0 and pi (c = 1/2). The c = 3/2 phase appears when a second pair of zero pairs enters (6 zeros). For eps = sin k + r sin 3k this needs r = h3/h1 < -1/3 (the velocity at k = 0 vanishes, RZFA's z = 3 Lifshitz point) or r > 1 (new zeros near k = pi/2).

[derived] Pure RZFA term (U_2 only). In the Ising state with s = sign(h_1), G_1 = -(2/pi) s and G_3 = -(2/(3 pi)) s, so

    h_3 = U_2 G_1 = -(2/pi) U_2 s,     h_1 = -t - (14/(3 pi)) U_2 s,     h_3/h_1 = -(2/pi) U_2 / |h_1|.     (2.7)

The Lifshitz condition h_3/h_1 = -1/3 needs U_2 > 0, i.e. g_RZFA = -U_2 < 0. Check: with t_B = -1 this gives the mean-field Lifshitz point at g = -0.29, RZFA's mean-field value (their DMRG gives -0.285). [numerical, hf_rzfa.out: zeros jump from 2 to 6 between g = -0.29 and -0.30.] For the attractive sign U_2 < 0, (2.7) gives h_3/h_1 = (2/pi)|U_2|/(|t| + (14/(3 pi))|U_2|) in (0, 3/7). The lam -> 0 value 0.4267 in hf_bmodel.out matches 3/7 = 0.4286.

[numerical, hf_bmodel.py] Full kernel, U_r = -2 K(r), r = 2 to 198, lam from -4 to 8, with critical A and with dimerized A (d = 0.05, 0.2): h_3/h_1 > 0 always, rising to 0.94 (critical A), 0.85 (d = 0.05), 0.64 (d = 0.2) as lam -> 0. The k = 0 slope v = sum_r r h_r keeps its sign (|v| >= 2.05 for critical A). The band has 2 zeros at every lam, c_MF = 1/2. The closest approach to the r > 1 branch is at lam -> 0, where the band flattens near k = pi/2. That is the nesting that the dimerized solution exploits instead (C144: <b_j> = +-(2/pi)(-1)^j at lam = 0).

[numerical, bsmall.out and C144 bscan] ED of h_B(lam) on rings of L = 24 to 40 Majoranas: lam = 0.1 and 0.25 have a twofold ground state and a finite gap (L (E - E0) = 29.6, 36.5, 43.0 and 53, 70, 87, linear in L), i.e. dimerized and gapped; lam = 0.5 to 1.5 gapped twofold (C144); lam >= 2 Ising to L = 40 (C144). No window shows the many-level tower of a c = 3/2 liquid.

### 2.4 The tricritical route and the wedge

On the attractive side the Ising-to-dimer transition of a short-range chain passes through the tricritical Ising point (c = 7/10), which with A critical would give c_tot = 6/5. Two facts close this route. [derived]

- Location. By (2.4) RZFA's tricritical point would sit at lam = 0.150/250, about 6e-4. Long before, at lam of order (1/pi) log(1/beta), the log-divergent staggered sum K~(pi) = (1/pi) log L - 0.0577 drives the B chain into the dimerized wedge (C144 section 3.2 and 3.3; the wedge width is 1.17, 1.02, 0.98 times beta^2 log(1/beta) at beta = 0.15, 0.3, 0.5). At that point g_RZFA/t is of order 0.1, deep in RZFA's Ising phase.
- Stability. The A-mediated interaction is the static eps_A eps_A correlator, an instantaneous eps_B(x) eps_B(y)/|x - y| coupling, of RG eigenvalue 3 - 1 - 2 Delta_eps(B). At the Ising point (Delta = 1) it is marginal (the log). At a tricritical point (Delta_eps = 1/5) it would be strongly relevant (eigenvalue 8/5), and its sign favours dimerization. Equivalently, the direct coupling beta eps_A eps_B has dimension 1 + 1/5 < 2 at Ising_A x TCI_B. So the decoupled Ising_A x TCI_B fixed point is unstable and is not the transition.

What the transition is, [heuristic, consistent with numerics]. In the A + B language the AT plane is the anisotropic Ashkin-Teller chain (C143: at alpha = 1 it is the XXZ chain with Delta = -beta). Outside the wedge, A and B form one c = 1 boson with a marginal coupling. The wedge is the phase where the translation-odd (Kramers-Wannier-odd) umklapp cos(sqrt(16 pi) phi) locks phi, so eps_A and eps_B order together. This is the XXZ Delta > 1 (Neel) phase in disguise, entered by a Kosterlitz-Thouless transition, with c = 1 on the critical side and 0 inside. The C144 L = 128 data (c fits fall smoothly from 1 to 0 across alpha/beta = 0.3 to 0.6, never above 1.09) fit this. The fine boundary scan at beta = 0.5 (alpha = 0.21 to 0.27, L = 64, 128, 192) is in Typhon job 17595396.

### 2.5 The A/B-mixing directions (XX + ZZ, B_y)

[proved, C144 section 1.3] The quadratic part of (1.1) has Bloch determinant det h(k) = (-4 sin k) eps_B(k) - 4 |w(k)|^2, with w(k) = e_xx e^{ik} + e_zz e^{-ik} - B_y and eps_B(k) = 4 alpha sin k. A is gapped at first order at k = 0, pi (gap 2|w(0)|, 2|w(pi)|), except on the rotation direction e_xx = -e_zz, B_y = 0. The free problem has at most 4 zeros (c <= 1).

[derived] With the plaquette added, replace eps_B by the Hartree-Fock band 4 sum_r h_r sin(rk). The zeros solve sin k eps_B(k) = -|w(k)|^2. For h_1, h_3 and real w = 2e cos k (B_y = 0), with s = sin^2 k this is the quadratic

    p(s) = -4 h_3 s^2 + (h_1 + 3 h_3 - e^2) s + e^2 = 0,     s in (0, 1).                                  (2.8)

Each root in (0, 1) gives 4 zeros. Two roots (8 zeros, c = 2) need p(1) = h_1 - h_3 to have the sign of p(0) = e^2 > 0 together with a negative minimum inside. With h_1 < 0 (the c = 1 side of the free problem) this requires h_3/h_1 > 1. With h_1 > 0 it requires h_3 < 0, i.e. h_3/h_1 < 0, and (at small e) h_3/h_1 < -1/3. Both are the Lifshitz conditions of 2.3, excluded for the attractive kernel (h_3/h_1 in (0, 0.94)). So mixing plus the plaquette gives at most one root, c_tot <= 1. A gapped by mixing also cuts the log tail of K (kernel exponential, K(2) and h_3/h_1 smaller: 0.64 at d = 0.2). [numerical check pending: XZ + alpha ZX + 0.3 YY + 0.1 (XX + ZZ), alpha = -0.15 to 0.19, L = 64 and 128, job 17595396.]

### 2.6 The P-odd directions (YX, ZY, B_x, B_z)

[derived, C144 section 1.4] X_j = S_j beta_j and Z_j = S_j alpha_j carry Jordan-Wigner strings, i.e. A order or disorder fields (dimension 1/8) times B strings. Their second-order kernel int dtau <sigma_A sigma_A> grows like L^{3/4}: there is no second-order B Hamiltonian, A acquires a mass m_A ~ eps^{8/15}, and any emergent B dynamics is of order eps^2/m_A ~ eps^{22/15} or smaller. For B_x or B_z alone the strings commute and both sectors gap (C144). YX and ZY: C154 found an exact period-3 twisted XYZ form (XZ + eps YX + eps' ZY has J = diag(1, eps, eps') P with P the cyclic permutation of axes), so the c of about 1 slow sector of C144 is a Baxter-type XYZ question, left to C154. The bisector field, the one P-odd direction with a reported critical line, follows.

## 4. The bisector field (Kitaev chain in a [110] field)

### 4.1 Model

C156 showed that the antiferromagnetic Kitaev chain in a [110] field (Sorensen, Gordon, Riddell, Wang and Kee, PRR 5, L012027 (2023), arXiv:2209.06221) is the uniform chain K sum S^x_j S^y_{j+1} - (h/sqrt2) sum (S^x + S^y). In the XZ frame with Pauli normalization this is

    H = sum_j X_j Z_{j+1} + B sum_j (X_j + Z_j)/sqrt2,      B = 2h/K,                                        (4.1)

with the field along x' = (x + z)/sqrt2. [proved, elementary] The pi rotation about x' combined with the site reflection j -> -j maps X_j Z_{j+1} to itself and fixes the field, and the field components along y or z' are odd under it. So (4.1) is the unique symmetric field direction. This fits Sorensen et al.'s finding that phi != 45 degrees or theta_z != 0 opens a gap. They report the line as gapless for h < h_c1 = 0.511 K (B < 1.022), "difficult to establish numerically", with no central charge, and a twofold soliton phase with staggered vector chirality for 0.511 < h < 0.726. In the XZ frame the vector chirality (S_j x S_{j+1})^y is b_j - a_j, so that phase is the same translation-breaking order as C144's wedge.

### 4.2 Exact two-qubit-cell form

[proved; block_check.out, operator identity to 1e-14 on rings L = 8, 10] Apply a Hadamard on even sites (XZ -> the compass chain X_{2i-1} X_{2i} + Z_{2i} Z_{2i+1}, the field stays along x + z) and group sites (2i - 1, 2i) into cells. Then

    tau^x_i = X_{2i-1} X_{2i},  tau^z_i = Z_{2i-1},  nu^z_i = Z_{2i-1} Z_{2i},  nu^x_i = X_{2i}

are two commuting qubits per cell. The A chain is H_A = sum tau^x_i + tau^z_i nu^z_i tau^z_{i+1}, with nu^z on the links and conserved Gauss operators G_i = nu^x_{i-1} tau^x_i nu^x_i (the flat B sector). Define

    sigma^z_i = tau^z_i nu^z_i tau^z_{i+1},  sigma^x_i = nu^x_i,  g^z_i = G_{i+1},  g^x_i = tau^z_{i+1}.

These are again two commuting Pauli algebras per cell (checked), and

    H = sum_i [ sigma^z_i + g^z_i sigma^x_i sigma^x_{i+1} + (B/sqrt2) ( sigma^x_i (1 + g^z_i) + g^x_i (1 + sigma^z_i) ) ].      (4.2)

At B = 0, sigma is a critical transverse-field Ising chain (A) whose bond signs g^z_i are conserved, with a spectrum independent of them on open chains (the 2^{L/2} flat B sector). The field does two things. The term sigma^x_i (1 + g^z_i) is a longitudinal field on the A order parameter, present only on cells with g^z = +1. The term g^x_i (1 + sigma^z_i) flips the B qubit only where the A transverse spin is excited (sigma^z = +1). In the critical A ground state <(1 + sigma^z)/2> = (1 - 2/pi)/2 = 0.18. A g flip changes the sign of an A bond, which in a fixed A gauge is the insertion of the A disorder string, so the second term is g^x (x) mu_A. The field is therefore h [ sigma_A (1 + g^z) + c mu_A g^x ]: it couples the A order and disorder fields (both of dimension 1/8, strongly relevant) to two non-commuting components of the B qubit. [derived]

Consequences. [heuristic] (i) With only one of the two terms, B is classical and A gaps (this is B_x or B_z alone, C144). (ii) With both, B cannot polarize along both g^z and g^x, the relevant A couplings are frustrated, and the first-order A gap is cancelled on average. That is why the emergent scale is so small (C156: gaps of order 1e-3 K at N <= 16). (iii) Under translation by one qubit (A's Kramers-Wannier map) sigma_A and mu_A are exchanged, so the symmetric line is self-dual. A self-dual line is either critical or spontaneously breaks translation. The soliton phase is the latter, and the gapless line the former. A plain self-dual Ising transition of A with B gapped would have c = 1/2. The incommensurate oscillations seen numerically (period 8 to 10 sites at B = 0.9, L = 32) point instead to a slow Luttinger-type sector with c = 1, and possibly to A + LL with c = 3/2. The decision is numerical.

## 5. Answer to the task questions

Task 1 (effective B Hamiltonian). Sections 1, 2.1, 2.5, 2.6 and 4.2. On the A/B-preserving part of the tuned hyperplane the B chain is (2.2), with explicit couplings and an alternating 1/r tail, and with exponential tails once A is gapped. On the mixing directions it is the hybridized two-band problem plus the plaquette. The P-odd directions have no second-order B Hamiltonian. For the bisector there is an exact two-qubit-cell form (4.2).

Task 2 (phase diagram of B). Section 3: Ising, dimerized gapped, and the lam = 0 staggered critical point. No phase with c_B > 1/2.

Task 3 (image of qubit directions). The qubit directions map onto the attractive half of RZFA (g_RZFA = 0.150 beta^2 > 0) plus a positive-semidefinite long-range tail, at every ratio lam. Mixing only adds bilinears and gaps A. No direction reaches c_B > 1/2 at second order.

Task 5 (why the loophole closes). The B chain's only interaction is induced by virtual A excitations. Second-order perturbation theory makes it -beta^2 sum K~(q) |b_q|^2 with K~ >= 0, peaked at the staggered channel q = pi. The c = 3/2 phase of interacting Majorana chains needs repulsion in that channel (g_RZFA < 0, Fock h_3/h_1 < -1/3). The tricritical point needs g/t of about 250 with short-range terms, but the log tail of K dimerizes the chain at g/t of about 0.1. So the second species at XZ is real, but its self-interaction has the sign that orders it (dimerization) rather than the sign that would split its Fermi points. Caveats: (a) this is a statement about second order around XZ, and at finite beta higher orders could change the sign of the induced r = 2 coupling. The C144 full-chain DMRG to beta = 0.5 found no c > 1.09. (b) It does not cover the P-odd directions, where B dynamics are non-perturbative. Of those, the bisector (the only one with a reported critical line) reads c = 1 at L = 128 (section 4.3), and YX/ZY are with C154.
