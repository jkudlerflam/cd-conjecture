## Verdict

The ladder picture gives a derived obstruction in every regime with a separation of scales except one. In that one it produces a mechanism that evades the obstruction, and the effective model there reads c ≈ 2 at the sizes reached. So the result is a candidate, not a closure.

- Obstruction [derived]. With rungs only, intra-leg motion is second order. A second band valley needs the first-order rung harmonic to be no larger than the second-order one it generates. That fails, so there is one valley and no second single-particle species, (i) whenever the first-order harmonic dominates (Lemma S, |t_1| > 8|t_2| + 2Σ r²|t_r|), (ii) at large field (gapped), (iii) in the classical spiral regime (the rungs lock the relative leg phase at first order, and with that coupling tuned away the classical legs have no intra-leg coupling at all), and (iv) in the dilute part of the one remaining controlled weak-rung regime, where the blockade makes the two valleys impenetrable and therefore immiscible, except in a wedge around a symmetric sub-stratum.
- Evading mechanism [derived], with a c = 2 reading [numerical, not converged]. Near the Fibonacci point (J ≈ J_z ZZ, B ≈ 2J_z ẑ), with the first-order transverse hop J_x + J_y + 2iD tuned to order Δ²/V (Δ = J_x − J_y, V = 4J_z), the pairing Δ acts only virtually across the Rydberg blockade and generates a leg hop t_2 = −Δ²/(2V). The chain becomes a zigzag ladder whose legs are coupled only by the blockade and by generated terms. At J_x + J_y = 0 it is a frustrated J1–J2 chain in disguise. The weak-rung regime that rung-only chains were thought to lack exists here, controlled by Δ/V. The effective model reads c = 2.13 (L = 48 → 72) and 1.93 (48 → 96) at the dilute symmetric point, against 1.88 for a c = 2 control and 0.90 for a c = 1 control, but L = 72 and 96 are not converged.

## Key claims

1. [proved] A nearest-neighbour chain is a zigzag ladder with rung couplings only, and translation exchanges the legs. "Two independent leg liquids" means one band with two occupied valleys (PROOF 1).
2. [proved] Lemma S: a band 2|t_1| cos(k − α) + R(k) with |R′| < |t_1|/2 and |R″| < |t_1| has exactly one minimum (PROOF 2).
3. [proved from C143's isolation results] The controlled weak-rung points (first-order band flat) are large field (gapped), XZ (C157, c ≤ 1) and the Fibonacci point. The ferromagnetic Heisenberg point is not one of them (PROOF 2).
4. [derived, checked numerically] The Fibonacci cusp model (PROOF eq. 3.2): blockade, hop w, leg hop t_2 = −Δ²/(2V), leg interactions V_2 = 2t_2 and V_3 = (2/3)t_2. Checked against the full L = 12 chain to 1.2e-2, 1.5e-3, 1.9e-4 at V = 20, 40, 80 (error ∝ V⁻³). At w = 0 each leg is the ferromagnetic Heisenberg chain exactly, and the model is C153's large-b staggered-charge box (c = 1 by the resonant conversion vertex). For complex w the two valleys are incommensurate and every conversion vertex carries momentum.
5. [observation] C152 PROOF 7 (strong repulsion, "hard rods are one species") assumes |t| ≫ Δ²/V and does not cover the cusp |t| ≲ Δ²/V. C141 and C143's "Fibonacci stratum is single-species" covers PXP and U(1) hopping, not pairing-generated leg hopping. Both are gaps, located in PROOF 3.
6. [derived] Dilute cusp: inter-valley scattering is impenetrable (generic 1D threshold law), so by Girardeau a second valley gains no kinetic energy and costs the valley offset. Add C164's interface cost for unequal masses and the gas fills one valley, except in a wedge |δ| ≲ J_⊥(n) ~ n³, |η| ≲ n^{1/2} around the J_x + J_y = 0 sub-stratum, where the valley-pseudospin sign decides (PROOF 4).
7. [derived] Classical spiral: one phason (PROOF 6). The cusp's leg hopping is a quantum effect of relative size ħ_eff and is invisible to semiclassics. This is consistent with C160.
8. [numerical] Effective-model DMRG (PROOF 5): every point away from φ ≈ π/2 is c ≤ 1 (one valley at low density, gapped commensurate or CDW states at higher density, a first-order jump into the CDW at small |w|). At φ = π/2, n = 0.17, the readings are S̄ = 1.646, 1.790, 1.869 at L = 48, 72, 96. They persist unchanged to φ = 1.56 and stay high at φ = 1.54, with S = 0.82 (one valley) by φ = 1.5. Ring ED shows a valley-balanced ground state (the pseudospin is not ferromagnetic).
9. Candidate bond (centre of the wedge, V/Δ = 40), in h = J_x XX + J_y YY + D(XY − YX) + J_z ZZ + (h_z/2)(Z⊗1 + 1⊗Z): J_x = 0.5, J_y = −0.5, D = 0.00625, J_z = 10, h_z = −19.97625. Its energy scale is |t_2| = 0.0125. It sits in the parity class on the J_x + J_y = 0 sub-stratum. The c = 2 region, if real, is open there, and it stays open in B_2 off the parity class because the parity-odd terms couple only to off-resonant vertices (PROOF 5.4) [derived, conditional on the c = 2 reading].

## Answer to the coordinator's note (C171 R3, R4)

- R3 (composites with wavevectors in Zθ + Zπ: n ≥ 3, n = 0, the 2Q_1 + π branch) is not covered in any regime. The ladder argument constrains valleys of the one-particle band. A composite has its own n-body band, which Lemma S does not see.
- R4 (repulsive host, θ-commensurate second boson with small K_2) is covered where the second boson would be a second single-particle valley, in the strong-rung regime (Lemma S) and in the dilute cusp off the symmetric wedge (impenetrability). Neither argument uses incommensurability. It is not covered when all couplings are of order one, or for composites. In T (B = 0) the Fibonacci point is absent, so the ladder route offers no mechanism for R4 in T. The commensurate-valley cusp (φ = 0, valleys at 0 and π, hard-rod K = (1 − n)² small) is a microscopic home for R4 in the parity class and in N. It reads c = 1.15 at n = 0.25 (48 → 96), i.e. one boson there.
- So the windows of R3 and R4 stay open in general. The concrete new danger this run found is not in them. It is incommensurate (pattern P3) and sits near the Fibonacci point at strong field.
