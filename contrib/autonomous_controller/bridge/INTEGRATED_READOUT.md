# One autonomous table including endpoint readout

**The blank-to-program bridge and endpoint readout now run as one
explicit finite reversible controller.** This extension starts with blank
dynamic tracks, computes the length parameter, creates and executes the
binary-digit quantum program, exposes its two selected quantum records
at the physical endpoints during safe READY intervals, restores those
records, and reverses the entire program and initialization. Every phase
handoff is a local collision entry.

The program remains the demonstration from [README.md](README.md), not
the sparse high-channel encoding circuit. No central-charge claim follows
from this construction. The main gain is eliminating separately supplied
program and readout-phase handoffs in a concrete end-to-end example.

## 1. Static grammar and reused tracks

For `n >= 4`, the static tape has the form

```text
LEFT OUT_L BULK ... BULK OUT_R RIGHT
```

`OUT_L` and `OUT_R` are therefore the first and last interior sites. There
may be zero BULK sites. During length computation, copying, and quantum
dispatch, both output marks behave exactly as BULK. All four dynamic bits
(counter, frontier, program, cursor) start at zero, and the single fresh
head starts on LEFT in `F:ADVANCE`. These are initialization contracts;
this finite controller alone does not supply their Hamiltonian penalties.

During readout only, the cursor bit is reused as READY at the endpoints.
This costs no extra bit: the program dispatcher uses cursor marks only on
interior instructions and clears every such mark before readout begins.
The readout controls never set a cursor bit on an interior site. Before
adjoint dispatch resumes, both endpoint READY bits are cleared.

The grammar has a local nearest-neighbor characterization once the two
boundaries are fixed: LEFT must be followed by OUT_L, OUT_L by either
BULK or OUT_R, BULK by BULK or OUT_R, and OUT_R by RIGHT. Unique placement
of the output marks is therefore not an arbitrary program supplied by
hand. Instantiating the initialization/clock penalties remains separate.

## 2. Literal composition

[integrated_readout.py](integrated_readout.py) takes the base bridge's
prescribed local rules, treats both output marks as BULK, and removes its
single forward-to-adjoint turnaround. It inserts the eight controls of
the [endpoint transport primitive](../readout/transport.py) and two travel
controls:

1. When forward DISPATCH reaches RIGHT, enter TO_READOUT and move left to
   OUT_L without changing any quantum record.
2. Enter the readout machine at OUT_L. Swap its quantum record left to
   LEFT and raise READY there. Travel to OUT_R, swap its quantum record
   right to RIGHT, and raise READY there.
3. Clear READY at LEFT **before** moving that record back to OUT_L.
   Similarly clear READY at RIGHT **before** returning its record to
   OUT_R. All swaps are ordinary whole-space two-site unitaries.
4. Replace the readout machine's closing cycle edge at OUT_L with
   FROM_READOUT. Move right to RIGHT, then enter `R:DISPATCH` and execute
   the base bridge's literal adjoint and cleanup.

The exact intermediate controller states are generated by the collision
table. The host simulator does not alter a phase, record, opcode, or head
position at these handoffs. Counter and program bits remain fixed during
readout. All quantum records are restored to their post-program locations
before the first adjoint program gate.

The active local endpoint operator is

\[
R_\ell=P_{\rm READY=1,\,no\ head}\otimes O_\ell.
\]

The no-head condition matters at the microsteps where a quantum record
is swapped and the READY bit has not yet been changed by the subsequent
on-site collision. At every intermediate `G` or `F` vertex, an active
endpoint carries exactly the selected prepared output record. This
property does not rely on looking only at completed sweeps.

## 3. Actual finite table and resources

There are five static marks and four dynamic bits, so `B=80`. The base
bridge supplies 30 controls, readout supplies eight, and travel supplies
two, so `K=40`. Hence

\[
\boxed{(1+2K)B=6480}
\]

controller states per site, or **12,960** including one neutral quantum
qubit per site. This is the dimension of this controller demonstration;
it excludes a history clock and is not a proposed violating value of
`d`. The dimension is independent of tape length.

[integrated_transition_table.json](integrated_transition_table.json)
contains all **3200** local collision entries. **497** prescribed entries
implement the algorithm. Completion preserves static marks on every
local input, including malformed ones. All finite local domain/range
collisions are checked before completion.

[local_gates.py](local_gates.py) also provides literal basis-column
evaluators for the complete one-site `F`, two-site `G`, and a LEFT seed
involution. Its public basis has 12,960 states; the two-site matrix has
167,961,600 rows and columns, represented by sparse columns rather than
materialized densely. `G` is the identity on zero-head and two-head
sectors. Its single-head blocks move the head with the chosen oracle or
adjoint and preserve both neutral symbols. `F` is defined using the full
collision permutation and its inverse.

The seed involution exchanges an ordinary record with a fresh
`F:ADVANCE` head **only on static LEFT**, preserving every dynamic bit and
the quantum record. It acts this way for all dynamic payloads, not only
the desired blank one, so it is a whole-space unitary. The separate
`initial_allowed` predicate accepts exactly the ordinary, dynamically
blank states (with any quantum record and any static symbol). This API
allows an initializing clock to pin before seeding. The seed must be
placed on the clock's appropriate transition; applying it manually in a
simulator would not establish Hamiltonian initialization.

For output marks next to the endpoints, readout applies exactly four
SWAP gates. Its head takes `4(n−1)` moves, and travel into/out of readout
takes `2(n−2)` additional moves. Therefore the complete cycle takes

\[
\boxed{T_{\rm cycle}=2\bigl(n^2+8n-11-2\operatorname{popcount}(n-1)\bigr)
+6n-8.}
\]

This counts machine moves, each implemented by a spatial `G` sweep and
an on-site `F` sweep. It does not yet count an initialized Hamiltonian
clock's vertices or determine that clock's readout weights.

## 4. Independent finite checks

Run:

```sh
python3 contrib/autonomous_controller/bridge/integrated_readout.py
python3 contrib/autonomous_controller/bridge/integrated_checks.py
python3 contrib/autonomous_controller/bridge/local_gates_checks.py
```

[integrated_results.json](integrated_results.json) records:

- every local collision and inverse, and the entire 6480-state classical
  basis of the fresh/updated involution;
- all 125 lengths `n=4,...,128`, **1,590,466 total moves**, with no
  unspecified local rule encountered and exact final cleanup;
- endpoint READY safety at every local `G` and `F` vertex for
  `n=4,...,24`, **535,782 vertices** in total;
- an arbitrary coherent four-dimensional input code on six quantum
  sites, with the prepared output computed independently;
- agreement, at every active vertex, between the endpoint raising
  operator's pullback and the intended prepared output operator, with
  maximum entry error below `3.2e−17`;
- full quantum cleanup error below `3.2e−17`;
- a negative control showing that omitting READY changes the averaged
  endpoint operators substantially.

[local_gates_results.json](local_gates_results.json) separately checks all
12,960 local basis states of `F` and the seed, 153,600 one-head columns of
`G` across every control/phase and all left neutral symbols, every
zero-head column, and 25,600 two-head control/phase/quantum cases. The
right neutral symbol is sampled at three anchors in the one-head checks;
both neutral factors are spectators by construction. A complete six-site
cycle also runs through **1826 literal sparse local-gate applications**,
develops a quantum superposition, and returns exactly to its initial
basis state. This supplements the vector and controller simulators with
a test of the actual public full-space gate API.

In the six-site coherent check there are 1826 sampled local-gate
vertices, of which 99 are active for each endpoint and 40 have both
endpoints active. The resulting uniform sample weight is `99/1826` for
each endpoint. These are weights for this listed gate sequence, **not**
asserted weights for the final initialized Hamiltonian history. The
operator-safety statement holds pointwise and survives insertion of idle
clock vertices; the actual weights must be recomputed for that clock.

The tests do not classify the energies of arbitrary malformed global
configurations. The complete local gates are well-defined unitaries on
them. A Hamiltonian proof must still enforce initial blankness, static
grammar, one head, the intended history sector, and any required input
code. Replacing the demonstration program by the actual sparse-circuit
generator and specializing the two output records to the fermionic ports
are the essential high-channel tasks still outstanding.
