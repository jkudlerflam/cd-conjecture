## A useful normalization for comparing qubit candidates

Consider the one-site-invariant nearest-neighbour family, with Pauli
normalization and fixed pairing scale,

```
H = sum_j [(XX-YY)/2 + D(XY-YX) + (V/4)ZZ] + h_z sum_j Z_j,
D = kappa/(4V),   h_z = -V/2 + a/V.
```

The general displayed form has four independent real coupling structures
before fixing the overall scale and six nonzero Pauli monomials: XX, YY,
XY, YX, ZZ and Z. A particular one-parameter prescription is a smaller
parameter set within that family; it is not proof that all four physical
structures can be deleted or replaced by one operator.

**[Exact coefficient arithmetic.]** For the illustrative prescription
kappa=a=1 and V>=2, the largest absolute coefficient is V/2-1/V and the
smallest is 1/(4V). The ratio is consequently

```
R = 2V^2 - 4.
```

| V | D | J_z | h_z | Coefficient ratio R |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 40 | 1/160 | 10 | -799/40 | 3196 |
| 16 | 1/64 | 4 | -127/16 | 508 |
| 8 | 1/32 | 2 | -31/8 | 124 |
| 4 | 1/16 | 1 | -7/4 | 28 |
| 2 | 1/8 | 1/2 | -1/2 | 4 |

This table gives a transparent simplicity target, **not evidence that any
of these rational points has c>1**. Even the first row is not exactly the
production field of the existing candidate; its actual a is close to one
and must be read from the saved source. Reducing V also weakens the
perturbative control that motivated the original mechanism, so the phase
and convergence must be checked rather than presumed to survive.

The comparisons remove a common energy scale and irrelevant constants.
They do not conceal small terms in a large identity or claim that an
emergent low-energy scale equals the bare coefficient ratio. Evidence,
number of structures, coefficient spread and tuning tolerance should be
reported separately when choosing the next preferred Hamiltonian.
