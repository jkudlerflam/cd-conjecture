## The question

C141 reduced the physics-level derivation of C(2) < infinity to local boundedness of A = sum_a c_a v_a at every point of the compact space B_2 of qubit bonds modulo gauge, scale and on-site unitaries (dimension d^4 - 2d^2 = 8). Regular bonds (a gapped unique or finitely degenerate ground state, or a z = 1 CFT reached at a finite crossover length) are harmless by the weak-perturbation c-theorem (C141 PROOF 5.2). Hidden fast towers can accumulate only at singular bonds h*, that is, gapped with an extensively degenerate ground manifold, or gapless with z > 1. C141 PROOF 6.1 lists three singular strata at d = 2:

1. the classical Fibonacci point h = J ZZ + b(Z1 + 1Z) with J > 0, b = +-J (ground space the Fibonacci shift, entropy log phi), and its images;
2. ferromagnetic frustration-free points (Bravyi–Gosset, arXiv:1503.04035: a frustration-free nearest-neighbour qubit chain is gapped unless the projector is onto an entangled state, the twisted ferromagnetic Heisenberg case with z = 2 magnons), including field-driven saturation points;
3. quadratic Lifshitz points (XY with gamma = 0, |h| = 1), z = 2.

It then argues that each stratum has a single emergent species, with c <= 1 and emergent velocity v_em = O(delta^alpha M), and that nesting depth is finite. The step with the least support is completeness of this list at strong coupling. The only evidence is that no other example is known and the sampled survey found none.

## Tasks

1. Make "singular" precise in a form you can test. Give an operational criterion on a finite ring (for example, the finite-size gap times L tends to 0, or the number of states within O(M/L) of the ground state grows faster than any CFT allows, or the ground-state degeneracy grows exponentially in L at a gapped point) and say which of these C141's argument actually uses.
2. Attack completeness analytically. Organize B_2 by symmetry: generic bonds have no continuous symmetry, the U(1) class (XXZ + z-field + z-DM, fully controlled by Bethe ansatz in C129 to C140), the XYZ family with uniform field, and the remaining components such as the Kitaev-type and DM-type terms after the on-site rotation. For each family decide, with references, where gapless points with z != 1, or extensively degenerate gapped points, can occur. Use exact results where they exist (Bethe ansatz for XXZ in a field, the free-fermion XY-in-field family, frustration-free classification, the classical bonds, the known exactly solvable points of XYZ in a field such as the Kurmann–Thomas–Müller factorization line). Pay attention to the cases most likely to be missed: XYZ points where the factorized ground state becomes degenerate, multicritical points where two transition lines meet, ferromagnetic points of anisotropic bonds, and points where a field closes a gap with a quartic or higher dispersion. Either prove the three strata are the only ones, prove it on an explicit open dense set of directions, or exhibit a new singular stratum.
3. Test numerically, on the critical path. Search B_2 for singular points with an exact-diagonalization or small-DMRG measure of the criterion from Task 1: minimize L times the finite-size gap, or the inverse number of low states, over bonds, at L = 8 to 20, with gradient or Nelder–Mead restarts from random bonds, and check that every minimizer lands on one of the three strata (or their images under on-site unitaries) and not elsewhere. Compare z by fitting the gap at two or three lengths. 4. If you find a new stratum, run C141's local analysis there: the emergent species, its c, its velocity scaling in delta, and whether a fast sector with long crossover can emerge. If the list is complete, also check C141's single-species and non-coexistence claims at each stratum (the cluster energy (n - 1)V argument against a second fluid of bound pairs, the p-wave pairing analysis under U(1)-breaking perturbations, and the PXP-type model at the Fibonacci point), since the conditional derivation also rests on them.
