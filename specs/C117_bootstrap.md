Your folder is `../work_C117/`. Read `../../specs/_bound_background.md` with all updates, and REPORT.md sections 9.68 to 9.78, first. Rules of `_bound_background.md` apply (tag claims, no probabilities in files, Python only in your folder; cvxpy is not available, so write SDPs with scipy or a simple self-written solver, or ask before installing anything; no network commands except a browsing tool for literature; plain prose without em-dashes; PROGRESS.md, RESULT.md, PROOF.md). Heavy numerics only as SLURM jobs on Typhon (ssh -o LogLevel=ERROR typhon; never on the login node). Target: A = sum_a c_a v_a <= C M f(d), any f, no symmetry assumption, crossover-free. Constraint: the campaign's constructions have unbounded c at fixed d with tiny velocities, so any valid bootstrap must bound A, not c.

Three bootstrap routes:
(i) Ground-state positivity bootstrap. The ground state of H on a ring or the infinite chain satisfies <[H,O]> = 0 and <O^dagger [H,O]> >= 0 for every local O, translation invariance, and positivity of all reduced density matrices of k consecutive sites (d^k-dimensional). These give semidefinite constraints linking ground-state correlators to the bond. Combine them with the infrared asymptotics that encode A (the 1/L ring energy, the long-distance energy-current or energy-density correlator, or the f-sum and spectral moments of C100 and C103) to derive an inequality on A. Look in particular for an inequality in which massive modes cannot contribute at the order of the Casimir term (for example by using commutator constraints at large separation, or quantities like <[h_q,[H,h_-q]]> that are exact moments).
(ii) Numerical SDP experiments. For d = 2 and 3 and representative bonds (Heisenberg, XXZ, SU(3), random), compute rigorous lower bounds on ring ground energies E_0(L) from k-site marginal relaxations with ring consistency (block sizes as large as feasible) and from the positivity constraints above, and determine whether such relaxations can resolve the 1/L Casimir term at all, and how the dual certificates are structured (they are the sum-of-squares certificates that C114 is looking for).
(iii) Conformal and modular bootstrap of the infrared with lattice inputs. The lattice constrains the infrared through the finite dimension of the local operator algebra (operators on k sites span a d^{2k}-dimensional space) and through Lieb–Robinson; modular and conformal bootstrap bounds relate central charges to spectral gaps and operator content. Determine whether lattice-imposed constraints on low-lying operator content of the fast sectors, combined with modular bounds applied sector by sector with their velocities, can bound A.
Report which route gives an actual inequality, its constant, and what lemma remains.
