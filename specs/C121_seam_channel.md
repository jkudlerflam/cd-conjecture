Target: R_L(beta) = log[Z(L,beta)^2/Z(2L,beta)] <= K(d) + k M f(d) beta/L for every nearest-neighbour bond, which implies A <= (4k/pi) M f(d). The classical proof (C118): with a nonnegative d x d transfer matrix, (Tr T^L)^2 <= d sum_s ((T^L)_ss)^2 <= d Tr T^(2L), so R_L <= log d. A channel applied to every site costs an extensive amount (upper and lower Berezin–Lieb or Peierls symbols differ by O(M) per bond, so bulk free energies do not cancel). The idea to develop: localize the quantum-to-classical map at the seam. Cutting the ring at a site exposes its whole Euclidean time column; classicalizing it at every microscopic time step costs about beta M log d (the trivial bound). On a ring of length L the low-energy dynamics at the seam has level spacing of order v/L, so the seam should need to be measured (or pinched, or projected onto a basis, or replaced by a classical label) only at a coarse set of times spaced of order L/M, giving an effective number of classical seam variables N_eff of order beta M/L and hence R_L <= K(d) + k (M beta/L) log d.

Task: make this rigorous. (i) Formulate the quantum analogue of the two classical steps with the seam column classicalized at N time slices: an operator Cauchy–Schwarz over the d^N classical seam histories, and a positivity step comparing the reconnected doubled ring with two copies, both expressed through insertions of seam projectors e^{-tau H} P_s e^{-tau H} with tau of order L/M. (ii) Bound the error of inserting the coarse seam measurements (equivalently of the pinching channel at spacing tau) in Z(L) and Z(2L) by a factor exp(O(1)) per insertion, using an approximate Markov or recovery property of the ring Gibbs state along the seam column, Lieb–Robinson bounds for Euclidean or real-time evolution over time tau, or a direct operator inequality such as Tr[e^{-beta H}] versus Tr[prod (e^{-tau H} Pinch)] with explicit constants; identify the right tau (it must make N of order beta M/L with errors O(1) each). (iii) Check the scaling on XX by free fermions at large L and on Heisenberg and SU(3) by exact diagonalization, and test whether stoquastic chains (where the Trotterized column has nonnegative weights) are an easier first case. State the theorem obtained or the precise remaining lemma.
