# Entanglement bootstrap for gapless states, read as a toolkit for c–d

Compiled 2026-10-27 for the c–d project. REPORT.md §3.7 (Lin–McGreevy, the d = 2 single-site solutions, the bounds c ≤ 10.7 ℓ ln d and c ≤ 3 log₂ d) is the starting point and is not repeated.

Tags. [read: arXiv, location] means I opened the arXiv abstract or HTML and the location is the paper's own label. [recalled] means from memory, not reopened. [own] means my inference or arithmetic, not in any paper.

Conventions. K_X = −ln ρ_X. For contiguous A, B, C, Δ̂ = K_AB + K_BC − K_A − K_C and Î = K_AB + K_BC − K_B − K_ABC. Their expectations are Δ = S(B|A) + S(B|C) and I = I(A:C|B), both UV finite in a CFT because every endpoint enters once with each sign. K_Δ(η) = ηΔ̂ + (1 − η)Î. Here c means the Virasoro central charge of a non-chiral CFT (c = c_L = c_R), while the Kim–Li–Lin–McGreevy–Shi papers use c_tot = c_L + c_R = 2c [read: 2404.03725, Example 4.2, S(ℓ) = (c_tot/6) ln(ℓ/ε)].

## 1. Lin–McGreevy vector fixed point and local reconstruction (2303.05444)

Statement and axioms. In the vacuum of a unitary 1+1D CFT on a line, any contiguous A, B, C with cross ratio η satisfy K_Δ(η)|ψ⟩ = (c/3)h(η)|ψ⟩, with h the binary entropy [read: 2303.05444, Eqs. (1)–(3)]. The circle and thermal cases hold with η → η_eff [read: Sec. II]. Equivalently |ψ⟩ is a critical point of S_Δ = ηΔ + (1 − η)I under normalized variations, with critical value (c/3)h(η) [read: Sec. II, Eq. (9)]. The inputs are S = (c/3) ln(ℓ/ε) and the interval modular Hamiltonian as a quadratic-weight integral of T_00 [read: Eqs. (5)–(6)]. The operator form K_Δ = (c/3)h(η)·1 is argued for QFT from Reeh–Schlieder [read: Sec. II].

Derived. Applying the operator form to triples (1, 1, n − 2), the expectation-subtracted K̃ of any n-cell interval becomes a fixed combination of one- and two-cell K̃'s with piecewise-quadratic weights [read: Eqs. (10)–(14)]. So the two-cell marginal fixes every interval marginal. H = (1/π) Σ_j [K̃_{j,j+2} − ½K̃_{j,j+1} − ½K̃_{j+1,j+2}], with M_10 and C_0 built the same way and P, D, C_1 obtained as commutators [read: Eqs. (15)–(20)]. On an L-site circle H_rec = (cot(π/L)/L) Σ_i (K_{i,i+1} − K_i) [read: Eq. (29); same as 2509.04596, Eq. (1.4)]. There is no velocity factor, since the modular flow fixes the normalization, and blocking gives H_2 = 2H_1 [read: Eqs. (22)–(23)].

Mechanism. Each K_X is a constant plus ∫ f_X T_00 with f_X quadratic on X. With the η weights the f_X cancel pointwise in K_Δ, and the surviving UV constants combine into (c/3)h(η). The decomposition comes from solving the operator identity for the largest region.

Lattice status. Approximate only. On circles with L = 4, 8, 12 at η = 1/2, the vector error for critical Ising is 0.028, 0.009, 0.0057, and for Heisenberg 0.056 (L = 8) and 0.028 (L = 12) [read: Table 1]. Free fermions give error roughly ∝ 1/L² [read: Fig. 2 and text]. The approximate operator equation fails on these lattices where the vector one holds, and the authors say the operator form typically fails in finite dimension because −ln ρ is singular [read: Sec. II]. The "sites" in the decomposition are infinite dimensional in the derivation [read: Sec. III]. They cite Ranard–Walter–Witteveen (Cor. 6.1): at finite local dimension momentum is not a sum of local terms, so e^{−iP_rec} matches translation only at low energy [read: Sec. III]. The converse (VFPE implies CFT) and a derivation of c ≥ 1/2 from information theory are open [read: Further directions (a), (d)].

Assessment. c is the eigenvalue of an entropy combination, so every bound is entropic, as REPORT §3.7 shows. Two pieces of non-entropic structure remain. (i) The operator form is a finite algebraic problem on invertible marginals, but the authors' numerics say it is not the robust version on lattices. (ii) H_rec is itself a translation-invariant nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian on the same (ℂ^d)^{⊗L}, so the bootstrap maps the c–d class into itself, ρ_2 ↦ H_rec ↦ ground state. That is structure, not a bound. The integer d enters as an exact invariant only through the index ln d of the lattice shift [recalled: GNVW index], which P_rec must fail to reproduce at high energy. That feeds LSM-type lower bounds on c, not upper ones.

## 2. Li–Lin–McGreevy, CFTs in very small spaces (2509.04596)

Statement and axioms. They impose the VFPE at η = 1/2 for single-site triples on a 4-site circle, over pure, translation-invariant, real states (5 real parameters for qubits, 23 for qutrits) [read: Sec. 2, App. A]. The cost is the standard deviation of ĉ_Δ, with c_Δ = ⟨ĉ_Δ⟩ normalized by 3/(2 ln 2) [read: Eqs. (2.1)–(2.3)]. Gradient descent plus Newton reaches about 10⁻⁸ [read: App. A].

Derived. H_rec is a sum of non-negative Δ̂ terms with predicted spectrum (2π/L)Δ_i [read: Eqs. (1.4)–(1.6)]. Candidates are identified by DMRG and periodic MPS on H_rec up to L ≈ 14–16 [read: Sec. 3].

Lattice status. Qubits give Ising (c_Δ ≈ 0.526), XX (1.211) and Heisenberg (1.245), plus zero-correlation-length points: cat states (0), W, ferromagnet, a "maximum point" at c_Δ = 6.000, and a golden-chain state at 4.165 [read: Table 1]. Qutrits give tricritical Ising, Potts, many c = 1 points and about 25 unidentified "mystery" CFTs with c_Δ up to 2.495 [read: Fig. 2, Tables 2–18]. States with c_Δ > 2 all have entanglement-inferred c < 2, which the authors call consistent with c–d [read: Sec. 3]. In their examples c_Δ overestimates c [read: App. B]. H_rec typically has correlation length 15–30 sites and need not flow to the CFT [read: Sec. 3]. They propose blocking 8 sites into 4 supersites of dimension d² [read: Sec. 4].

Assessment. A 4-site, η = 1/2 estimator obeys c_Δ ≤ 3(Δ + I)/(2 ln 2) ≤ 6 log₂ d, since Δ ≤ 2S_B and I ≤ 2 min(S_A, S_C) [own]. The qubit maximum point at 6.000 saturates this, which is a good consistency check [own]. For qutrits the ceiling is 9.51 [own]. The overestimation c_Δ > c cannot be a theorem for large d. For ULS with single-site rulers the analogous entropic ceiling is below d − 1 once d ≥ 31 (§3), so the pattern must reverse there [own]. The method is a counterexample generator, which is how the authors pitch it, and not a bound.

## 3. Kim–Li–Lin–McGreevy–Shi, conformal geometry from entanglement (2404.03725)

Statement and axioms. From a "conformal ruler" define Δ and I (Eq. (4.1)), then c_tot and η by e^{−6Δ/c_tot} + e^{−6I/c_tot} = 1 with η = e^{−6Δ/c_tot} [read: Eq. (4.2)]. No distance is used. The main assumption is that c_tot is stationary under all norm-preserving variations (Assumption 4.6). Theorem 4.8 shows this is equivalent to K_Δ(η)|ψ⟩ ∝ |ψ⟩ with the state-defined η [read]. For chiral edges they also assume bulk A1 and c₋ ≠ 0. For non-chiral edges they replace c₋ ≠ 0 by genericity, meaning Δ̂_{abc}|ψ⟩, Δ̂_{bcd}|ψ⟩ and |ψ⟩ are linearly independent (Assumption 6.1) [read: Sec. 6]. The 1+1D CFT appears as an edge with empty bulk (Example 4.10), and the abstract claims similar 1+1D results under "a suitable set of assumptions". I did not locate a separate 1+1D assumption list in the parts I read [read: abstract, Example 4.10].

Derived. The three candidate cross ratios agree, η = η_J = η_K (Prop. 5.1). The η's obey the complement relations (Prop. 5.2) and the decomposition relations η(ab,c,d) = η(b,c,d)/(1 − η(a,b,c)) and its partners (Prop. 5.3; non-chiral version Prop. 6.3 and Eq. (6.18)) [read]. These are the axioms of cross ratios, so the edge embeds in a circle with η geometric, uniquely up to PSL(2,ℝ) (Prop. 5.4) [read]. c_tot is the same for every ruler (Prop. 5.5), and constancy of c_tot is equivalent to consistency of the η's (Prop. 5.6) [read]. Constancy alone does not give a CFT (Appendix H.1, Non-example 4.13) [read]. With η geometric, c_tot equals the Casini–Huerta c-function 6r ∂_r S (Example 4.3) [read].

Mechanism. Differentiating Eq. (4.2) gives δc_tot ∝ η δΔ + (1 − η) δI, so stationarity is the VFPE with the state's own η [own check, consistent with Thm. 4.8]. At a solution ηΔ + (1 − η)I = (c_tot/6)h(η), matching 2403.18410, Eq. (7.6) [read]. The decomposition relations come from matching coefficients of linearly independent vectors between overlapping rulers [read: Sec. 6.1].

Lattice status. Checked numerically on an irregular p+ip edge, with c_tot ≈ 1/2 and VFPE error about 10⁻³ [read: Example 4.5, App. G]. The robustness of stationarity is called "a puzzling surprise" [read: footnote 13]. Bulk A1 holds only approximately at finite dimension [read: Sec. 4].

Assessment. This is the right language for scale consistency. Constancy of c_tot and the decomposition relations are the multi-scale constraints that kill the d = 2 one-scale solutions of REPORT §3.7. The bound is entropic. Maximizing c_tot with Δ, I each at most 2 ln d gives Δ = I and c_tot ≤ 3(Δ + I)/ln 2, so single-site rulers give c ≤ 6 log₂ d [own]. ULS has c = d − 1 > 6 log₂ d for d ≥ 31 [own arithmetic]. Section 7 asks whether c_tot is a c-function [read]. For translation-invariant nearest-neighbour chains, monotone decrease from the lattice scale is then impossible: for ULS at d ≥ 31, c_tot must rise from at most 12 log₂ d to 2(d − 1) [own]. Any c-theorem for c_tot has to start at an onset scale, and the c–d content would sit in a linear-in-d bound at that scale, which nothing here supplies.

## 4. Chiral Virasoro algebra from a single wavefunction (2403.18410)

Statement and axioms. They assume bulk A0 and A1, plus Hypothesis 1, an isometry from a chiral CFT Hilbert space onto the edge subspace that sends "good" modular-flow generators to CFT modular Hamiltonians [read: Sec. 4.1]. The edge VFPE (Eq. (4.13)) follows from a cylinder assumption via Prop. 4.2 [read: Sec. 4.3].

Derived. L₀ = [4π tan(π/N)]⁻¹ Σ_i (Δ̂_i − ⟨Δ̂_i⟩) on N edge intervals [read: Eq. (6.6)]. This is the chiral cousin of H_rec. Twisted alternating sums of K's on 2n intervals give L̃_{±n} = Σ_m λ_{n,m} L_m, with λ_{n,m} = (n³ − n)/(m³ − m) on m = (−1)^k(2k + 1)n and L̃_{±1} = L_{±1} [read: Eqs. (6.15)–(6.23)]. The central charge appears as a commutator expectation, ⟨[L̃_n, L̃_{−n}]⟩ = (πc₋/24) n(n² − 1)² sin²(π/4n)/cos(π/2n) [read: Eq. (6.38)].

Mechanism. Under Hypothesis 1 a good-flow generator acts as ∫ f T plus a constant. The combinations are chosen so that f is a Fourier mode up to controlled harmonics, and the commutators then follow from the Virasoro algebra.

Lattice status. On a p+ip superconductor (L_x = 240) the commutators for n = 1 to 6 agree to about 10⁻⁵ [read: Sec. 7.1]. A semion state needs 24 sites for good agreement [read: Sec. 7.2]. The isometry can be exact only for an infinite-dimensional edge. Hypothesis 1 holds only on a modular-time window (−t*, t*), and the commutation relations are exact only in the thermodynamic limit [read: Secs. 4–5]. The checkable conditions imply only that the generators span a vector space, not that they close [read: Sec. 8.3]. The non-chiral case fails as stated, since finite modular flow makes η_L(t) ≠ η_R(t) [read: Sec. 8.2]. An extension to Kac–Moody currents via logarithms of flux-insertion operators is proposed [read: Sec. 8.3]. McGreevy's 2025 KITP slides announce a non-chiral 1+1D Virasoro construction as work in progress [recalled from a search-result snippet; the slides would not load].

Assessment. Here c is a commutator, not an entropy. Two finite-dimensional facts limit it. First, Virasoro with c ≠ 0 has no finite-dimensional representation, since the trace of [L_n, L_{−n}] = 2nL₀ + (c/12)(n³ − n) vanishes for two values of n [own, elementary]. The same trace kills a Kac–Moody level. So the central term measures where exactness fails and is not bounded by d. Second, |⟨[A, B]⟩| ≤ 2σ_A σ_B, and the variance of a modular Hamiltonian on a D-dimensional region is at most about ¼ ln² D [recalled: Reeb–Wolf-type bound]. Commutator routes therefore give c ≲ (ℓ ln d)² at region size ℓ, which is polylogarithmic [own, rough]. The Kac–Moody extension is more interesting for c–d. Currents are where a count linear in operator-space dimension could enter (d² − 1 traceless on-site operators), and SU(d)₁ saturates through Sugawara, c = (d² − 1)/(d + 1) = d − 1 [recalled]. This is the entanglement-bootstrap face of the parton level bound in REPORT §4(iii), and it does not exist yet.

## 5. Modular commutator and the finite-dimension no-go (2206.00027, 2408.10306, 2609.09019)

Statement. J(A,B,C) = i Tr ρ[ln ρ_AB, ln ρ_BC] [read: 2206.00027, intro]. For contiguous intervals in a 1+1D vacuum, J = (πc₋/6)(2η − 1) [read: Eq. (1)], and a general Cauchy slice separates c_L and c_R [read: Eq. (18)]. Also dS_BC/ds = −J under the modular flow of AB [read: Eq. (5)].

Mechanism. The flow of AB is a boost inside its causal diamond, and the null cutoffs at the moving endpoint rescale oppositely, so the first-order change of S_BC is ∝ c_L − c_R [read: Eqs. (6)–(7)].

No-go. Theorem 3.1 of 2408.10306 says no state on a finite-local-dimension tensor product satisfying bulk A1 has c₋ ≠ 0 [read]. The proof uses the "instantaneous modular flow" (Def. 4.1). Lemma 5.1 shows J is invariant along the flow, so S_BC(t) = S_BC + πc₋t/3 grows without bound, against S ≤ ln dim ℋ_BC [read: Eqs. (5.2)–(5.4)]. Finite dimension enters only through that entropy ceiling (and a bounded charge spectrum in Thm. 3.2) [read]. 2609.09019 (T.-H. Yang) makes this quantitative, proving a lower bound on the CMI needed for nonzero J [read: abstract].

Assessment. For c–d, J vanishes on a time slice because c₋ = 0 for these chains (Kapustin–Spodyneiko, REPORT §1) [read: Eq. (1)]. The value is the template, "growth at a rate set by c against a ln-dimension ceiling". A non-chiral analog needs an entropy that grows at a rate ∝ c_L + c_R under some flow [own heuristic: S_BC under finite one-sided flow of AB plausibly does, since both null cutoffs at the moved endpoint contract at late modular time; unchecked]. Even then the output is c·t* ≲ ℓ ln d, logarithmic unless t* is controlled separately [own]. I found no published 1+1D analog saying exact VFPE at all scales is impossible at finite d [own reading of the papers above]. REPORT's c ≤ 3 log₂ d for exact all-scale fixed points already rules out ULS for d ≥ 12.

## 6. Markov gap (2011.11864, 2110.11965)

Statement. h(A:B) = S_R(A:B) − I(A:B) ≥ 0, with S_R the reflected entropy, and g = 2E_P − I [read: 2011.11864, Eqs. (3), (5)]. h = 0 iff the state is a sum of triangle states, and g = 0 iff it is a triangle state [read: Thms. 2, 4]. h equals a CMI I(Ā:B|A) in the canonical purification [read: Eq. (43)]. For adjacent intervals in a pure state on a ring, h = (c/3) ln 2 for every CFT tested, while g depends on operator content [read: Table 1]. In 2D, after minimizing over local "smoothers" at the trijunctions, h_IR = (c₊/3) ln 2 with c₊ = c_L + c_R the minimal total edge central charge [read: 2110.11965, Eqs. (1), (4)]. h = 0 for string nets [read].

Lattice status. Periodic MPS up to N = 84 with power-law extrapolation [read: 2011.11864]. Chern insulator h_R = 0.2316 against 0.2310 [read: 2110.11965, Table 1]. Bare h ≥ h_IR [read].

Assessment. Unlike J, h sees c_L + c_R, but h ≤ S_R ≤ 2 min(S_A, S_B) gives c ≤ 6ℓ log₂ d [own]. The structure theorems characterize c = 0, not a ceiling. No help beyond log d.

## 7. Other lattice-to-CFT tools (brief)

Wang–Zou–Vidal build lattice Kac–Moody generators and extract the level numerically for XXZ and J₁–J₂ chains [read: 2206.01656, abstract]. This is the most direct numerical probe of the level bound k ≤ ind(R). Zou, and Liu–Zou–Ryu, get c and OPE data from wavefunction overlaps [read: 2104.00103, 2203.14992, abstracts]. Vardhan–Wei–Zou show the twirled-Petz recovery fidelity for A, B, C depends only on c and η and exceeds the CMI lower bound [read: 2307.14434, abstract]. It is still controlled by CMI, hence entropic [own]. Ramirez Camasca–Li–Lin–McGreevy generalize H_rec to any dimension and estimate the VFPE error as max(R⁻⁴, R^{−2−2Δ_min}) [read: 2606.12540, Eq. (C.18)]. For ULS the leading correction is the marginally irrelevant current-current coupling [recalled], so VFPE errors should decay only logarithmically. LM's Heisenberg errors are indeed about five times Ising's at L = 12 [read: 2303.05444, Table 1]. That makes the onset scale for c–d even harder to control.

## 8. Exactness and finiteness claims, collected

Operator VFPE fails in finite dimension (§1). Local momentum is impossible at finite local dimension (§1). The edge isometry is exact only in infinite dimension, with a modular-time window t* (§4). Bulk A1 plus finite local dimension forces c₋ = σ_xy = 0, with a quantitative CMI version (§5). No finite-dimensional Virasoro or Kac–Moody representation has a nonzero central term [own, §4]. Exact all-scale VFPE gives c ≤ 3 log₂ d (REPORT §3.7), and single-site c_tot gives c ≤ 6 log₂ d [own, §3].

## 9. Which tools could help with c–d

None of these tools breaks O(log d) alone. Every quantity that equals c in them is an entropy combination, a commutator controlled by modular-Hamiltonian variances, or a CMI. Each is bounded by polylog d at fixed region size. What they do supply is (i) intrinsic, velocity-free normalizations, (ii) exact scale-consistency structure, and (iii) generators written as sums of local terms on the same (ℂ^d)^{⊗L}, which is where tensor-product structure can enter. A proof would need one of these combined with a linear-in-d input at the onset scale, or a current-algebra count.

Ranked by likely use.
1. c_tot and the cross-ratio axioms (§3). They turn "consistent at the next scale" into exact algebraic relations and give a single-ruler ceiling, c ≤ 3(Δ + I)/(2 ln 2) ≤ 6 log₂ d, tighter than the fixed-η one. They are the natural frame for the two-scale problem in REPORT §5.5.
2. Velocity-free local reconstruction (§1, §2). H_rec lives on the same (ℂ^d)^{⊗L}, so c–d becomes a statement about the self-map ρ_2 ↦ H_rec ↦ ground state, and the spectrum of H_rec is non-entropic data.
3. Currents from the wavefunction (§4, Sec. 8.3 of 2403.18410, with Wang–Zou–Vidal in §7). This is the only place where a count linear in operator dimension, rather than in ln d, could appear.
4. The small-space search (§2) as a falsification engine at d = 2, 3, with d² blocking.
5. The finite-dimension no-go (§5) as a template for showing exact axioms fail at finite d and for bounding the onset scale.
