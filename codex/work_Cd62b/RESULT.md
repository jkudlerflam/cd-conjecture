# Neither (P) nor (D)

[proved] The quadratic velocity budget extends to all orders in the coupling on an isolated interface. Solve each two-cell problem exactly and sum its effective interface energies. If this sum is number-conserving quadratic in a common cell Fock space, then \(A\le C(d)J_*\) and the chiral crossing count obeys \(N_F(u)\le B(d)(J_*/u)^{a(d)}\) with \(a(d)<1\). A sufficient threshold is
\[
 \frac{g}{\Delta}\le \frac{1}{1728\sqrt{10}\,d}.
\]
For the full linked Hamiltonian through any fixed order \(K\), including all multi-interface and on-cell terms, the same conclusions hold if the truncation is quadratic. Its constants may depend on \(K\) and \(d\). Both results are uniform in cell length, code dimension and ring length. [Proof, Sections 2 to 5](PROOF.md#5-an-all-order-quadratic-budget-and-a-full-fixed-order-budget-proved)

[proved] At order \(k\) the horizontal effective Hamiltonian uses connected intervals of at most \(k\) interfaces. Its resolvent words contain exactly \(k\) ports and \(k-1\) reduced inverses. Expanding each inverse into successive low-rank approximations gives a convergent bound on the \(p\)-th powers of the normalized operator Schmidt coefficients of an exact isolated interface, for some \(p<1\) at the displayed threshold. The exact rank itself can grow without bound already at order two with \(d=4\) and port rank two. The example has static gate bits and gives a positive Gram matrix of rank at least the cell length. [Proof, Sections 1 and 2](PROOF.md#2-resolvent-words-and-ranks-proved)

[proved] There is a microscopic all-order comparison on the full ring. For \(g/\Delta\le1/8\), a product of at most three layers of two-cell unitaries gives a Hamiltonian preserving the code plus a bounded-range remainder. The code block of the reference is the sum of the exact isolated-interface energies. With \(x=n\min(1,\Delta/J_*)\), the remainder has norm per cell at most
\[
 C_m\frac{g^2}{\Delta}(1+x)^{-m}
\]
for every fixed positive integer \(m\). The reference also has a positive cost for leaving the code above its ground energy when \(4\|l\|_1 g<\Delta\), with \(l\) the explicit cutoff kernel in the proof. Every fixed-order contribution involving several interfaces has the same spatial decay, with order-dependent constants. These bounds include interactions induced between the ends of one cell. [Proof, Section 3](PROOF.md#an-all-order-change-of-basis-on-the-full-ring-proved)

[proved] Almost commuting neighbouring interface terms bound the largest speed even in an interacting truncation. If adjacent commutators have norm at most \(\gamma\) and the remaining bounded-range terms have density \(\epsilon\) in cell units, the Lieb–Robinson speed in physical sites is at most \(C n(\sqrt\gamma+\epsilon)\), with \(C\) depending on range. If both the isolated reference and the full dressed code compression are quadratic, the velocity sum of the compression is also bounded at finite coupling. This uses a trace-norm estimate on the residual hopping and allows the narrowest bands to change. [Proof, Sections 4 and 5](PROOF.md#4-propagation-speed-with-interactions-proved)

[proved] For a reciprocal Gaussian liquid, an upper bound on the current stiffness by the bare velocity matrix \(bV\) and on the density stiffness by \(b(V+n\epsilon I)\) gives \(\sum_j v_j\le b(\sum_j u_j+\sqrt{n\epsilon}\sum_j\sqrt{u_j})\), where \(u_j\) are bare physical speeds. The half moment is controlled for the isolated quadratic model at a smaller explicit threshold. The borderline bare tower \(u_j=J_*/j^2\) acquires a divergent harmonic velocity sum in an abstract Gaussian liquid if the density stiffness receives a common positive shift. A microscopic realization is open. A chiral stiffness trace bound works also for unequal right and left speeds. Weak coupling to excited cells alone permits any two-site logical interaction of a fixed smaller site dimension already at first order. [Proof, Section 6](PROOF.md#6-what-the-interaction-matrices-would-have-to-satisfy)

[proved] The frozen nine-letter chain fits the cell decomposition in a fixed uniform antikink sector, at zero field or modulo the scalar field at fixed total charge. For the matched nine-orbital member the physical cell length is \(10\), the ground dimension is \(512\), the cell gap is \(2[\cos(\pi/11)-\cos(2\pi/11)]\), and the port norm is \(\sqrt2\,2^{-359}\). The published \(2^{-163}\) is the intracell conversion amplitude. \(J_*=2020\) is a valid internal bond bound in walker hopping units. [Proof, Section 8](PROOF.md#8-the-frozen-nine-letter-chain)

[numerical] At those parameters the quoted \(10^{-2.74}\) geometric factor is reproduced by the eigenvalues of the symmetric hopping matrix. Their smallest-to-largest log ratio is \(-21.93059\) in base ten. Direct differentiation at all 18 Fermi points gives a log ratio of **\(-1.87762\)** for the absolute group velocities. The leading quadratic Casimir coefficient is \(A_{\rm free}=3.91992\,10^{-22}\, b E_u\), with cell length \(b=10\) and \(\log_{10}E_u=-377.99136\). Two decimal precisions and independent determinant and null-vector formulas give the same printed slopes. [Calculation and normalization, Section 8](PROOF.md#8-the-frozen-nine-letter-chain)

[physics-level] For the entire frozen clock family, if its microscopic liquid has total locally propagating central charge at most \(b-1\) as predicted, the weak-port light cone and the clock gap give \(A\le18e\pi^2 t_0\), where \(t_0\) is the walker hopping. This uses the mode count and allows arbitrary renormalization of the velocities. [Proof, Section 8](PROOF.md#8-the-frozen-nine-letter-chain)

[assumed] A microscopic velocity sum for the full class still needs control of the interacting infrared stiffness or mode count and of the off-block remainder at the infrared scale. The per-cell error gives only \(4\epsilon_{\rm cell}L^2/n\) for the scaled doubling defect. The intermediate coupling window remains open. The Gaussian example supplies a target stiffness pattern for a possible counterexample. [Remaining problem, Sections 6, 7 and 9](PROOF.md#9-remaining-statements)
