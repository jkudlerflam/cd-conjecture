# C191 result: the pseudospin RG of the two-valley gas from the scattering data

Derivations in PROOF.md, log in PROGRESS.md. Scripts in this folder: scat191.py (exact two-body S-matrix at the Fermi-surface kinematics, written independently of C175's code), gauss191.py (scaling dimensions in the four-chiral-boson Gaussian theory), lowE.py (Landau parameters and g₁), aba_valley.py (one valley in the asymptotic Bethe ansatz), hybrid.py and predict.py (Luttinger parameters, observables), ktflow.py (KT flow), ring2_frame.py (two-boson ring check of the frame), gy_check.py (Gaudin–Yang check), aba_phi13.py (one-valley check against C179's rings), check1.py, fs_data.py, rr_check.py. Outputs: lowE.out, aba_valley.out, aba_phi13.out, hybrid.out, predict.out, predict.json, scan.out, ktflow.out, gy_check.out. All local, at most one core, no Typhon job.

Model: C173 PROOF eq. (3.2), |w| = 1, φ = π/2, units |t₂| = 1. Conventions (PROOF 4): K_c and K_s are defined by the scaling dimensions x(cos √8φ_s) = 2K_s and x(e^{i√2θ_c}) = 1/(2K_c) in the frame where each valley field is fermionic within the valley and the two valleys are mutually bosonic. In this convention two decoupled XX legs have K_c = K_s = 1, the attractive Gaudin–Yang gas has K_s < 1, and the valley-imbalance fluctuations in a block of length ℓ grow as (2K_s/π²) ln ℓ.

Any drift C189 sees should be estimator finite-size, of the same size and sign as on its c = 2 control.
3. Other observables in the same frame (n = 3/16, hybrid central and band). Inter-valley pair function at momentum π decays with exponent 1/K_c = 0.76 (0.73 to 0.79). 4. What would refute this. A K_s at n = 3/16 below 1.2, or any K_s below 1 at the three densities, or a downward drift toward 1 that C189's c = 2 control does not show.

## Key claims

1. [numerical, exact two-body] The two-body data at the Fermi-surface kinematics (Fermi points from Luttinger's theorem per valley, with the band's cubic asymmetry kept). The like-valley phase δ_s (fermion reference) is +0.50, +0.56, +0.62 at n = 1/8, 5/32, 3/16, strongly attractive. The inter-valley reflection amplitude |r| is 0.103, 0.022, 0.039 and passes through zero near n ≈ 0.167. The transmission is t ≈ e^{−0.19i}, e^{−0.33i}, e^{−0.47i} for the pair (aR, bL) and e^{+0.14i}, e^{+0.03i}, e^{−0.06i} for (aL, bR) (fs_data.py, lowE.out). The code reproduces C175's eigen-lengths a_±(q) to all printed digits and gives the hard-rod phase −q exactly.
2. [numerical, exact two-body ring] The frame. Relative to free fermions the transmission carries a phase near π, relative to free bosons it is small. So the weakly coupled description has valley fields that are mutually bosonic (fermionic within a valley), the frame of two XX legs. A two-boson ring of 600 sites at K = π confirms it: the reflectionless Bethe–Yang levels with the boson-frame phase match the exact levels to 2.7e-6, the fermion-frame alternative misses by 9e-3, half a level spacing (ring2_frame.py). This fixes the operator content (PROOF 3, 4).
3. [derived] Resonant operators on the symmetric line in (3.2). Only the inter-valley backscattering g₁ cos √8φ_s is resonant at generic density. Valley conversion, pair hopping aa → bb (momentum 4s) and charge umklapp (order 16 or higher at these densities) all carry momentum or are strongly irrelevant. The two-pair vertex O₄ is absent from (3.2) (it changes N by 4). In the qubit chain it has dimension 2/K_c ≈ 1.5, relevant, but with a locking length of order 1e6 sites at V/Δ = 40 (PROOF 8).
4. [derived, physics standard] K_c and K_s, table above. The dominant input is the like-valley attraction, which raises K_s and K_c together (one valley alone has K_∥ = 1.36, 1.46, 1.49 in the Bethe ansatz). The inter-valley forward scattering at k_F is weakly repulsive and raises K_s further, the threshold (R–R) inter-valley attraction lowers it slightly. Pseudospin is not SU(2): K_s − 1 is set by intra- against inter-valley forward scattering, while g₁ is set by the inter-valley reflection, and the two are unrelated here.
5. [derived] g₁ is weak because at q = k_F the two inter-valley eigen-phases nearly coincide modulo π. The shallow bound state (a₊ = 20.7) makes the even channel attractive only for q ≲ 0.15. By q ≈ 0.19 its phase has unwound to zero, and at k_F the hard core and the finite range dominate. The zero-range Yang–Gaudin template of C175 (γ = 2/(n a₊)) misses this.
6. [derived] KT flow verdict. All three candidate densities start deep on the gapless side of the separatrix, with 2(K_s − 1) exceeding |y₁| by a factor of 10 (n = 1/8) to between 45 and 95 (n = 5/32, 3/16). The flow ends on the fixed line at K_s* = K_s⁰ − (3e-3 or less). There is no pseudospin gap, so c = 2 in the thermodynamic limit of (3.2) at these densities. The verdict needs only K_s > 1 + |y₁|/2 ≈ 1.03, 1.01, 1.01, and it survives normalizing y₁ by the dressed spin velocity (margins stay at +0.5 or more).
7. [derived, with the same inputs] The separatrix is crossed at n_KT ≈ 0.07 (0.066 to 0.074 over the variants with y₁ normalized by the bare Fermi velocity, scan.out, and up to about 0.085 if y₁ is normalized by the dressed spin velocity, which is up to 1.9 times smaller). Below it the pseudospin flows to strong coupling (paired liquid, c = 1), with ξ_s ≈ 750 sites at n = 0.05 and 2e4 to 6e4 at n = 1/16. Close to n_KT the gap is unobservably small (KT essential singularity). This reconciles C175's dilute pairing with C176's c ≈ 1.9 at n ≥ 1/8. The gap scale at the candidate densities is zero, and the crossover length of g₁ is of the order of the lattice spacing.
8. [checks] Gaudin–Yang: the same construction puts the attractive gas on the SU(2) separatrix, 2(K_s − 1) = −|y₁| to first order, and the KT time gives Δ ∝ exp(−π²/(2|γ|)), the exact Bethe-ansatz exponent, to 0.01 to 0.8 percent for |γ| = 0.05 to 0.4 (gy_check.out). Hard rods: phase −q exact, Bethe-ansatz K = (1 − ρ)² to 1e-4 (aba_valley.out). Free two-leg XX: K_c = K_s = 1, y₁ = 0, exponents 1, 1/2, 3/2 for pair, boson and fermion (gauss191.py). One valley at φ = 1.3: Bethe-ansatz K = 1.34 at n = 1/6 against C179's ring values 1.46 to 1.57 (aba_phi13.out), so the one-valley treatment if anything underestimates the attraction.
9. [consistency, not blind] Numbers known before this run from REPORT §9.158 (C176, L = 256, n = 3/16): pair exponent 0.78, fermion envelope exponent near 2.0, L·Δ_P ≈ 2.2 to 2.4. The predictions 0.73 to 0.79, 1.72 to 1.82 and 2.38 to 2.75 (hybrid) are close. The pure first-order Landau variants give L·Δ_P = 3.0 to 3.95, which is why the hybrid is the central estimate.
10. [tension] C178's small rings (N = 4 to 8) give R = D_Néel/D_ps falling from 1.7 to 1.1. In this convention R = K_s², so those rings read K_s ≈ 1.06 to 1.31, below this prediction and falling with N. This run cannot reproduce rings that small. The L = 128 to 256 data are the test.

## Answers to the spec's items

- Low-energy theory: two valley Fermi seas, mutually bosonic, with intra- and inter-valley forward couplings (four Landau parameters, because the cubic band term makes the (aR, bL) and (aL, bR) pairs differ), plus g₁. Nothing else resonant in (3.2).
- K_c, K_s, g₁: table above, from the exact phases.
- KT flow: gapless side at all three densities, margin 0.57 to 1.75. Separatrix at n_KT ≈ 0.07.
- Gap and crossover length: none at the candidate densities. Below n_KT, ξ_s ≈ 750 sites at n = 0.05.
- Known limits: free two-leg XX, attractive Gaudin–Yang and hard rods all pass (claim 8).

## What is left open

1. The in-medium value of the R–R inter-valley Landau parameter. The vacuum value is dominated by the shallow bound-state pole, which Pauli blocking removes in the medium. It moves K_s by ±0.1 at n = 3/16 and does not touch the verdict.
2. Second-order inter-valley effects and the dressing of inter-valley vertices by the intra-valley interaction are not computed. Inter-valley phases at k_F are at most 0.47, so the error is of order 0.1 in K.
3. The C178 small-ring tension (claim 10).
4. The qubit chain is not addressed beyond the O₄ estimate.

## Comparison with C189 (written after the registration above)

C189's RESULT.md, read after the registration, contains only C189's own registration (conventions, estimators, decision rule, infinite-chain plan). It has no K_s, K_c or drift numbers yet, so there is nothing to compare against. Three points from reading it bear on how its numbers should be read against this prediction (PROOF 7.6, 7.7, wall.py, stiff.py).

1. Frame of the operator relations. C189's E_D (η_D = K_s + 1/K_c) and E_G (η_G = (K_c + 1/K_c + K_s + 1/K_s)/4) are the relations for mutually fermionic valley fields. Section 3 of PROOF shows (exact two-body ring, 2.7e-6 against 9e-3) that this model is weakly coupled in the mutually bosonic frame, where the pair exponent is 1/K_c alone and the fermion carries a string over the other valley. If the frame here is right, E_D read with C189's relation gives K_s^D = η_D − 1/K_c ≈ 0 at all three densities, and E_G gives K_s + 1/K_s ≈ 4, both artifacts. C189's two-leg XX control should show the same failure (true pair exponent 1, where η_D = K_s + 1/K_c predicts 2; true η_G = 3/2, where the formula predicts 1), while the ff2v free-fermion control passes. So C189's own control rule should reject those two estimators if this analysis is right.
2. Boundary condition. C189 assumes the wall swaps valleys (θ_s pinned). The single-particle wall of (3.2) reflects mostly within a valley (|R_intra| = 0.98 and 0.95, |R_inter| = 0.20 and 0.30 at n = 1/8 and 3/16, wall.py), i.e. φ_s pinned at short scales. With K_s > 1 the inter-valley boundary term has dimension 1/K_s < 1 and is relevant, so the far-infrared boundary condition may become θ_s-pinned, with a crossover length of very roughly 1e2 sites at n = 3/16 and 1e3 at n = 1/8 [heuristic]. The measured L·Δ_P (2.18, 2.24, 2.37) fits the φ_s-pinned formula (π/2)(u_c/K_c + u_s/K_s) = 2.38, 2.48, 2.75 and not the θ_s-pinned one (π/2)(u_c/K_c + u_s K_s) = 3.42, 4.13, 4.94. If φ_s is pinned at L ≤ 256, C189's E_χ combination (v_s K_s)(K_s/v_s) becomes (v_s/K_s)(K_s/v_s) = 1, so E_χ would read K_s ≈ 1 whatever the true K_s. The same caution applies to E_F, whose relation also assumes θ_s pinned.
3. Robust readouts. The estimators that do not depend on the frame or the boundary are the density structure factor (K_c, predicted 1.32 at n = 3/16, band 1.25 to 1.55), the bulk pseudospin susceptibility on its own, χ_s = ∂(n_a − n_b)/∂(δ/2) = 2K_s/(πu_s) = 0.77 at n = 3/16 (band 0.48 to 0.98), and bipartite fluctuations of the valley imbalance, (2K_s/π²) ln ℓ. A direct K_s needs u_s from elsewhere (for example the infinite-chain transfer spectrum).
