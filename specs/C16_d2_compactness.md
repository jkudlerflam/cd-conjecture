# C16: c ≤ 1 for qubit chains via compactness and the c-theorem

Read `../../specs/_d2_background.md` first; it defines the problem, the parametrization, what is known and the rules. If `PROGRESS.md` exists in your folder, continue from it.

**Your part: (b) of the d = 2 program.** The 8-parameter family is compact. By the c-theorem, c is locally bounded near any point whose infrared is a CFT with finite c (a nearby flow passes close to that CFT before leaving it), and a gap is stable, so any sequence of critical points with growing c, or any isolated c > 1 point, must sit near special points. Turn this into a reduction of the d = 2 question to a finite, checkable list.

Tasks.
1. **Gapless non-conformal points.** Classify the points of the family with z ≠ 1 or macroscopic degeneracy: ferromagnetic points (product or spiral ground states with gapless magnons), saturation and band-edge points, Lifshitz and commensurate–incommensurate points, and frustration-free points with degenerate ground spaces. For each, derive the low-energy theory of its neighbourhood (dilute magnon or domain-wall gases, Pokrovsky–Talapov) and the largest c reachable nearby. Show it is 1, or exhibit the exception.
2. **Multicritical points.** Critical points with c > 1 can only be multicritical (codimension at least one) or stable multi-component phases. Using symmetry and operator counting, classify which multicritical CFTs with c > 1 (for example Ising × Luttinger with c = 3/2, Ising × Ising × Luttinger, two Luttinger liquids with c = 2) a nearest-neighbour qubit chain can reach in each symmetry stratum of the residual set (one unitary Z₂, antiunitary only, none), and with what codimension. For stable multi-component phases without symmetry (incommensurate multi-mode Luttinger liquids), find what a qubit bond would need to supply (independent incommensurate modes, their stiffness) and whether rank J ≤ 3 limits it.
3. **The reduction.** Combine 1 and 2 into a statement of the form "c ≤ 1 holds on the whole family unless one of the following finite list of structures exists", and give for each item a concrete test (a quantity to compute, or a small numerical check). Carry out the tests you can.
