## Standard of this run

The standard is theoretical physics: a derivation using RG, bosonization, controlled perturbation theory, Lieb–Robinson bounds, sum rules and similar tools, not mathematical rigor. But it must be a derivation. Numerical evidence may support or test a step and cannot replace it. Every step is tagged with what it assumes.

## The question

A = sum_a c_a v_a is the Casimir coefficient. Every known physics-level argument for A <= C(d) M (the entropy bound on dyadic velocity shells, giving A <= (12/pi) V log d, and C100's thermal Drude sum rule, giving A of order M^3/T_*^2) assumes that every gapless sector is conformal by a bounded crossover length. Any argument that uses the chain at temperature T or length L sees only the sectors already conformal there, and fast sectors can hide behind long crossovers (C106, a free d = 4 chain with v/M fixed and diverging crossover). So A can grow toward the infrared, and no monotonicity from the ultraviolet bounds it. C133 showed the missing statement must be an integrable budget: sum over sectors with v_a >= u of c_a <= B(d) (M/u)^p with p < 1, uniformly in crossover lengths. For free fermions the budget exists: A <= pi |e_hop| because every Fermi point costs hopping energy.

The task is to find the interacting budget: a finite microscopic resource that every emergent fast sector consumes, whenever in the RG it emerges, or to show that none exists by constructing a hidden tower.

## Tasks

1. d = 2 first. A physics derivation that a nearest-neighbour spin-1/2 chain has total central charge bounded by a constant (c_tot <= 1, or any constant) would give C(2) < infinity, since Lieb–Robinson gives v_a <= 4eM. At weak coupling, Jordan–Wigner gives one band with at most two Fermi points. Decide whether strong coupling can generate further gapless sectors (bound-state strings forming additional Fermi seas, Ising sectors coexisting with a Luttinger liquid, emergent symmetries), using what is known about spin-1/2 chains (XYZ in fields, DM terms, integrable points, known multicritical points). Either derive a bound with every assumption stated or exhibit a candidate spin-1/2 bond with c_tot > 1 and test it.
2. General d: the budget. Candidates to derive or refute: (a) the ground-state energy as the budget, generalizing A <= pi |e_hop|, for instance through a variational or sum-rule argument that each emergent fast sector lowers the ground energy by an amount proportional to its velocity; (b) the zero-temperature energy-current f-sum weight, a ground-state expectation of a local operator bounded by order M^3; (c) the entanglement-generation rate (small incremental entangling, of order M log d per cut), noting that entanglement is velocity-blind and probably gives only p = 1; (d) operator-space dimension d^2 - 1 per site combined with bosonization of emergent sectors. For each, derive what it gives for N_c(u) = sum_{v_a >= u} c_a, or state the precise reason it fails.
3. Hidden towers. Revisit the hierarchical construction left open by C133 (sector k conformal beyond length b^k with velocity of order M) at the physics level: estimate, by perturbative RG or by an explicit coupled-wire or encoding argument, whether its velocities can stay of order M at fixed d, and what the budget candidates of Task 2 say about it.
4. State the conclusion: a derivation of C(d) < infinity (or C(2) < infinity) with its assumptions, or the sharpest physics-level obstruction, and how it bears on the rigorous program.
