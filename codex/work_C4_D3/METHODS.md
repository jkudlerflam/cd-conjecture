# D3 numerical method and qualifications

This is a closed-book calculation. No pre-existing project files, data, or references were read. All numerical inputs are generated by the scripts in this directory. The supplied continuum identifications of the four calibration chains are inputs, not results proved by ED.

## Hamiltonians and conventions

Every calculation uses one fixed, real, exchange-symmetric two-site matrix, repeated on all L periodic bonds. Site 0 is the least significant base-d digit. Energies and entropies use natural units and natural logarithms.

For spin s, let X = S_i dot S_(i+1). Its eigenvalue in total pair spin J is

    x_J = [J(J+1) - 2s(s+1)]/2.
    P_J = product_(K != J) (X - x_K I)/(x_J - x_K).

The numerical checks verify orthogonality, completeness, and ranks 2J+1. No guessed polynomial coefficients enter the projectors.

* ULS is h = swap, with d = 3 or 4. Balanced color occupations reduce the Hilbert space. The use of balanced occupations to obtain the ULS ground state is an assumption checked against the larger spin or spin-orbital sector at overlapping sizes. Only L divisible by d is used for the vacuum Casimir fit.
* TB is h = 2 sum_J H_J P_J, where H_0 = 0 and H_J = sum_(j=1)^J 1/j. For spin 1 this is 3I + (X-X^2)/2. The spin-3/2 eigenvalues are (0,2,3,11/3). Identification with the supplied TB benchmarks is assumed.
* Sp(4) uses the spin-3/2 realization. The antisymmetric pair space is J=0 plus J=2. Thus P_1 = P_(J=0), P_5 = (I-swap)/2-P_1, and P_10 = (I+swap)/2. The scanned Hamiltonian is cos(theta) P_5 + sin(theta) P_10.
* The SU(2) sphere removes the shift direction (1,1,1,1). Its orthonormal frame is (1,-1,0,0)/sqrt(2), (1,1,-2,0)/sqrt(6), (1,1,1,-3)/sqrt(12). A Fibonacci sphere samples its unit sphere. Positive overall scale is fixed. Antipodal points remain distinct Hamiltonians.
* For the spin-orbital family set A = S_i dot S_(i+1), B = T_i dot T_(i+1). The stored coefficients multiply (AB,A,B). The product family is sign*[AB+yA+xB], differing from sign*(A+x)*(B+y) by the constant sign*x*y. Both signs are scanned. Exchange of spin and orbital maps x to y, so only x <= y is needed. The SU(4) point is x=y=1/4, sign=+1, where h=swap_4/4-I/16.
* The spin-1 coefficients multiply (X,X^2,Sz_i Sz_(i+1),(Sz_i^2+Sz_(i+1)^2)/2). The last coefficient is the on-site D after summing bonds. In the U(1) grid, coefficients (1,beta,Delta-1,D) mean SxSx+SySy+Delta SzSz+beta X^2+D sum_i Sz_i^2.

## Charge and momentum reduction

For SU(2)-invariant chains, the smallest allowed |Sz| sector contains a state from every total-spin multiplet. This follows from the weights -S,...,S of each irreducible SU(2) representation. Spin 1 uses Sz=0 for both even and odd L. Spin 3/2 uses Sz=0 for even L and Sz=1/2 for odd L. The stored spin-3/2 charges are twice Sz. For SU(2) times SU(2), each factor is treated the same way. These reductions cannot miss a global energy minimum.

For U(1)-only spin-1 chains this argument fails. Spin inversion relates M and -M, but does not relate different |M|. The grid searches all M=0,...,L, and the initial deformations receive a separate charge audit. Corrected records supersede higher-energy M=0 records in analysis. A mixed-state or sector-constrained entropy is never used as the global ground-state entropy after this correction.

Sp(4) refinements optionally use its two Cartan charges, with local weights (1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(-1,0). This gives a smaller subspace than Sz=0. Agreement at overlapping sizes is checked numerically. Extension of that equality to L=12 is an assumption, except at points independently checked in another sufficient sector. The report retains this qualification.

Let T cyclically move site 0 to site L-1. Choose the least integer representative r of each orbit, with period p_r. At k = 2 pi n/L, an orbit is allowed only if exp(i k p_r)=1. Define

    |r,k> = p_r^(-1/2) sum_(t=0)^(p_r-1) exp(-i k t) |T^t r>.

Then T|r,k> = exp(i k)|r,k>. If acting with a bond term on representative r produces the basis word T^q r', with amplitude h, translation invariance gives

    <r',k|H|r,k> contribution = h sqrt(p_r/p_r') exp(i k q).

To see the normalization, sum over the p_r translates in the ket. The bra phase for T^(q+t)r' is exp(i k(q+t)), cancelling the ket phase exp(-i k t). The remaining factor is p_r/sqrt(p_r p_r'). Incompatible output orbits have zero projection.

All momentum blocks are Hermitian to the stated numerical tolerance. Since every local matrix is real, complex conjugation relates n and L-n, so only n=0,...,floor(L/2) needs diagonalization. These are all momenta up to that exact relation, not a restriction to an assumed ground momentum.

`selftest.json` compares every momentum block with an explicitly constructed charge-sector Hamiltonian at L=4, then compares the union of momentum eigenvalues to its entire spectrum. Maximum errors are below 8e-15. The production solver records an eigenvector residual. A small residual verifies an eigenpair, but by itself does not prove that Lanczos found every lower eigenvalue. Dense small-size checks and repeated symmetry realizations are the available safeguards.

## Entropy

The normalized momentum eigenvector is expanded into its complete charge-sector basis. For each contiguous cut, Schmidt singular values are computed separately in additive-charge blocks. Their squared sum is checked to be one. The periodic-CFT ansatz is

    S(l,L) = s0 + (c/3) log[(L/pi) sin(pi*l/L)] + corrections.

Ordinary least squares fits c and s0 for l=cut,...,floor(L/2), with cut=1,2,3. These are assumptions about a CFT regime, not identities for a finite chain. `c_ent2` uses cut=2. The regression standard error measures only scatter around that ansatz. `cut_systematic` is the maximum difference from cut=2 when changing to cut=1 or 3. Neither quantity is a controlled error bound on thermodynamic c. The calibration demonstrates this failure directly.

An independent diagnostic uses the change of half-chain entropy with L,

    c_half(L1,L2) = 3 [S(L2/2,L2)-S(L1/2,L1)] / log(L2/L1).

Only compatible even-size sequences should be interpreted this way. Ground-state changes and multiplet choices can spoil it. All entropy values at degeneracies depend on the chosen pure eigenvector. The solver's seed makes its choice reproducible in a fixed numerical environment, not physically canonical.

## Energy, velocity, and towers

Under the single-velocity CFT vacuum assumption,

    E0(L)/L = e_infinity - A/L^2 + ...,
    A = pi*c*v/6.

For an assumed dimension-one current at momentum k_ground +/- 2pi/L,

    v_L = L [E_min(k_ground +/- 2pi/L)-E0] / (2pi).

The lower of the two adjacent gaps is retained. This step assumes the relevant state has scaling dimension one and that the vacuum sector is being compared. Neither assumption is automatic. Incommensurate SU(4) lengths provide a direct countercheck.

The calibration fit envelope compares (i) a linear fit of E0/L versus L^-2 using the largest two compatible lengths and (ii) a quadratic fit using the largest three. Velocity is extrapolated linearly against L^-2 on the respective window. The midpoint and half-width of the two resulting c values are reported as a fit-choice envelope. This is not a statistical confidence interval. Logarithmic corrections are not under control at these sizes.

Tower coordinates are x_eff = (E-E0)*L/(2pi*v_L), with lattice momentum recorded independently. Their interpretation assumes the same velocity and the correct current. For supplied ULS identifications, the fundamental nonchiral primary has x=(d-1)/d at relative momentum 2pi/d. For the supplied SU(2)_(2s) TB identification, the j=1/2 primary has x=3/[2(2s+2)] at relative momentum pi. These continuum formulas are used as consistency checks, not derived from the lattice calculation. We do not identify a full CFT from one approximately matching level.

For several velocities the Casimir term is instead

    E0(L) = e_infinity L - pi/(6L) sum_a c_a v_a + ... .

Dividing by the smallest velocity gives sum_a c_a v_a/v_min, not sum_a c_a. The decoupled spin-orbital control with couplings 1 and 3 illustrates a false violation: its energy estimate tends to 4 while the sum of two assumed SU(2)_1 charges is 2. Its ground state and entropy are independent of the positive coupling ratio. The usual SU(2)_1 limit of the AF spin-1/2 chain is an additional continuum identification assumed for this control.

## Exact exclusions available without extrapolation

For Sp(4), write h-sin(theta)I = -sin(theta) P_1 + [cos(theta)-sin(theta)] P_5. On theta in [5pi/4,2pi], both coefficients are nonnegative. Every completely symmetric SU(4) tensor annihilates both antisymmetric projectors and attains E0=L sin(theta). Thus this whole interval has a large exact ground space. In a one-defect state above a polarized product, choosing the defect so it does not form the Sp singlet gives

    E(q)-E0 = [cos(theta)-sin(theta)] (1-cos(q)).

This is a quadratic branch. At the endpoint where its coefficient vanishes, degeneracy increases. These chains cannot be justified as a collection of only relativistic unitary CFTs. Large entropy slopes of arbitrary vectors in this ground space do not supply a counterexample.

For the SU(2) spin-3/2 sphere, a_3=min_J a_J proves H-L a_3 I is positive and a fully polarized state is a ground state. The one-defect pair mixes only J=3 and J=2, giving E(q)-E0=(a_2-a_3)(1-cos q). This supplies the analogous exact exclusion wherever the coefficient is positive, and a degeneracy warning on its boundary.

For the spin-1 bilinear-biquadratic line the pair energies are a_0=-2cos(theta)+4sin(theta), a_1=-cos(theta)+sin(theta), a_2=cos(theta)+sin(theta). The condition a_2=min(a_0,a_1,a_2) is theta in [pi/2,5pi/4]. The magnon branch is -2cos(theta)(1-cos q). The endpoints have additional degeneracies.

For spin-orbital product lines x=-1/4, use A-1/4=-P_singlet^S. If sign=+1 and y<=-1/4, or sign=-1 and y>=3/4, each full product bond is positive. Any completely symmetric spin state tensored with any orbital state is a zero-energy state of the full product Hamiltonian. This is an extensive degeneracy. The stored, shifted Hamiltonian has energy -sign*x*y*L there. The same statement holds on exchanged lines.

These arguments exclude specific apparent violations. They do not bound c in the remaining families.

## Sp(4) representation labels and the two-velocity test

`sp4_towers.py` constructs ten Hermitian generators using the real antisymmetric singlet matrix J_(i,3-i)=(-1)^i. Start from an orthonormal Hermitian SU(4) basis and project each T to (T+J T^T J)/2. Gram-Schmidt with Tr(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2 gives ten generators satisfying T_a J+J T_a^T=0. The local Casimir is checked to be sum_a T_a^2=5I/4.

For each normalized many-body eigenvector the global Casimir is evaluated as

    <C_2> = sum_a ||sum_i T_a(i)|psi>||^2.

The output states of each generator are retained even when they leave the original Cartan sector. Thus this is the full global Casimir, not its incorrect projection within the input sector. At the adjacent momentum, the two lowest Cartan-zero states at the tested points have C_2=3 (adjoint 10), while a separate state has C_2=2 (vector 5). These representation labels are numerical lattice results. The code also applies the complete Casimir within the invariant Cartan sector and records ||(C_2-<C_2>)psi||^2, so a coincidental expectation value in a mixed representation is not accepted as a label.

The additional continuum hypothesis is SO(5)_1 times Ising, equivalently five Majoranas of velocity v_5 and one of velocity v_1. It has c_T=5/2+1/2=3. The five-dimensional mixed bilinear has velocity v_mix=(v_5+v_1)/2, while the adjoint current has velocity v_5. Therefore

    v_1 = 2 v_mix - v_5,
    A_predicted = pi/6 * [(5/2) v_5 + (1/2) v_1]
                = pi/6 * (2 v_5 + v_mix).

We identify the lowest adjoint and vector at the adjacent momentum with these currents as an assumption. Their representations alone do not prove that identification. The independent test is whether velocities from these excitation energies predict A from ground-state scaling. The data at theta=2pi/3 and pi agree within 0.7 percent using L=4,8,12 fits. The single-velocity division at the same points gives apparent charges 3.422 and 4.720. This is evidence for a total charge 3 with split velocities, not a numerical demonstration of charge above 3.

## Spin-1 saturation control

At beta=1, X+X^2=P+I. Each of the three color populations is conserved even when Delta and D break SU(3). The local matrix is

    h = P+I+(Delta-1) Sz Sz + D (Sz^2 tensor I + I tensor Sz^2)/2.

Relative to the all-zero product's bond energy 2, the potentially lowest two-site eigenvalues are D/2-2 for the antisymmetric |0,+> or |0,-> pair, D-1-Delta for the antisymmetric |+,-> pair, and D+Delta-1 for equal nonzero spins. Consequently D>=max(4,1+|Delta|) proves h>=2I and E0=2L for the all-zero product state. A single nonzero spin above that vacuum has

    E(q)-E0 = D-2+2cos(q).

At D=4 it is quadratic around q=pi. The saturation point is gapless, but it does not furnish a relativistic CFT counterexample. The targeted D=3,3.5,4,4.5 calculations check the approach to this endpoint. Their full charge and momentum minima are retained.

## Exact spin-orbital dimer point and strict saturation gap

At x=y=3/4 with positive product sign, the unshifted bond is P_triplet^S P_triplet^T. Put spin singlets on alternating bonds and orbital singlets on the complementary bonds. Every bond then annihilates the state, since at least one factor is a singlet projector's orthogonal complement. Translation by one site gives a second ground state. Their translation eigenstate combinations have Schmidt rank at most eight across a contiguous interval, so their entropy is bounded by 3 log 2. This constructs exact dimerized ground states, but does not prove a bulk spectral gap or a complete ground-space classification.

For the spin-1 saturation bound above, let mu=min(D/2-2,D-1-|Delta|)>0. Then

    h-2I >= mu [I-|00><00|]
           >= (mu/2) [n tensor I + I tensor n],

where n=Sz^2. Summing bonds gives H-2LI >= mu sum_i n_i. The all-zero vector is therefore the unique ground state and every orthogonal state has energy at least mu above it. This is an actual gap proof for D>max(4,1+|Delta|), not a finite-size extrapolation.
