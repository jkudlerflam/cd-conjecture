# C124. Reconnection for commuting bonds and the remaining thermal estimate

2026-10-30. Natural logarithms. Status tags apply to the paragraphs and formulas that follow them.

## 1. Definitions and scope

[Assumed] Use the Hamiltonians, optimized bond half-spread M, and vacuum-compatible ring lengths in the background specification. Set beta=N tau. For the L-ring write

    F=exp(-tau H_L/4),  E=exp(-tau H_L/2),
    W_s=F P_(s_N) E ... E P_(s_1) F,
    q_s=Tr(W_s W_s^dagger),  S_N=sum_s q_s^2.

The denominator D_N=Q_D is the same positive channel construction on the 2L-ring, with both sites 0,L measured at each of the N times. Thus G_N=log(S_N/D_N). The full recorded histories are used. They are not replaced by coherent Euclidean amplitudes.

[Proved, inherited from C121] For any measurement basis and any finite Hermitian Hamiltonian,

    R_L-3N log d <= G_N <= R_L+2N log d,                 (1)
    R_L=2 log Z_L(beta)-log Z_(2L)(beta).

[Proved here] If adjacent bonds commute, there is an explicitly specified on-site basis in which G_N<=0 for every N,tau and L>=2. No sign condition on the bond matrix is needed. In every other on-site measurement basis,

    G_N <= log r+2N log d <= (2N+1)log d,               (2)

where r<=d is the number of central sectors in the interaction-algebra decomposition below. Thus b_0=log d, b_1=2 log d suffice for this subclass in an arbitrary basis, at every a>0. In the adapted basis b_0=b_1=0 suffice. The same conclusions hold if the gauge class of h has a commuting representative, since the ring Hamiltonian and all channel traces are unchanged by the coboundary.

[Open] The commuting assumption is substantial. This does not prove the lemma for noncommuting stoquastic chains, Heisenberg, XXZ, SU(3), or general interacting critical chains. Under the stated unitary CFT hypothesis the commuting subclass has A=0, as shown below. Its significance here is an exact quantum separator with entangled bond eigenstates, not a new positive-A critical family.

## 2. The on-site interaction algebras

[Assumed for this section] The same bond h is placed on every oriented edge and [h_12,h_23]=0. Disjoint bonds already commute.

[Proved] Choose a Hermitian operator Schmidt expansion

    h=sum_mu A_mu tensor B_mu,

with both collections linearly independent. Such an expansion follows by a real singular-value decomposition of h in Hermitian orthonormal operator bases. Expanding the adjacent-bond commutator gives

    0=sum_(mu,nu) A_mu tensor [B_mu,A_nu] tensor B_nu.

Apply the dual linear functionals to the first and third factors. Every coefficient vanishes, so [B_mu,A_nu]=0. The two unital finite-dimensional star algebras generated by the A's and B's therefore commute.

[Proved] The finite-dimensional matrix-algebra decomposition gives

    C^d = direct_sum_alpha
            (C^(l_alpha) tensor C^(r_alpha) tensor C^(m_alpha)),
    sum_alpha l_alpha r_alpha m_alpha=d.                 (3)

The incoming B algebra acts only on the l factor and the outgoing A algebra only on the r factor. For completeness, first decompose the outgoing algebra as a direct sum of full matrix algebras acting on their irreducible factors, with multiplicity spaces. Its commutant acts only on those multiplicity spaces and preserves the central blocks. Decompose the incoming algebra within each multiplicity space in the same way. The combined block label is alpha and its remaining multiplicity is m_alpha. This proves (3), including the identities carried by unused factors. Each alpha block has dimension at least one, hence r, the number of blocks, is at most d.

[Proved] In the sector (alpha,beta) on adjacent sites, the bond has the form

    h_(alpha,beta)=k_(alpha,beta) on R_alpha tensor L_beta,

with identity operators on L_alpha,R_beta and the two multiplicity factors. All central dependence is contained in the family k_(alpha,beta). At a fixed sector configuration on the ring, the different k's act on disjoint virtual factors. Their eigenstates can be entangled across the corresponding physical bond.

[Literature attribution] This is the finite-dimensional interaction-algebra mechanism used for commuting two-local Hamiltonians by [Bravyi and Vyalyi, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0308021v2). The short argument above supplies the special one-dimensional form needed here. The recorded-history contraction in Section 4 is derived separately.

## 3. A partition-ratio bound in every basis

[Proved] Define positive numbers and an entrywise positive r by r matrix

    z_(alpha,beta)=Tr_(R_alpha,L_beta) exp[-beta k_(alpha,beta)],
    T_(alpha,beta)=m_alpha z_(alpha,beta).

At fixed sector labels, every virtual bond traces independently. The multiplicity space at site i contributes m_(alpha_i). Summing the sector labels therefore gives

    Z_L= sum_(alpha_0,...,alpha_(L-1))
             product_i m_(alpha_i) z_(alpha_i,alpha_(i+1))
        =Tr T^L.                                          (4)

No Hermiticity or positive semidefiniteness of T is asserted. Let C=T^L. Entrywise nonnegativity and scalar Cauchy-Schwarz give

    (Tr C)^2 <= r sum_alpha C_(alpha,alpha)^2
              <= r sum_(alpha,beta) C_(alpha,beta) C_(beta,alpha)
              =r Tr C^2.

Consequently R_L<=log r. Equation (1) proves (2).

[Proved consistency] At fixed L, divide R_L<=log r by beta and send beta to infinity. This gives E_(2L)-2E_L<=0. If the assumed vacuum Casimir expansion holds, its limit is pi A/(4L), so A<=0. Unitary sectors with nonnegative velocities have A>=0, hence A=0. The theorem neither bounds the number of slow noncommuting sectors nor assumes that number is finite uniformly in d.

## 4. An exact separator for the positive recorded histories

[Proved basis choice] Use the same basis |alpha,l,r,m> from (3) on every site and every circumference. This is a basis-selection rule depending only on h. A nonzero record

    s=(s_1,...,s_N),  s_j=(alpha_j,l_j,r_j,m_j)

has alpha_j independent of j and m_j independent of j. Sector and multiplicity labels are preserved by H. A record which violates either condition has zero weight. The l and r labels are allowed to vary between measurements.

[Proved definitions] For a virtual bond k_(alpha,beta), use the same F,E construction as in Section 1, but pinch only its R_alpha endpoint with the sequence r_s. Call the resulting squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm f^R_(alpha,beta)(r_s). Define f^L_(alpha,beta)(l_t) by pinching only the L_beta endpoint. These numbers are nonnegative, including for complex k. An unmeasured virtual bond contributes z_(alpha,beta) from Section 3, at the full time beta=N tau.

[Proved construction] For valid records s,t at the ends of an arc of L bonds, define

    B_st = sum_(alpha_1,...,alpha_(L-1))
              [product_(j=1)^(L-1) m_(alpha_j)]
              f^R_(alpha_s,alpha_1)(r_s)
              [product_(j=1)^(L-2) z_(alpha_j,alpha_(j+1))]
              f^L_(alpha_(L-1),alpha_t)(l_t).             (5)

Set B_st=0 if either record is invalid. At L=2 the middle product is empty. The seam multiplicity labels are fixed by s,t, so there is no extra endpoint factor m in (5). B is entrywise nonnegative. Its size can be d^N; no bound on the number of full microscopic histories is being assumed.

[Proved factorization] At fixed central labels, H is a sum of operators on disjoint virtual bonds. Every seam projector in the chosen basis is a tensor product of its l,r,m projectors. Thus W_s factors over the virtual bonds, and its squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm factors as well. Unmeasured bonds give z, the two incident bonds give f^R and f^L, and unmeasured multiplicity spaces give m. Summing the unmeasured central labels gives

    q_(L,s)=B_ss.                                         (6)

For the doubled ring, cut it into the oriented arcs 0 to L and L to 0. The projectors at both endpoints still factor into their incoming and outgoing virtual parts. Conditional on the two records s,t, all factors on the two arcs are disjoint. The same calculation now gives

    q_(2L,s,t)=B_st B_ts.                                 (7)

This is an identity for the positive channel histories, not just for their coherent counterparts. There is no complex-amplitude inequality in (6)-(7). Each virtual-bond factor has already been traced as W W^dagger.

[Proved reconnection] Sum (7) over both independent records and use (6). Then

    D_N=sum_(s,t) B_st B_ts,
    S_N=sum_s B_ss^2 <= D_N,
    G_N<=0.                                              (8)

The inequality keeps the diagonal s=t terms of a nonnegative sum. Unlike the generic stoquastic path integral, the record in this basis does separate the two incident quantum factors throughout each interval. That separation, established from commutation, is the extra input.

[Proved checks] At beta=0 only constant records survive, giving G=-log d, consistent with (8). Scalar ring Hamiltonians give the same value. The proof is independent of tau, so taking N=max(1,ceil(beta M/(aL))) causes no additional restriction. No ring-size gap or lower bound on velocity was used.

## 5. The separate top-eigenspace boundary estimate is false

[Proved, inherited notation from C123] On the two-copy and doubled geometries define

    K_S=H_L tensor I+I tensor H_L,   K_D=H_(2L),
    B_j=exp(-tau K_j/2),
    J_S(X)=sum_s (P_s tensor P_s)X(P_s tensor P_s),
    J_D(X)=sum_(s,t) P_(s,t)X P_(s,t),
    T_j=J_j [B_j (.) B_j] J_j,   u_j=J_j(B_j).

The T_j are self-adjoint positive semidefinite operators on Hilbert-Schmidt space. They also preserve positive matrices. C123 proves

    S_N=<u_S,T_S^(N-1)u_S>,
    D_N=<u_D,T_D^(N-1)u_D>.                              (9)

If lambda_j is the top eigenvalue, Pi_j its full eigenspace projector, and w_j=||Pi_j u_j||_2^2, its sufficient estimate is

    G_N <= C_L(tau)+(N-1)kappa_L(tau),
    C_L=log(||u_S||_2^2/w_D),
    kappa_L=log(lambda_S/lambda_D).                       (10)

The inequality is valid. What fails is a bound C_L<=C(d) uniform in h and L.

[Proved counterexample] Take d=3 and the diagonal on-site Hamiltonian

    k_epsilon=diag(0,epsilon,1),
    h=(k_epsilon tensor I+I tensor k_epsilon)/2,
    H_L=sum_i k_epsilon^(i),
    z(t)=1+exp(-epsilon t)+exp(-t),  0<epsilon<1.

The bond's half-spread is 1/2. This is gauge optimal: its expectations in |0,0> and |2,2> differ by one, and any coboundary has zero expectation in each of these uniform product states. Thus M=1/2.

[Proved channel calculation] All projectors commute with H. A record must be constant, and for s=0,1,2 its weight is

    q_s=exp(-beta k_s) z(beta)^(L-1).

Both-seam pinching also leaves the doubled trace unchanged. Therefore

    S_N=z(2beta) z(beta)^(2L-2),
    D_N=z(beta)^(2L),
    G_N=log z(2beta)-2 log z(beta) in [-log 3,0].          (11)

The upper bound follows by expanding z(beta)^2 and keeping its diagonal terms; the lower bound is Cauchy-Schwarz on its three positive summands. In particular, the reconnection lemma holds with zero cost for this example.

[Proved temporal calculation] In the computational matrix-unit basis, T_j multiplies a retained |x><y| by exp[-tau(E_x+E_y)/2]. The unique zero-energy state is the all-zero configuration, on both geometries. Consequently

    lambda_S=lambda_D=1,  w_D=1,  kappa_L=0.

The vector u_S is diagonal and retains precisely the configurations with equal seam labels on its two copies. Its norm is

    ||u_S||_2^2=z(2tau) z(tau)^(2L-2),
    C_L(tau)=log z(2tau)+(2L-2)log z(tau).                (12)

This uses the full top eigenspace; there is no degeneracy convention involved.

[Proved divergence] For each integer L choose epsilon=L^(-2) and tau=2aL=aL/M. Expanding log(1+exp(-x)) at x=0 yields

    C_L(tau)=(2L-1)log 2-2a+O_a(1/L),
    G_N=-log 2+O_(a,N)(L^(-2))                           (13)

for fixed a,N. Hence no finite C(d) bounds the boundary factor, already at fixed d=3. The Hamiltonian is allowed to vary with L because the proposed constants were required to be uniform in h. For each fixed epsilon the eventual low-temperature behavior is harmless; it is the uniform statement that fails.

## 6. The same obstruction with a critical stoquastic chain at d=4

[Proved] Tensor a stoquastic XX spin with a second on-site qubit whose Hamiltonian is k=diag(0,epsilon). The physical dimension is d=4 and

    h_total=h_XX+(k tensor I+I tensor k)/2,
    h_XX=-(Sx tensor Sx+Sy tensor Sy)

on the first species. The summands act on different species. The total bond has M=(1+epsilon)/2. To prove optimality, take its extremal XX Bell eigenstates and the uniform spectator states |00>,|11>. Each test state has identical left and right marginals, so coboundary expectations vanish, and their energy difference is 1+epsilon.

[Assumed infrared identification, inherited for XX] At every fixed epsilon>0 the spectator is gapped with a unique ground state and the first factor has the usual XX c=1 infrared sector. Thus this example has a nontrivial critical infrared limit. The following finite-size obstruction does not depend on that identification.

[Proved tensor factorization] Use product seam projectors. The W operators, the fixed-record weights, the sums S_N,D_N, the temporal transfers and the endpoint vectors factor across the two species. Thus G and kappa add. The full top eigenspace projections also factor, because the transfers have nonnegative spectra with positive top eigenvalues. Therefore C_L adds as well.

[Proved] Choose epsilon=L^(-2), tau=aL/M=2aL/(1+epsilon), and let z_s(t)=1+exp(-epsilon t). The spectator contributes

    C_s=log z_s(2tau)+(2L-2)log z_s(tau)=2L log 2+O_a(1),
    G_s=log z_s(2N tau)-2log z_s(N tau) in [-log 2,0].    (14)

For the XX factor C_XX>=G_XX at N=1 because w_D<=||u_D||_2^2. C118's shifted-grid pressure bounds give

    R_XX(tau)>=-log 2-(5pi/3)tau/L.

Together with (1) and tau/L<=2a this implies

    C_XX>=-4log 2-(10pi/3)a.

Consequently C_total>=2L log 2-O_a(1). The proposed separate boundary estimate fails even in critical stoquastic chains at fixed d=4. Conversely G_total=G_XX+G_s<=G_XX, so the inherited XX reconnection theorem still bounds the actual G_total. This is not a counterexample to the desired lemma or to the velocity-weighted bound.

## 7. A sufficient estimate that retains the thermal contribution

[Proved identities] For a fixed slab length tau, let S_n,D_n use beta=n tau and n measurements, and define

    Gamma_n(tau)=log(S_(n+1)/S_n)-log(D_(n+1)/D_n).

All these quantities are positive before taking logarithms. Equation (9) gives the exact telescoping identity

    G_N=G_1+sum_(n=1)^(N-1) Gamma_n.                    (15)

It also gives a Rayleigh quotient expression. Set v_(j,n)=T_j^((n-1)/2)u_j. Then

    exp(Gamma_n)=
      [<v_(S,n),T_S v_(S,n)>/||v_(S,n)||_2^2]
      /[<v_(D,n),T_D v_(D,n)>/||v_(D,n)||_2^2].         (16)

The endpoint vectors have evolved before the quotient is taken. Neither quotient is replaced by a top eigenvalue. At fixed finite L,tau, Gamma_n tends to kappa_L as n tends to infinity, since the positive top-eigenspace weights are nonzero.

[Open sufficient estimate] Fix a numerical a>0. It would suffice to prove constants c_0(d),c_1(d)>=0 such that

    G_1(L,tau)<=c_0(d),          0<tau M/L<=a,
    Gamma_n(L,tau)<=c_1(d),      a/2<=tau M/L<=a, n>=1,  (17)

uniformly in the chain and vacuum-compatible L, using one basis rule for both geometries. For the prescribed ceiling, N>=2 implies a/2<=tau M/L<=a. Equations (15)-(17) then give G_N<=c_0+c_1(N-1), and hence the requested lemma with b_0=c_0,b_1=c_1. The N=1 case uses the first line alone.

[Proved scope of the reduction] Condition (17) is sufficient and can be stronger than necessary. The exact remaining requirement is the bound on the full sum in (15), not a bound on each increment separately. A bound only on kappa_L controls the asymptotic slope in N and leaves finite-N corrections. A bound on C_L from (10) is unavailable by Sections 5-6. Equation (16) preserves the thermal cancellation that was lost in that boundary estimate.

[Open] Neither line of (17) has been proved for noncommuting stoquastic chains or for arbitrary chains. Stoquasticity makes their evolution matrices entrywise nonnegative but supplies no comparison between the two Rayleigh quotients in (16). The commuting proof succeeds because it supplies the stronger spatial factorization (6)-(7). A low-energy level spacing of order v/L, even when available, neither bounds the thermal multiplicity nor gives a lower bound on v/M. No such premise is used to claim a general proof here.

[Proved conditional consequence, inherited] Any completion G<=b_0+b_1N gives

    A <= [4/(pi a)] [3log d+b_1(d)] M

under the vacuum Casimir assumption. No new universal noncommuting bound on A is established in this folder.

## 8. Numerical verification and limits

[Numerical] The calculations test (6)-(8) with entangled virtual bonds, both real stoquastic and complex, and with a direct sum of distinct central sectors. They also check (2) after an on-site rotation that mixes those sectors. Full small-ring matrices are compared to the independently formed B_st contractions. The direct-sum partition traces are independently compared to (4).

[Numerical] Fresh full-spin XX determinant calculations reach L=8192 and doubled length 16384, retaining both Jordan-Wigner sectors and the zero-mode parity terms. Full-spectrum ED covers XX, Heisenberg and XXZ at Delta=-0.9,0.5 on L=4,6 with doubled rings through 12 sites, and SU(3) at L=3 with doubled length 6. The SU(3) lengths used here are vacuum-compatible. All eigenvalues are retained. Increment checks keep tau fixed as n varies, as required by (15).

[Numerical scope] The requested noncommuting models violate the extension G<=0 outside the commuting hypotheses. Their observed G and increments remain finite on these samples. No finite sample establishes uniformity in L, h or crossover scales. The diagonal and critical-spectator counterexamples are analytic; their large-L computations check the displayed formulas rather than replace the proof.
