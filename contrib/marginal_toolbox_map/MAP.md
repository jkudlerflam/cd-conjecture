# Marginal-side toolbox map for the kinematic conjecture (K)

2026-11-07, Opus 5.5 literature mapper. Complements `contrib/rigorous_toolbox_map/TOOLBOX.md` (Bethe ansatz, constructive RG, cluster expansions, gap stability, thermal locality), which is not repeated here. This file covers the quantum-information and quantum-marginal side.

Target, from the brief. For L >= 2, Omega_L is the set of nearest-neighbour marginals of translation-invariant states on the L ring of C^d, and Omega_inf the set of nearest-neighbour marginals of translation-invariant states on Z. The conjecture is

    (K)  every element of Omega_L is within trace distance K(d)/L^2 of Omega_inf, for all L,

equivalently (cd_free_route PROOF section 1, with the C132 correction that the dual-M norm equals the trace norm on the gauge-annihilated slice) E_L >= e_0 L - K(d) M(h)/L for every bond h.

Tags. [Lit, read: X] means I opened the source and read statement X (abstract only when marked). [Lit, recalled, unverified] means I did not open it in this survey. [Proved, elementary] means a short argument given here that the next agent should re-check. [Numerical] refers to `lti_probe.py` in this folder, output in `lti_probe_output.txt` (cvxpy 1.9.2 with Clarabel, already installed, run locally in a few minutes). [Assessment] is my judgment. Every arXiv ID below was checked against its abstract page on 2026-11-07.

## 0. Bottom line

[Assessment] No theorem in the quantum-marginal or quantum-information literature gives a 1/L^2 (or any o(1/L)) comparison between finite-volume and infinite-volume translation-invariant marginal sets, for any interacting non-commuting class. The best proved rate anywhere in this literature is O(1/n), by tiling or by cutting one ring bond (Fawzi–Fawzi–Scalet arXiv:2305.06855 Appendix B; Blakaj–Wolf arXiv:2308.14585 Proposition 2). Rates beyond 1/n exist only for commuting Hamiltonians at finite temperature (Fawzi–Fawzi–Scalet arXiv:2311.18706 Theorem 1.5, exponential) and for weak perturbations of separable chains (Lin–Rivera Cardoso–Bondesan arXiv:2609.40336, abstract), neither of which touches a critical ground state. The rigorous O(1/L) Casimir-type lower bounds for non-free interacting chains that I know of are the ones already in the repo (frustration-free and ferromagnetic classes, C132) and the perturbative constructive-RG results of TOOLBOX section 7. I found none in this literature.

The single most useful finding is numerical, from Kull, Schuch, Dive and Navascués (arXiv:2212.03014, PRX 14, 021008): the locally translation-invariant (LTI) outer relaxation of Omega_inf at level n has energy error Delta E_LTI(n) proportional to n^{-alpha} with alpha about 2 in all six models they test (critical TFI, Heisenberg, XX, J1-J2, and gapped XXZ at Delta = 2 and spin-1 Heisenberg, the gapped ones converging faster), with different even-n and odd-n slopes. Since every ring marginal set Omega_L with L >= n sits inside the level-n LTI relaxation (section 7), a uniform rate e_0 - E_LTI(n) <= K M/n^2 would imply (K). A small probe in this folder adds a structural observation that I did not find in print. On Heisenberg, XX and critical TFI at d = 2 the LTI optimum at level n <= 6 coincides with the minimum of E_L/L over rings L >= n to solver accuracy (1e-8). So for these bonds the LTI hierarchy is the ring problem in disguise, and its n^{-2} rate is the Casimir term itself.

Honest verdict on 1/L^2. Nothing in this literature proves 1/L^2 for an interacting class. The LTI direction is ranked first because it converts (K) into the rate of a convex hierarchy that has a dual operator-inequality certificate, that has been measured at n^{-2} by an independent group on six models, and whose optimizer appears (in the small probe) to be a ring or twisted-ring state, so that numerical certificates at small n are directly about the ring problem. It is not ranked first because anything in it is proved.

## 1. Translation-invariant marginal problems and their hierarchies

Barthel and Hübener, "Solving condensed-matter ground-state problems by semidefinite relaxations", PRL 108, 200404 (2012), arXiv:1106.4966. [Lit, read: abstract] Relax the ground-state problem to an SDP in the thermodynamic limit, giving strict lower bounds on the energy density, compared with Anderson bounds on XXZ and spinless Hubbard. Constants: none, no rate. Fit: irrelevant to the rate; it is the generic outer approximation.

Baumgratz and Plenio, "Lower bounds for ground states of condensed matter systems", NJP 14, 023027 (2012), arXiv:1106.5275. [Lit, read: abstract] Moment-matrix relaxation with translation invariance, Ising and Heisenberg tests, convergence proof of the numerical solver (not of the hierarchy). Fit: irrelevant to the rate.

Kull, Schuch, Dive, Navascués, "Lower bounding ground-state energies of local Hamiltonians through the renormalization group", PRX 14, 021008 (2024), arXiv:2212.03014. [Lit, read: Sections II and V of the arXiv PDF, Figs. 2 and 5] Defines the LTI problem E_LTI(n) = min Tr[h rho^(2)] over n-site states with Tr_L rho^(n) = Tr_R rho^(n), notes E_LTI(n) is nondecreasing and converges to e_0, and compresses the hierarchy by coarse-graining maps taken from a variational MPS or tree tensor network. Rigorous lower bounds result. Statement relevant here, all numerical: "the accuracy obtained from the exact hierarchy truncated at level n displays an algebraic decay n^{-alpha} with alpha about 2" (their Section II F), seen "throughout all models" in Section V, with gapped models (XXZ at Delta = 2, spin-1 Heisenberg, gapped TFI) converging super-algebraically, and even-n and odd-n slopes differing. Their relaxation reaches effective LTI sizes of about 120 (critical TFI) and 60 (Heisenberg). Constants: none proved. Fit: suggests the construction. This is the strongest available evidence that the LTI outer approximation converges at the Casimir rate, which is exactly what (K) needs (section 7). Their compressed relaxation is also the only practical way to probe effective n in the tens.

Fawzi, Fawzi, Scalet, "Entropy constraints for ground energy optimization", J. Math. Phys. (2024), arXiv:2305.06855. [Lit, read: abstract, Section 4 and Appendix B of the arXiv PDF] Adds von Neumann entropy (weak monotonicity) constraints to LTI-type relaxations, shows these are implied by doubling the number of sites (so at most a quadratic saving in matrix size), and relates the approach to the Markov entropy decomposition. Statement relevant here, proved in Appendix B: the LTI relaxation value converges to e_0 at rate O(1/l), by tiling an infinite chain with copies of the optimal l-site state and averaging translations; only the seam bond is wrong, so the error is at most 2^D ||h||/l in D dimensions. Constants: ||h||, dimension-free. Fit: supplies the only proved rate, which is the trivial one. It is the precise statement that (K) improves by one power of 1/n.

Fawzi, Fawzi, Scalet, "A subpolynomial-time algorithm for the free energy of one-dimensional quantum systems in the thermodynamic limit", Quantum 7, 1011 (2023), arXiv:2209.14989. [Lit, read: abstract] For 2-local TI chains at any fixed T > 0, free energy to additive error eps in time O((1/eps)^c) for every c > 0, via the spectral radius of a noncommutative transfer map whose leading eigenvector approximates Gibbs marginals. Constants: depend on T, degenerate as T -> 0, and the abstract notes the ground-state problem is expected to be hard. Fit: irrelevant at zero temperature. Scalet, arXiv:2402.19030 (found in search, not opened) improves the runtime with Araki expansionals, which puts it under lesson 4.

Fawzi, Fawzi, Scalet, "Certified algorithms for equilibrium states of local quantum Hamiltonians", arXiv:2311.18706. [Lit, read: abstract and Theorems 1.1 to 1.5 of the arXiv PDF] Convex hierarchies (with an added KMS-type constraint) giving certified two-sided bounds on equilibrium and ground-state observables, decidability of TI ground-state observables (Theorem 1.4), and Theorem 1.5: for commuting TI Hamiltonians, in D = 1 at every finite beta (and in D = 2 at high temperature), the hierarchy error is at most c1 ||O|| e^{-c2 l}, with c1, c2 depending on the interaction and dimension. Fit: irrelevant. Commuting chains already have K = 0, and no zero-temperature rate is stated.

Lin, Rivera Cardoso, Bondesan, "Local relaxation hierarchies for quantum ground state energies: convergence guarantees and message passing algorithms", arXiv:2609.40336 (September 2026). [Lit, read: abstract only] Pairwise-consistency level exact for commuting Hamiltonians on trees; an interval hierarchy converging exponentially in interval size for weak perturbations of separable Hamiltonians on a chain; message passing in O(n/eps^2) and O(n/eps). Fit: irrelevant at criticality. The exponential rate is the perturbative, gapped regime.

Lin and Lindsey, "Variational embedding for quantum many-body problems", arXiv:1910.00560 (CPAM, per the search result; the abstract page lists no journal). [Lit, read: abstract] Lower-bound SDP relaxations of marginal representability mixing local and global constraints, with a TI one-dimensional two-marginal variant. No rate. Fit: irrelevant.

Han, Hartnoll, Kruthoff, "Bootstrapping matrix quantum mechanics", PRL 125, 041601 (2020), arXiv:2004.10212, and Han, "Quantum many-body bootstrap", arXiv:2006.06002. [Lit, read: abstracts] Positivity of moment matrices plus stationarity <[H, O]> = 0, giving lower bounds on ground energies (Hubbard in 1D and 2D in the second paper). No rate. Fit: irrelevant to the rate. The repo's `contrib/bootstrap_ring/README.md` already ran the ring-aware version and found that fixed-support bootstraps keep a per-site offset, so they cannot certify A. That negative result is the bootstrap-side analogue of the open L-LTI lemma.

Wang, Surace, Frérot, Legat, Renou, Magron, Acín, "Certifying ground-state properties of quantum many-body systems", PRX 14, 031006 (2024), arXiv:2310.05844. [Lit, read: abstract] Combine SDP lower bounds and variational upper bounds to certify ground-state observables on Heisenberg chains and lattices with hundreds of sites, using symmetry and sparsity. Fit: a numerical tool. On rings it could produce certified two-sided brackets of E_L at L in the hundreds, which is what a large-L falsification test would need, but it proves nothing about rates.

Undecidability context. The TI ground-energy problem is QMA_EXP-complete (Gottesman–Irani, arXiv:0905.2419, already in `lit.md`), and FFS 2311.18706 Theorem 1.4 nonetheless proves TI ground-state observables are computable. Fit: neither bears on a uniform constant.

## 2. Extendibility and de Finetti theorems with rates

Christandl, König, Mitchison, Renner, "One-and-a-half quantum de Finetti theorems", CMP 273, 473 (2007), arXiv:quant-ph/0602130. [Lit, read: abstract] De Finetti for U(d) representations with near-optimal bounds; the standard form is a 2-marginal of an N-exchangeable state within O(d^2/N) of separable [Lit, recalled, unverified constant]. Fit: irrelevant. Complete-graph symmetry, and the rate is 1/N.

Navascués, Owari, Plenio, "The power of symmetric extensions for entanglement detection", PRA 80, 052306 (2009), arXiv:0906.2731. [Lit, read: abstract] A perturbation of order O(1/N) is sufficient and in general necessary to make an N-Bose-extendible state separable, while for states with PPT symmetric extensions the required noise falls at least as O(1/N^2). Fit: suggests a construction. It is the clearest example where an extra positivity condition (partial transpose, a reflection-type symmetry) upgrades 1/N to 1/N^2. Section 7 notes that the LTI optimum coincides with ring energies exactly for real reflection-symmetric bonds, where the probe shows no gap at all, and that a gap opens for chiral bonds. Whether a reflection or time-reversal structure is what produces the second-order cancellation in the ring problem is a question I could not settle from the literature.

Fang and Fawzi, "The sum-of-squares hierarchy on the sphere, and applications in quantum information theory", Math. Program. (2020), arXiv:1908.05155. [Lit, read: abstract] SOS relaxations for homogeneous polynomials on the sphere converge at rate O(d^2/l^2) for l >= Omega(d), a quadratic improvement over Reznick and Doherty–Wehner, by a polynomial kernel technique; the matrix-valued version covers Best Separable State. Fit: suggests a construction. The mechanism (a kernel with vanishing first moment and second moment of order 1/l^2) is the same as the repo's momentum box smoothing (1 - sinc(pi/L) <= pi^2/(6L^2)), which proves (K) for quasi-free chains. An interacting ring has no momentum decomposition, which is where this direction stalls.

Brandão and Harrow, "Product-state approximations to quantum ground states", CMP 342, 47 (2016), arXiv:1310.0017. [Lit, read: abstract] De Finetti without symmetry; product states approximate ground energies of 2-local Hamiltonians on high-degree or low-expansion graphs. Fit: irrelevant. One-dimensional chains are the worst case for it.

Jeronimo, Wu, Xu, "Optimal quantum de Finetti theorems via argmax rounding", arXiv:2608.02590 (August 2026). [Lit, read: abstract] Bosonic two-site marginals within sqrt(d-1)/(N-1) of a mixture of products, O(d/N) for exchangeable states, O(t d/N) for t-site marginals, none at 1/N^2. Fit: irrelevant.

Allerstorfer, Christandl, Grinko, Nechita, Ozols, Rochette, Verduyn Lunel, "Monogamy of highly symmetric states", arXiv:2309.16655 (CMP, DOI 10.1007/s00220-025-05483-3). [Lit, read: abstract; graph-extendibility framing from a search snippet] Exact extendibility thresholds for Werner, isotropic and Brauer states on complete graphs; the paper frames G-extendibility for general graphs. A cycle graph C_L is the ring, but the abstract treats only complete graphs. Fit: irrelevant as it stands. A cycle-graph extendibility theorem for Werner-type two-qubit states would be an exact description of a slice of Omega_L at d = 2, and could serve as a test bed.

O'Connor and Wootters, "Entangled rings", PRA 63, 052302 (2001), arXiv:quant-ph/0009041. [Lit, read: abstract] Maximal nearest-neighbour entanglement of TI qubit rings within an ansatz, compared with the antiferromagnetic ring ground state. Fit: a historical example of an L-dependent face of Omega_L at d = 2 (concurrence direction), not a rate.

## 3. Finitely correlated states and MPS

Fannes, Nachtergaele, Werner, "Finitely correlated states on quantum spin chains", CMP 144, 443 (1992), no arXiv; Project Euclid open access. [Lit, read: abstract via ADS/Project Euclid search] Finitely correlated (C*-finitely correlated, MPS) states are dense in the TI states on Z; purely generated ones are ground states of gapped finite-range parent Hamiltonians. Fit: supplies the inner approximation of Omega_inf, with no rate.

Verstraete and Cirac, "Matrix product states represent ground states faithfully", PRB 73, 094423 (2006), arXiv:cond-mat/0505140. [Lit, read: abstract and Lemma 3 in the arXiv PDF] For a TI state on Z with half-chain Schmidt coefficients lambda_alpha, there is a TI MPS of bond dimension D whose two-site marginal sigma_D obeys ||sigma - sigma_D||_1 <= 2(sqrt(eps(D)) + eps(D)) + gamma_D/2, with eps(D) the Schmidt tail beyond D and gamma_D a related tail sum. For critical finite chains, D poly(L) suffices for accuracy eps_0/L in norm. Also the first plots of Omega_inf at d = 2 in the XX-ZZ plane. Fit: inner approximation with constants set by the entanglement spectrum, which at criticality decays only polynomially. Useful for the falsification numerics (an inner hull of Omega_inf), not for a proof of (K).

Verstraete, Porras, Cirac, "DMRG and periodic boundary conditions: a quantum information perspective", PRL 93, 227205 (2004), arXiv:cond-mat/0404706. [Lit, read: abstract] Periodic-boundary MPS fix the poor DMRG performance on rings. That an open-boundary MPS needs roughly D^2 to represent a periodic state of PBC bond dimension D is [Lit, recalled, unverified]. Pirvu, Verstraete, Vidal, PRB 83, 125104 (2011), arXiv:1005.5195. [Lit, read: abstract] TI PBC MPS for rings, cost g(D, xi/N) D^3; for critical rings a model-dependent D(N) is needed to reproduce the power-law correlations across the whole ring. Pérez-García, Verstraete, Wolf, Cirac, "Matrix product state representations", Quantum Inf. Comput. 7, 401 (2007), arXiv:quant-ph/0608197. [Lit, read: abstract] Canonical forms with and without translation symmetry. That some TI ring states need a TI tensor of bond dimension growing with L (momentum states) is [Lit, recalled, unverified]. Fit for all three: they quantify the cost of periodicity, which is the right object, but in bond dimension, not in the marginal. The natural ring-to-Z map (take a TI PBC tensor A for the ring state and use the same A on Z) changes the two-site marginal by terms of order (|mu_2|/mu_1)^L in the transfer-matrix spectrum. For critical rings both D and the transfer gap scale with L, so this gives no rate. Irrelevant for a proof.

Huang, "Approximating local properties by tensor network states with constant bond dimension", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 72, 4930 (2026), arXiv:1903.10048. [Lit, read: abstract] An area law for a Rényi entropy with alpha < 1 gives an MPS reproducing all local properties to accuracy delta with D = poly(1/delta), almost linear for gapped ground states. Dalzell and Brandão, "Locally accurate MPS approximations for ground states of one-dimensional gapped local Hamiltonians", Quantum 3, 187 (2019), arXiv:1903.10241. [Lit, read: abstract] D = (k/eps)^{1+o(1)} for k-local accuracy on gapped ground states, and exp(O(k/eps)) for completely general states, independent of chain length. Fit: these produce open-boundary MPS, not TI states on Z, and a periodization step is still needed. Irrelevant for (K), though the general-state bound exp(O(k/eps)) shows that local marginals of arbitrary ring states have N-independent MPS descriptions, which is consistent with (K) and says nothing about its rate.

Schuch, Wolf, Verstraete, Cirac, "Entropy scaling and simulability by matrix product states", PRL 100, 030504 (2008), arXiv:0705.0292. [Lit, read: abstract] A von Neumann area law alone does not guarantee MPS approximability. Fit: a caution for any MPS-based inner approximation used in numerics.

## 4. Recovery maps and approximate quantum Markov chains

Fawzi and Renner, CMP 340, 575 (2015), arXiv:1410.0664; Sutter, Fawzi, Renner, Proc. R. Soc. A 472, 20150623 (2016), arXiv:1504.07251; Junge, Renner, Sutter, Wilde, Winter, AHP 19, 2955 (2018), arXiv:1509.07127. [Lit, read: abstracts] I(A:C|B) bounds from below the distance (fidelity form) between rho_ABC and a recovered state R_{B->BC}(rho_AB), with a universal (rotated, twirled Petz) recovery map depending only on rho_BC. Constants: dimension-free. Fit: supplies the admissible channels of C132 Lemma P', and nothing more. The C132 audit already records that at criticality the CMI of the two end sites of a half-ring block is (c/3) pi^2/L^2 [heuristic, CFT], so an information-only closure carries c rather than A, up to logarithms.

Kato and Brandão, "Quantum approximate Markov chains are thermal", CMP 370, 117 (2019), arXiv:1609.06636; Kuwahara, Kato, Brandão, PRL 124, 220601 (2020), arXiv:1910.09425; Bluhm, Capel, Pérez-Hernández, Quantum 6, 650 (2022), arXiv:2104.04419. [Lit, read: abstracts] 1D Gibbs states are approximate Markov chains with CMI decaying like exp(-q(beta) sqrt(|B|)) (Kato–Brandão), exponentially above a threshold temperature (KKB), and exponential mutual-information decay at every temperature in 1D (BCPH). Constants degenerate as beta -> infinity. Fit: irrelevant at zero temperature (lesson 4, already in TOOLBOX section 3).

Svetlichnyy and Kennedy, "Decay of quantum conditional mutual information for purely generated finitely correlated states", J. Math. Phys. 63, 072201 (2022), arXiv:2210.09387. [Lit, read: abstract] Purely generated FCS are approximate quantum Markov chains with trace-distance error and CMI exponentially small in the buffer. Fit: the gapped analogue of Lemma P', with exponential rather than 1/L^2 behaviour. Irrelevant at criticality but a useful sanity check: on gapped chains the Petz defect should decay exponentially, which the repo's TFI g = 1.6 numbers show.

Kim, "Markovian marginals", arXiv:1609.08579 (no journal listed). [Lit, read: abstract] Locally consistent marginals with an internal exact quantum Markov structure always have a global extension, by a constructive argument. Fit: suggests a construction. It is the exact (zero-CMI) version of the periodization channel. An approximate version with an energy-weighted error is what Lemma P' asks for, and I found no such theorem.

Poulin and Hastings, "Markov entropy decomposition: a variational dual for quantum belief propagation", PRL 106, 080403 (2011), arXiv:1012.2050. [Lit, read: abstract] Free-energy lower bound from strong subadditivity plus relaxed local consistency, dual to quantum belief propagation. Fit: irrelevant to the rate, but it is the finite-temperature ancestor of the FFS entropy-constrained LTI relaxation.

Critical ground states. Zou, Siva, Soejima, Mong, Zaletel, "Universal tripartite entanglement in one-dimensional many-body systems", PRL 126, 120501 (2021), arXiv:2011.11864. [Lit, read: abstract] Tripartite measures g and h of 1D critical ground states depend on the infrared CFT (h only on c). [Assessment] Together with the scale invariance of contiguous-block CMI in CFT, this confirms that critical ground states are not approximate Markov chains at fixed cross ratio. Only the single-site end configuration of the periodization channel has CMI of order 1/L^2, and its coefficient is c, not A. I found no lattice theorem bounding recovery errors of critical ground states.

## 5. Finite-size lower bounds via operator inequalities

Anderson, "Limits on the energy of the antiferromagnetic ground state", Phys. Rev. 83, 1260 (1951) [Lit, recalled, unverified beyond search snippets]. Cluster bound e_0 >= lambda_min(cluster)/(cluster bonds). Refinements by Nishimori–Ozeki (variational bond weights) and longer-range counterterms that cancel on tiling [Lit, read: search snippet citing cond-mat/0312160]. Osborne, "Lower bound for the ground-state energy density of a 1D quantum spin system", arXiv:cond-mat/0508428. [Lit, read: first two pages] Golden–Thompson plus a 16 x 16 transfer matrix gives lower bounds on e_0, and an example where the blocked Anderson bound is stuck at -1/m, so Anderson bounds converge only as 1/m in general. Kránitz and Penc, arXiv:2601.07800 [Lit, read: search snippet only] note that cluster size alone does not guarantee improvement. Fit: wrong direction as stated (they bound e_0 or E_L from below by cluster energies), with O(1/n) convergence. The useful observation is section 7: the dual of the LTI problem is the Anderson bound with an arbitrary telescoping counterterm X, and (K) follows from a rate for that certificate.

Sum-of-squares certificates on rings. The SDP lower bounds of Barthel–Hübener, Baumgratz–Plenio, Han and Wang et al. are SOS certificates. None states a rate. The repo's `bootstrap_ring` result (fixed support keeps an offset of 5e-4 to 8e-4 per site at d = 2, shrinking roughly as 1/k^2 between k = 6 and 7) is consistent with the LTI picture: the offset is e_0 minus the level-k relaxation, and its 1/k^2 decay is the Casimir term of the smallest admissible ring.

Rigorous E_L >= e_0 L - c/L for non-free interacting classes. [Assessment] I found none in this literature. The repo has the frustration-free and ferromagnetic Heisenberg classes (C132, K = 0 for the energy) and quasi-free chains; TOOLBOX section 7 has constructive RG near free points and Bethe-ansatz asymptotics at fixed parameters, neither stated as a uniform finite-L theorem.

## 6. Other items bearing on Omega_L versus Omega_inf

Blakaj and Wolf, "On the set of reduced states of translation invariant, infinite quantum systems", LMP 114, 28 (2024), arXiv:2308.14585. [Lit, read: full text] Red_d (= Omega_inf) is convex and compact (Prop. 1), e_0(h) = min over Red_d (Prop. 2, proved by closing an open chain into a ring at cost ||h||/N, the O(1/N) argument), and Red_d is not semialgebraic for d >= 2 (Theorem 2), so every algebraic inner or outer hierarchy is exact only in the limit; under Schanuel's conjecture, adding exp, log, arctan does not help either (Theorem 4). Constants: none. Fit: a constraint, not a step. Each Omega_L is semialgebraic, so (K) cannot hold with K = 0 at any finite L for every direction, which the singlet already shows. It also rules out any proof of (K) that identifies Omega_inf with a finite-level object.

Wang, Singh, Navascués, "Entanglement and nonlocality in infinite 1D systems", PRL 118, 230401 (2017), arXiv:1608.03485. [Lit, read: abstract] Classical TI boxes in 1D form a polytope; linear witnesses detect entanglement of infinite TI states from the nearest-neighbour marginal; some separable marginals have only entangled TI extensions. Yang et al., npj Quantum Inf. 8, 89 (2022), arXiv:2112.11646 [Lit, read: abstract] and Zeng, Yang, Zhang, Wang, arXiv:2606.19033 and arXiv:2608.10365 (2026) [Lit, read: abstracts] study contextuality witnesses as TI Hamiltonians; the 2606 paper reports that finite periodic rings at selected periods reproduce the infinite-chain quantum value exactly in several models, and the 2608 paper builds SDP outer and symmetrized-MPS inner approximations for period-p block structure. Fit: irrelevant to the rate. The exact ring-equals-chain cases are frustration-free or classical-bound phenomena (K = 0 directions).

## 7. The LTI reformulation and a small probe

Notation. For n >= 2 let LTI_n be the set of states rho_n on n sites with Tr_1 rho_n = Tr_n rho_n, and pi_2(LTI_n) its set of (1,2) marginals. Then E_LTI(n) = min over pi_2(LTI_n) of Tr(h rho).

(a) [Proved, elementary] Omega_L is contained in pi_2(LTI_n) for 2 <= n <= L, since the n-site window marginal of a TI ring state is LTI. Omega_inf is contained in pi_2(LTI_n) for all n. Hence E_LTI(n) <= min(e_0, min over L >= n of E_L/L).

(b) [Proved, elementary] Strong duality (the maximally mixed state is strictly feasible) gives

    E_LTI(n) = max { t : h (x) 1_{n-2} + X (x) 1 - 1 (x) X >= t 1 },   X Hermitian on n - 1 sites.

Summing translates of the operator inequality around any ring of length L >= n telescopes the X terms and gives E_L >= t L. So the dual is an Anderson bound with a telescoping counterterm, and it lower-bounds every ring of length at least n at once.

(c) [Proved, elementary] Consequently the uniform LTI rate

    (L-LTI)  e_0(h) - E_LTI(n) <= K M(h)/n^2   for all h and n

implies (K) with the same K, taking n = L. (L-LTI) is at least as strong as (K) and could be strictly stronger.

(d) [Proved, elementary, check] The periodization construction of cd_free_route PROOF section 3 and its C132 extension to any channel R with R(rho_{l-1}) = rho_l use only Tr_1 rho_l = Tr_l rho_l. They apply verbatim to an LTI state, so Lemma P' can be posed for the LTI optimizer, where complementary slackness (H_n - t + X (x) 1 - 1 (x) X) rho_n = 0 is available as the energy-weighted input.

(e) [Numerical, `lti_probe.py`, d = 2, n <= 6, rings to L = 16] E_LTI(n) agrees with min over L >= n of E_L/L to 1e-8 for the Heisenberg bond (values -0.75, -0.5, -0.5, -0.467129, -0.467129 at n = 2 to 6, the rings 2, 4, 4, 6, 6), for XX, and for critical TFI (h = -ZZ - (X (x) 1 + 1 (x) X)/2, where every L >= n is attained at L = n). Kull et al.'s different even-n and odd-n slopes fit this picture, since odd rings are frustrated. For the chiral bond h = -|psi><psi|, psi = (|01> + i|10>)/sqrt(2), which is the Heisenberg bond minus 1/4 after a pi/2-per-site twist (so e_0 = -ln 2), E_LTI(n) lies below every untwisted ring of length at most 16 (by 0.25 at n = 2 and 0.0107 at n = 5, 6) and equals the twisted Heisenberg ring value E_6^Heis/6 - 1/4 = -0.717129 at n = 5, 6. The interpretation is that n-site windows of twisted rings are LTI. For two random complex bonds the gap to the best ring vanishes (to 1e-8) at n = 6 for both and at n = 4 for one, and is otherwise between 5.6e-4 and 2.9e-2, shrinking with n, with the n = 2 values 0.11 to 0.19 set aside; for one real random bond that is not swap-symmetric and has strongly L-dependent ring energies, the gap is 7.6e-3 to 2.4e-2. In every case n^2 (e_0 - E_LTI(n))/M stays below about 1 at n = 6 on the crude e_0 estimates available, so the probe contains no evidence against (L-LTI). The complex SDP at n = 7 failed in Clarabel and was not pursued.

[Assessment] What (e) suggests is a "ring saturation" phenomenon. For reflection-symmetric real bonds, the LTI relaxation at level n appears to be the minimum over rings of length at least n; for bonds with an on-site symmetry it includes twisted rings. If that holds in general (with twisted rings), (L-LTI) is equivalent to the twisted-ring form of (K), Kull et al.'s n^{-2} data become evidence for (K) itself, and the LTI dual certificate X becomes an object one can compute, inspect and try to construct. If it fails, the LTI optimum exhibits a "fake" boundary-free state that no ring realizes, and the size of the gap measures how much stronger (L-LTI) is than (K).

## 8. Top three directions and the lemma each leaves

1. LTI rate via telescoping certificates. Missing lemma (L-LTI) at d = 2: for every bond h on C^2 (x) C^2 and every n, there is a Hermitian X on n - 1 sites with h (x) 1 + X (x) 1 - 1 (x) X >= (e_0(h) - K M(h)/n^2) 1. A weaker but sufficient intermediate is ring saturation for reflection-symmetric bonds plus (K) for that class. Nothing proves 1/n^2; the proved rate is 1/n (FFS 2305.06855 App. B). DRAFT_C135 attacks this.

2. Periodization channels for LTI optimizers. Missing lemma: Lemma P' (C132) for the LTI optimizer, using complementary slackness with the dual X in place of ring ground-state stationarity. The recovery literature supplies the channels and nothing at criticality.

3. Second-order smoothing. Missing lemma: for d = 2 chains with a U(1) symmetry, a TI state on Z built from the twisted-ring ground states psi_theta, theta in [-pi/L, pi/L), whose energy density exceeds the flux-averaged ring energy by O(M/L^2). This is the interacting analogue of the repo's momentum box smoothing and of the Fang–Fawzi kernel mechanism. It would cover the U(1) stratum of DRAFT_C131 by a route independent of the Bethe ansatz, but I found no construction of such a state in the literature.
