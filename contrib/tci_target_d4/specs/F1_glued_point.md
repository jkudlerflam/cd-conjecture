# F1. Relevant symmetric operators at a glued Sp(4)_1 x tricritical-Ising point of a spin-3/2 chain

You are working closed-book. Do not consult files, papers or the internet. Show every step, and check each numerical claim (conformal weights, fusion rules, monodromy charges, spins) by an independent route, for instance a short script you write and run. State plainly where an argument is a heuristic rather than a proof.

## Lattice setting

A periodic chain of L sites, each carrying C^4 = spin 3/2 of SU(2). The Hamiltonian is H = Σ_j h_{j,j+1}, one Hermitian 16 x 16 bond, the same on every bond, with h = Σ_{J=0}^{3} a_J P_J, where P_J projects the pair onto total spin J. Every such h is real symmetric in the S^z basis, invariant under exchange of the two sites, and commutes with time reversal Θ = exp(iπS^y)K. The symmetries are therefore: on-site SU(2) (spin 3/2, a projective representation of SO(3), so the Lieb–Schultz–Mattis anomaly between translation and SO(3) is present), one-site translation T, site-centred inversion I_s, bond-centred inversion I_b (note T = I_b I_s up to orientation), and time reversal Θ. When a_1 = a_3 the bond is also invariant under Sp(4) ≅ Spin(5), with the 4 of Sp(4) restricting to spin 3/2 under the principal SU(2), and 4 ⊗ 4 = 1 ⊕ 5 ⊕ 10 corresponding to J = 0, J = 2 and J = 1 ⊕ 3. At a_0 = a_2, a_1 = a_3 the symmetry is SU(4) and the chain is the SU(4) Uimin–Lai–Sutherland model, whose infrared theory is SU(4)_1 with T acting as the Z_4 centre generator (primaries in the 4 and 4bar appear at momentum ±π/2, those in the 6 at π).

SU(4)_1 = Spin(6)_1 is the bosonization (sum over spin structures, diagonal GSO) of six free Majorana fermions ψ^A, A = 1..6. Under Sp(4) = Spin(5) ⊂ Spin(6), the six split as 5 + 1: five Majoranas ψ^a (a = 1..5, the 5 of Sp(4), which is spin 2 under the principal SU(2)) and one singlet Majorana ψ^6. In the Sp(4)-symmetric chain there is an extended phase with c = 3 described as Sp(4)_1 × Ising = (5 Majoranas) × (1 Majorana) glued back together, with the singlet Majorana's mass term iψ^6_L ψ^6_R forbidden because T acts on it with eigenvalue −1 (it sits at momentum π relative to the ground state).

## The target fixed point

Replace the Ising sector by tricritical Ising (TCI, M(4,5), c = 7/10): the glued theory is the extension of Sp(4)_1 × M(4,5) by the integer-spin simple current (v, ε''), where v is the vector primary (h = 1/2) of Sp(4)_1 and ε'' is the h = 3/2 primary of TCI. Equivalently, it is the bosonization of (five free Majoranas) ⊗ (the fermionic TCI, i.e. the N = 1 superconformal minimal model with c = 7/10) with the diagonal fermion parity. Its central charge is 5/2 + 7/10 = 16/5. TCI primaries: 1 (0), ε (1/10), ε' (3/5), ε'' (3/2), σ (3/80), σ' (7/16). Sp(4)_1 primaries: 1 (0), v (1/2, the 5), s (5/16, the 4).

The physical idea: deep in the c = 3 phase the singlet Majorana is free; tuning the lattice may make it strongly interacting and drive it through a tricritical point (as in a single Majorana chain with translation, where the tricritical point is reached with one tuning parameter) while the Sp(4)_1 sector stays gapless.

1. Construct the operator content of the glued theory (the extended primaries, their conformal weights, their decomposition into Sp(4)_1 × TCI primaries, their fusion, and how the fixed-point resolution works). Cross-check the total quantum dimension and the modular data where you can.

2. Determine how T acts in the infrared at this point, in the scenario where the point is reached continuously from the c = 3 phase (and before that from SU(4)_1) without breaking T. In the c = 3 phase T acts on the six Majoranas as a chiral fermion parity, with the mod-8 anomaly ν = 6 of (−1)^{F_L} for six Majoranas. Work out the mod-8 anomaly of each chiral fermion parity of the fermionic TCI (for example from the spins in the corresponding twisted sector), and hence which chiral parity of the TCI factor T must be, given that the singlet sector carries ν = 1 in the c = 3 phase. State the order of T on the local operators of the bosonic glued theory and the momenta (relative to the ground state) at which each class of operators appears. Compare with an alternative assignment in which T acts through the monodromy of a simple current of the extended algebra, and say what would distinguish the two.

3. List every Lorentz-scalar local operator with scaling dimension Δ ≤ 2 in the glued theory, with its Sp(4) representation, its principal-SU(2) spin content, its T eigenvalue, and its parities under I_s, I_b and Θ (fix these by continuity with lattice operators where you can: for instance, staggered Sp(4)-invariant bond operators Σ_j (−1)^j P_J(j, j+1) are Θ-even, I_b-even and I_s-odd). Identify the relevant (Δ < 2) operators invariant under all lattice symmetries, first with Sp(4), then with only SU(2). Also list the marginal (Δ = 2) symmetric ones. The number of relevant symmetric operators is the codimension of the glued point, so say how many lattice parameters must be tuned to reach it, in the Sp(4) family (one free parameter after shift and scale) and in the SU(2) family (two free parameters).

4. Give velocity-independent ratios that diagnose the TCI sector (for example ratios of levels that live entirely in the TCI sector), and contrast with the c = 3 phase Sp(4)_1 × Ising and with SU(4)_1.
