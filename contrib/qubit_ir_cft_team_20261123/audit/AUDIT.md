## Independent covariance and locking derivation

### A conditional amplitude measurement from normal and anomalous pairs

Independently calibrate the candidate *pure* inter-valley annihilator `O=V_l`, remove its physical pi carrier, and take bulk, zero-temperature connected correlations. Let `x=x_O`, `x4=4x`. Choose a fixed length cutoff `a`, a reference velocity `u0` and dimensionless vertices normalized by `<V_l†(r)V_l(0)>=(a/r)^(2x)` at the Gaussian point. Define the dimensionless quartet coupling in the action by

```
delta S = (g4/2) integral dx d(u0 tau)/a² [V_(2l)+V_(-2l)].
```

The normal/anomalous ratio `R(r)=<O(r) O(0)>_c/<O†(r) O(0)>_c` vanishes at the Gaussian point by its charge selection rule. To first order the only charge-neutral numerator comes from the `V_(-2l)` insertion. For equal speeds set `u0=v` and use `z=(x_space+i v tau)/a`, `R0=r/a`. The normalized Gaussian three-point function is `R0^(2x) |z|^(-4x) |z-R0|^(-4x)`. Rescaling the integration coordinate and dividing by the normal correlator gives the leading long-distance linear response

```
R(r) = -(g4/2) I(x) (r/a)^(2-x4) + cutoff and higher-order corrections,
I(x) = integral d²w |w|^(-4x) |w-1|^(-4x).
```

The two short-distance singularities are integrable for `x<1/2`, and the large-distance tail is integrable for `x>1/4`. Thus `I(x)` is finite and positive for the target hypothesis `x=.45965`. With two finite nonzero speeds the chiral factors use their own `x_space ± i v_i tau` coordinates. For this symmetric commuting-field vertex the opposite-speed chiral weights pair. The product is homogeneous of the same degree and has the same convergence conditions. The coefficient then depends on both velocities and the decomposition of the vertex among the modes. It must be computed in the same normalization before extracting an absolute coupling.

This test predicts a factor `4^(2−x4)≈1.25` across a fourfold change in radius only in a perturbative scaling window, with independently calibrated quadratures and controlled competing couplings. A microscopic pair `P_d=c O+c' O†+...` already has an anomalous signal at `g4=0` when both coefficients occur. Bulk connected subtraction, field calibration, boundary control and error bars are essential. An undetected signal supplies a sensitivity limit over the available window, not an all-scale vanishing theorem. An independent causal ceiling holds for the microscopic dynamics. The two-site bond has eigenvalues `2,0,-1±3/16` and centering it by `(13/32)I` gives norm `J=51/32`. The onsite field can be removed by local time-dependent rotations, preserving each interaction's support and norm. For disjoint single-site probes X,Y at graph distance R, iterate the local-commutator inequality. Jacobi gives the commutator's homogeneous unitary evolution plus an inhomogeneous sum bounded by twice the norm of X times the norms of commutators of the interactions overlapping its support with Y. The supremum over supported operators applies also to their onsite-rotated values at the time in question. At order n the time-ordered integral is at most `|t|^n/n!`; there are at most `2*3^(n-1)` paths of consecutive overlapping bonds, including self-overlaps, and none for `n<R`. Thus

```
||[X(t),Y]|| <= (4/3)||X|| ||Y|| sum_(n>=R) (6J |t|)^n/n!
             <= (4/3)||X|| ||Y|| exp[-R+6 e J |t|].
```

This bounds locally detectable signal speeds by `153e/16≈25.99` in lattice units. Positivity of a target infrared speed and any equality of speeds require data.
