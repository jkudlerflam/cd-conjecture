# F2. Interacting fermion chains, zero counts, and central charge

Closed-book research note, 22 October 2026. Only the facts supplied in the task, explicit arguments below, and the accompanying small numerical checks are used. No pre-existing files or external sources were consulted. The code is `f2.py`; its output is `f2_checks.json`.

## Result and status

The free determinant zero count has no universal extension as a count of isolated zero-energy singularities or a momentum/Matsubara winding of the physical one-electron Green function. A concrete nearest-neighbor, translation-invariant, spinful-fermion construction below has a charged spectral gap, a momentum-independent electron Green function, and a neutral CFT with $c=1$. A gapped model on the same local space has the same Matsubara zero locus and Green-function homotopy class. Their common value $G(k,0)=0$ for every $k$ also rules out an isolated-zero formula applying to all the chains in the question.

Four positive statements survive.

1. If a lattice model has a faithful continuum RG description with a quasi-free ultraviolet CFT, and the hypotheses of the relativistic $c$-theorem hold, then $c_{\rm IR}\leq c_{\rm free}\leq\log_2 d$. This is a conditional theorem. No open ball in microscopic bond norm is proved to satisfy its matching hypotheses.
2. A regular fermionic TBA with complete quasiparticle content and $m$ occupied intervals has the thermal contribution of $m$ compact bosons, with dressed velocities. Under the stated branch-completeness assumption, $c=m$. For $r$ nesting species with at most one interval each and no massless auxiliary sector, $c\leq r$. Taking $r=d-1$ gives the desired bound in this restricted class. Neither the one-interval hypothesis nor completeness follows from nesting rank alone.
3. If the full IR theory is Gaussian and its modes are resolved by commuting onsite conserved charges, then the rank of their zero-temperature susceptibility equals $c$ and is at most $d-1$. This is a density-response replacement with a sharp onsite bound, under an explicit completeness hypothesis.
4. A full WZW infrared theory satisfying the representation-index bound given in the question obeys $c\leq d-1$. An affine subalgebra alone does not control a commuting neutral CFT.

Thus the general missing step is a bound on all neutral stress-tensor degrees of freedom from the microscopic nearest-neighbor tensor structure. Charge winding and Luttinger constraints do not supply it. The note proves a no-go result for these counts, not the general $c$-$d$ conjecture.

Conventions: $c=c_L=c_R$ for a nonchiral CFT, and $c_T$ is the sum over sectors. A complex nonchiral massless fermion has $c=1$, and a real one has $c=1/2$. A sector of velocity $v$ contributes

\[
 f(T)-f(0)=-\frac{\pi c}{6v}T^2+o(T^2).
\]

The words “given”, “proved”, “hypothesis”, and “checked” distinguish the logical status of the claims. Standard continuum frameworks used conditionally below include bosonization and the relativistic $c$-theorem. Specific uncomputed Green functions are not assigned invented zero counts.

## 1. What a Green-function count can and cannot measure

### 1.1 The free argument loses both its degree bound and its counting identity

In the free theorem supplied in the task, two separate statements hold:

\[
 c=\frac14 N_{|z|=1}(\det h),\qquad
 N_{|z|=1}(\det h)\leq 2\,\operatorname{rank}A(1).
\]

The first identifies physical fermion zeros with all stress-tensor modes. The second uses finite Laurent degree. Interactions generally destroy both statements, not just the second.

A Dyson convention such as

\[
 G^{-1}(k,i\omega)=i\omega-h$k$-\Sigma(k,i\omega)
\]

has no finite Laurent-degree consequence from the range of the bare Hamiltonian. Eliminating intermediate many-body states can propagate over arbitrarily many bonds. A gap can imply decay of suitable kernels without making them finite range. At criticality even that decay need not be exponential. A complex-$z$ continuation may have branch cuts, and its existence and annulus of analyticity need separate hypotheses. The argument principle is therefore not automatically available.

Even if a particular $G$ happens to be meromorphic, counting its poles minus zeros counts a property of a charged two-point function. It does not identify the coefficient of the stress-tensor two-point function. The latter is the central charge.

For an isolated singular point in the real $(k,\omega)$ plane one can define, provided the matrix is nonsingular along the loop,

\[
 \nu=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint d\log\det G^{-1}.
\]

This needs no holomorphic extension in $z$. In a free complex-fermion theory it gives a signed chirality count. Left and right points have opposite signs, so the total signed count on the Brillouin zone is not $2c$. Half the unsigned count, including flavor multiplicity, gives $c$ in the free complex convention. A Majorana or Nambu description has a different redundancy factor, fixed by the free theorem in the question. Absolute winding is not generally protected against collisions and pair creation, and its identification with $c$ needs an independent completeness statement.

There is a stronger elementary restriction on a winding in momentum alone. The Lehmann representation of a canonical fermion Green matrix has a positive matrix spectral measure,

\[
 G(k,i\omega)=\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{A(k,dE)}{i\omega-E},
 \qquad A\geq0,\qquad \int A=I.
\]

For $\omega>0$,

\[
 \operatorname{Im}G(k,i\omega)
 =-\omega\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{A(k,dE)}{\omega^2+E^2}<0
\]

as a matrix quadratic form. Every normalized vector has strictly positive integrated spectral weight, which proves strict definiteness and hence invertibility. The cone of matrices with negative-definite imaginary part is convex. Interpolating the loop $k\mapsto G(k,i\omega)$ to the constant $-iI/\omega$ stays in that cone. Its determinant winding around zero is therefore zero. The same holds for $G^{-1}$ and for $\omega<0$ with the opposite sign. This statement assumes continuity in $k$ on the loop. Thus a determinant winding on a fixed nonzero Matsubara-frequency slice cannot count channels, even before considering interactions. Nontrivial Fermi-point windings require loops that probe the zero-frequency singular set. At zero frequency a finite, nonsingular Green matrix in a charged spectral gap is Hermitian, so its determinant also has no ordinary complex phase winding along momentum.

### 1.2 A restricted Luttinger-liquid identity

Hypothesis LL: the low-energy theory consists of $r$ compact Gaussian bosons; the physical electron fields faithfully identify the corresponding pairs of chiral branches; their leading singularities remain isolated and have unit winding; there is no further neutral CFT.

Under LL, $c=r$, and a count of those $2r$ chiral branches gives $2c$. This follows from canonical diagonalization of the Gaussian action, not from the zero-frequency determinant. Spin-charge separation is compatible with this statement: two spin-resolved pairs can reorganize into two bosons without changing their total central charge.

The assumptions are substantial. For example, the continuum expansion of a spinless electron can allow harmonics

\[
 \psi(x)\sim\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z} A_m
 e^{i(2m+1)k_Fx}\,e^{-i(2m+1)\phi(x)+i\theta(x)},
 \qquad
 \Delta_m=\frac{(2m+1)^2K+K^{-1}}4.
\]

This formula assumes the usual compact-boson identification of the electron. It lists allowed operators; it does not prove that every $A_m$ is nonzero in a particular microscopic chain. If many are nonzero, one boson gives many momentum nonanalyticities. The higher ones can occur only in high derivatives. At an irrational filling their allowed momenta are distinct modulo $2\pi$. Counting every singular harmonic cannot count independent CFT sectors.

For the leading spinless harmonic,

\[
 \alpha=2\Delta_0-1=\frac{K+K^{-1}-2}{2}.
\]

The nonanalytic part of $n$k$$ near a Fermi point scales as $\operatorname{sgn}(q)|q|^\alpha$, subject to the usual logarithmic exceptions, and the singular part of $G(q,0)$ scales as $|q|^{\alpha-1}$ with a chirality-dependent phase/sign. Analytic background terms can dominate when the exponent is large. Thus even the nature of a “Fermi point” depends on which singularity is meant. The central charge remains one throughout the Gaussian theory.

These observations identify failures of simple counts. They do not exclude recovering $c$ from sufficiently detailed dynamical data by a non-topological reconstruction.

### 1.3 A proved nearest-neighbor counterexample to electron winding as a complete count

Theorem 1. There are translation-invariant, parity-even, charge-conserving, nearest-neighbor Hamiltonians on a spinful fermion site ($d=4$) with the following properties:

* every charged excitation has a positive gap;
* $G_{\sigma\sigma'}(k,z)=\delta_{\sigma\sigma'}g(z)$, independent of $k$;
* $g(0)=0$, and $g(i\omega)/( -i\omega)>0$ for real nonzero $\omega$;
* one model has a neutral $c=1$ CFT, while another is gapped with $c=0$.

Consequently, neither a momentum winding nor a homotopy invariant of these Matsubara Green matrices within the causal class, with their common zero sheet retained, can equal the central charge for all such chains. An isolated-point winding formula is undefined on these examples. This statement concerns the Matsubara domain, not an invariant required to preserve every real-frequency threshold or branch cut.

Proof. Let

\[
 N_i=n_{i\uparrow}+n_{i\downarrow},\quad
 Q_i=(N_i-1)^2,\quad
 S_i^a=\frac12c_i^\dagger\sigma^a c_i.
\]

The spin operators vanish on the empty and doubly occupied states. Consider

\[
 H=U\sum_i Q_i+\sum_i b_{i,i+1},\qquad
 b_{i,i+1}=J_{xy}(S_i^xS_{i+1}^x+S_i^yS_{i+1}^y)
             +J_zS_i^zS_{i+1}^z.
 \tag{1}
\]

An onsite term is included in the required bond form by putting $U(Q_i+Q_{i+1})/2$ on each bond. All interactions are even fermion operators and have support on at most two sites. In particular, the XY term is quartic in physical electrons. There is no intersite one-electron hopping.

First prove that the low-energy Hilbert space is exactly the singly occupied space. Write $B=\|b\|$. A sector with $m$ nonsingly occupied sites has energy $mU$ plus a spin Hamiltonian with at most $2m$ deleted bonds. Extend any normalized state of its remaining spins by arbitrary spin states on the deleted sites. Its energy in the full singly occupied spin Hamiltonian is at most its old spin energy plus $2mB$. If $E_0^{\rm spin}$ is the full spin-chain ground energy, this gives

\[
 E_D^{\rm spin}\geq E_0^{\rm spin}-2mB,\qquad
 E_D\geq E_0^{\rm spin}+m(U-2B).
 \tag{2}
\]

For $U>2B$, the ground state is singly occupied, and every sector reached by one physical electron costs at least $\Delta_{\rm ch}=U-2B>0$. This bound does not assume a spin gap.

Now set $J_{xy}=J>0,J_z=0$. Within the low-energy space, (1) is the spin-1/2 XX chain. A Jordan-Wigner transformation of these spins gives a nearest-neighbor complex fermion with dispersion $J\cos k$, two linear Fermi points, and $c=1$ by the supplied free theorem. These Jordan-Wigner fermions carry neutral spin information. They are not the physical electrons $c_{i\sigma}$.

Instead set $J_{xy}=0,J_z>0$. The spin Hamiltonian is the antiferromagnetic Ising chain with no transverse field. It has two Néel ground states and a nonzero domain-wall gap. Its central charge is zero. For its Green function choose the translation- and spin-flip-invariant finite-volume ground-state superposition, or the corresponding invariant mixture. The finite vacuum degeneracy is not a CFT contribution. Translation invariance of the Hamiltonian, rather than a unique symmetric vacuum, is the hypothesis of the question.

For both models, $[H,N_i]=0$ for every $i$. In a singly occupied ground state, an electron inserted at $j$ changes the conserved $N_j$. Removing an electron at $i\ne j$ cannot restore the original set of local occupations. Therefore

\[
 G_{ij,\sigma\sigma'}(z)=0\quad(i\ne j).
 \tag{3}
\]

Spin $U(1)$ invariance makes the onsite Green matrix diagonal, and spin flip makes its two diagonal entries equal. The onsite particle-hole rotation generated by

\[
 \eta^x_i=\frac12(c^\dagger_{i\uparrow}c^\dagger_{i\downarrow}
                       +c_{i\downarrow}c_{i\uparrow})
\]

exchanges empty and doubly occupied states, commutes with every $S_i^a$, and preserves $Q_i$. It maps an electron of one spin into a hole of the other spin up to phase. Combined with spin flip, it equates the addition and removal spectral measures of a fixed spin. Hence the Lehmann representation is

\[
 g(z)=\int_{\Delta_{\rm ch}}^\infty d\mu(E)
       \left(\frac1{z-E}+\frac1{z+E}\right),
 \quad d\mu\geq0,\quad \int d\mu=\frac12.
 \tag{4}
\]

This proves

\[
 g(0)=0,\qquad
 g(i\omega)=-2i\omega\int\frac{d\mu(E)}{\omega^2+E^2}.
 \tag{5}
\]

The integral is strictly positive. In particular the zero at frequency zero is simple in the frequency direction and extends over all momentum. The momentum distribution is $n_\sigma$k$=1/2$ for all $k$, since all offsite equal-time electron correlators vanish.

Finally, the convex interpolation of the two positive, normalized, gapped spectral measures remains positive, normalized, and gapped, with a common lower gap chosen below both gaps. It gives a continuous homotopy of Green functions with no poles or zeros on the real Matsubara-frequency axis except their common $\omega=0$ zero sheet. The asymptotic normalization $g(z)\sim1/z$ is unchanged. After dividing out the simple frequency zero, the remaining function on the Matsubara axis is positive up to a fixed sign and is topologically trivial. These Green functions cannot be distinguished by an invariant of this homotopy class. Their central charges differ. This proves the theorem.

The interpolation in the last paragraph is a homotopy in the space of causal single-particle Green functions. It is not claimed to give a realization of every interpolating measure by a nearest-neighbor Hamiltonian. The two endpoints themselves do have the required microscopic realization. A putative invariant requiring additional microscopic information would no longer be an invariant of $G$ alone in this class.

Checks. For the XX bond, $B=|J|/2$, so (2) gives $\Delta_{\rm ch}\geq U-|J|$. For the Ising bond, $B=|J_z|/4$, giving $\Delta_{\rm ch}\geq U-|J_z|/2$. The code checks the onsite Lehmann cancellation for a six-site ring without fitting any central charge. The theorem determines $c$ analytically. Neither model violates $c\leq d-1$. They show why bare hopping rank and charged topology are the wrong invariants for general interactions.

### 1.4 Luttinger and flux-insertion constraints are not upper bounds

Suppose an integer-valued onsite charge $q_j$ is conserved, with filling $\nu=Q/L$. The large gauge transformation

\[
 \mathcal U=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{L}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}j q_j\right)
\]

has, with one translation convention,

\[
 T\mathcal U T^{-1}=\mathcal U e^{-2\pi iQ/L}.
 \tag{6}
\]

The boundary factor is one because the onsite charge is integral. Thus flux insertion or a variational twist changes momentum by $2\pi\nu$ modulo $2\pi$, with the sign fixed by orientation. For a bounded NN Hamiltonian the twist changes each bond by an angle of order $1/L$. If the ground-state current term vanishes, the total variational energy change is $O(L/L^2)=O(1/L)$. This last conclusion requires the current condition, or the usual corresponding control in a flux-insertion argument. Adiabatic spectral flow through a gapless spectrum is not automatic.

Equation (6) fixes a momentum mismatch, not a number of stress-tensor sectors. It forces gaplessness or ground-state degeneracy when the mismatch is nontrivial under the appropriate hypotheses. Adding a neutral sector leaves it unchanged. Multiple Fermi seas can redistribute the same total filling.

At half-filled spinful Hubbard, $\nu=1$, so total-charge flux gives no nontrivial momentum. Separate spin-species twists, when those charges are conserved, use $\nu_\uparrow=\nu_\downarrow=1/2$ and give momentum $\pi$. The neutral spin sector can satisfy this constraint when charge is gapped. The same species fillings occur in the SU(4)-symmetric state on the four spinful onsite states. These filling data are compatible with $c=1$ and $c=3$.

A “Luttinger volume” defined from the sign of $G(k,0)$ has additional hypotheses and is not assumed to obey a universal theorem in a Mott insulator. Even granting the usual filling count, its value is not a central-charge count.

For the usual half-filled Hubbard Hamiltonian in a particle-hole-symmetric convention, the transformation $c_{j\sigma}\mapsto(-1)^j c_{j\sigma}^\dagger$ gives

\[
 G_\sigma(k,z)=-G_\sigma(\pi-k,-z).
\]

In a symmetry-invariant state with a one-electron gap, $G_\sigma(k,0)$ is finite. At $k=\pi/2,3\pi/2$ it is therefore zero. Both spin species have these zeros. Their locations survive the loss of the charge boson, while the supplied central charge is $c=1$, not 2. This argument proves the forced zeros; it does not prove that there are no additional zeros or determine their isolated-point winding.

## 2. What RG continuity proves

### 2.1 A precise conditional lattice theorem

Fix a quasi-free NN Hamiltonian $H_0$ with only linear crossings. Require a strictly positive lower velocity $v_*$ at its crossings and an energy separation $\Delta_*$ from zero outside chosen disjoint momentum windows. Let its free continuum CFT be $\mathcal C_0$, with $c_0$ given by the supplied zero theorem.

Define a continuum-matching neighborhood as a set of bond perturbations $\delta h$, with $\|\delta h\|<\epsilon$, for which all of the following are established:

RG1. The entire IR stress tensor of the lattice Hamiltonian is reproduced by a unitary local continuum QFT obtained as a relevant/marginal deformation of $\mathcal C_0$. Its ultraviolet limit is $\mathcal C_0$. In particular there are no omitted neutral sectors or additional soft lattice modes.

RG2. Lattice irrelevant operators can be removed without changing the terminal IR CFT or its central charge. A mere negative engineering RG eigenvalue is not a proof of this uniform statement.

RG3. This continuum QFT is relativistic with one velocity after rescaling coordinates, or is a product of decoupled relativistic RG flows, each with its own fixed choice of spacetime units. Interacting sectors with unequal velocities are not covered by appealing to emergent Lorentz invariance only at the endpoint.

RG4. The continuum flow reaches a unitary conformal IR endpoint, or a gapped endpoint counted with $c=0$, and the hypotheses of the continuum $c$-theorem hold along the flow.

Theorem 2. For every perturbation in such a continuum-matching neighborhood,

\[
 c_{\rm IR}\leq c_0
 \leq\frac{\operatorname{rank}A_0(1)}2
 \leq\log_2 d
\]

in the Majorana convention, or $c_{\rm IR}\leq c_0\leq\operatorname{rank}T_{0,1}\leq n_c$ for the charge-conserving free reference theory.

Proof. For a relativistic flow, the (c)-function has

\[
 \frac{dC}{d\log\ell}=-\kappa\,\beta^i\mathcal G_{ij}\beta^j\leq0,
 \qquad \kappa>0,
\]

where unitarity makes the appropriate two-point-function metric positive semidefinite. At the UV and IR fixed points it equals their Virasoro central charges. Integration gives $c_{\rm IR}\leq c_0$. For decoupled velocity sectors, apply the theorem to each and sum. RG1 and RG2 identify this endpoint with the full lattice endpoint. The remaining inequalities are the free theorem supplied in the question. No interacting Green-function identity enters the proof.

This statement is not a proof that the set so defined contains an open microscopic norm ball. A stronger version would assert that there exists $\epsilon(H_0)>0$ for which every perturbation satisfying appropriate symmetry and criticality conditions obeys RG1–RG4. That is a missing weak-coupling matching theorem, not a consequence of the $c$-theorem itself.

In perturbative language, the needed smallness is dimensionless: interaction amplitudes divided by relevant velocities and cutoff scales. A fixed $\|\delta h\|$ says little when $v_*$ tends to zero. Relevant couplings can generate a scale of order $\Lambda g^{1/(2-\Delta)}$, and marginally relevant ones an exponentially small scale. These estimates motivate scale separation but do not prove that all infrared degrees of freedom have been included.

### 2.2 An actual open neighborhood within the Gaussian continuum class

Theorem 3. Consider $r$ canonical compact bosons with a parity-even quadratic Hamiltonian

\[
 H=\frac1{2\pi}\int dx\left[
 (\partial_x\theta)^T V_\theta(\partial_x\theta)
 +(\partial_x\phi)^T V_\phi(\partial_x\phi)\right],
 \tag{7}
\]

where both real symmetric matrices are positive definite and the fields have the standard canonical pairing. Then the theory has $r$ nonchiral Gaussian modes, hence $c_T=r$, for arbitrary unequal mode velocities. The positive-definite domain is open.

Proof. The symmetric matrix $V_\theta^{1/2}V_\phi V_\theta^{1/2}$ is positive definite, so an orthogonal matrix diagonalizes it with positive eigenvalues $v_a^2$. Choose

\[
 \phi=M\widetilde\phi,\quad
 \theta=M^{-T}\widetilde\theta,\quad
 M=V_\theta^{1/2}O\,\operatorname{diag}(v_a^{-1/2}).
\]

The transformation preserves the canonical pairing. Direct substitution gives

\[
 M^T V_\phi M=\operatorname{diag}(v_a),\qquad
 M^{-1}V_\theta M^{-T}=\operatorname{diag}(v_a).
\]

Thus (7) is a sum of $r$ free compact-boson Hamiltonians. The compactification lattice can mix under the real linear transformation; that changes the allowed vertex operators, not the oscillator central charge. Each boson contributes one. If both matrices initially have minimal eigenvalue at least $a>0$, perturbations of operator norm less than $a$ keep them positive. This proves openness within this class.

For the lattice application, “the full low-energy theory is (7)” is still a matching hypothesis. Relevant vertices, marginally relevant backscattering, and emergent neutral sectors have not been ruled out by diagonalizing (7). If allowed perturbations instead give a relativistic RG flow satisfying Theorem 2, they can reduce $c$, but cannot increase it above the free parent.

### 2.3 How far the claim extends

| Change along a family of lattice Hamiltonians | What can be concluded |
|---|---|
| Forward scattering within the positive Gaussian class | $c$ is constant, even with distinct velocities. |
| A faithful relativistic continuum deformation of the same free UV CFT | $c_{\rm IR}\leq c_0$. |
| Half-filled weakly repulsive Hubbard, with the stated continuum description | The charge mode can gap while the spin mode remains, giving the supplied $2\to1$ example. The matching is an input here. |
| Coupled unequal-velocity sectors without a relativistic flow | The ordinary continuum $c$-theorem alone proves no inequality. |
| A new band extremum touches zero, or a Fermi velocity vanishes | The fixed linear-crossing continuum chart fails. A Lifshitz or multicritical description may be needed. |
| A composite or previously massive neutral sector becomes soft | RG1 fails unless that sector was already included in the UV theory. |
| Strong coupling destroys the weak-coupling separation of scales | No comparison with the original free $c_0$ follows. |

A continuous path of microscopic Hamiltonians is not an RG trajectory. Central charge need not be monotone along such a path. If a model with $c>\log_2 d$ is reached from a free chain, at least one of RG1–RG4 must fail somewhere in any attempted description using the same free UV parent. This is a logical obstruction, not a metric lower bound on the distance to the free subspace.

In particular, the supplied SU(4) example cannot be a relativistic RG descendant of a complete two-orbital free UV CFT: $3>2$. It needs a different continuum parent or a failure of the stated RG assumptions. The ULS mechanism reorganizes onsite states into collective modes rather than preserving a complete set of physical electron channels.

For the qutrit ULS example, a comparison with an onsite canonical-fermion Hamiltonian also needs a specified embedding or constrained low-energy subspace: dimension three is not the dimension of a full finite onsite fermion Fock space. The SU(4) realization on two physical orbitals avoids this bookkeeping issue and already gives the obstruction.

“Far from free” has no absolute bond-norm meaning without normalizing the energy scale and imposing $v_*,\Delta_*>0$ uniformly. Rescaling any Hamiltonian makes its bond norm arbitrarily small and brings it close to the zero Hamiltonian, but the zero Hamiltonian has an extensive degeneracy and no linear-crossing CFT. It is outside the theorem. Even after normalization, this note does not prove a universal distance estimate from every free critical chain.

## 3. A regular-sea TBA theorem and its limits

### 3.1 Explicit hypotheses

The following is a conditional analytic theorem about a specified TBA system. Deriving that system and its completeness from a microscopic $R$-matrix is a separate step.

Let $a=1,\ldots,r$ label fermionic rapidity species. Absorb chemical potentials into bare energies $e_a$, and write

\[
 \epsilon=e-K^T F,\qquad
 F_a(\lambda)=T\log(1+e^{-\epsilon_a(\lambda)/T}),
 \qquad f=f_{\rm vac}-\langle a,F\rangle .
 \tag{8}
\]

Here $a_a(\lambda)$ is a bare density of states, not a species index when used in the inner product. The transpose includes integration and species summation. Suppose:

T1. At $T=0$, the occupied sets $I_a=\{\epsilon_{0a}<0\}$ are finite unions of intervals, with a total of $m$ intervals and simple endpoints. Their dressed densities are positive.

T2. The root-density equation is

\[
 \rho^t=a+K\rho,\qquad \rho=\chi\rho^t,
 \tag{9}
\]

where $\chi$ multiplies by the indicator of the occupied sets. The relevant resolvents are bounded, and the low-temperature solutions admit a uniform Sommerfeld expansion through order $T^2$. This excludes a singular dressing operator, a collapsing sea, and an endpoint of zero velocity.

T3. There are no additional massless auxiliary or string sectors omitted from (8). The quasiparticle state counting is the fermionic one encoded in (8), without a further critical degeneracy factor.

T4. For the central-charge conclusion, the low-energy finite-size spectrum is exhausted by these regular density modes, each with dressed momentum derivative $p_a'=2\pi\rho_a^t$. There is no independent neutral CFT. Opposite endpoints form the left and right branches of a nonchiral mode. For the simplest expression below assume their speed magnitudes are equal; the endpoint formula does not require that equality.

The use of T4 is explicit. A single total coefficient of $T^2$, without separately known velocities or spectral completeness, does not determine the sum of central charges of an arbitrary multivelocity CFT.

### 3.2 Derivation of the thermal coefficient

Theorem 4. Under T1–T3,

\[
 f(T)-f(0)=-\frac{\pi T^2}{12}
  \sum_{\text{Fermi endpoints }q}\frac1{|v(q)|}+o(T^2),
 \qquad
 |v(q)|=\frac{|\epsilon_0'(q)|}{2\pi\rho^t(q)}.
 \tag{10}
\]

Under T4 as well, every occupied interval contributes one compact-boson mode and $c_T=m$. In particular, if every species has at most one interval, $c_T\leq r$.

Proof. Put $\epsilon^- =\epsilon\,\mathbf1_{\epsilon<0}$ and $b_T=\pi^2T^2/6$. The fermionic Sommerfeld distribution identity is

\[
 F=-\epsilon^-+b_T\delta(\epsilon_0)+o(T^2),
 \tag{11}
\]

where the shift of $\epsilon$ itself must also be included. To check the coefficient, subtract the zero-temperature expression in an energy coordinate. The even excess is

\[
 2T^2\int_0^\infty du\,\log(1+e^{-u})
 =2T^2\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2}
 =\frac{\pi^2T^2}{6}.
\]

The simple-root hypothesis converts $\delta(\epsilon_0)$ into endpoint delta functions divided by $|\epsilon_0'|$.

Let $\delta\epsilon=\epsilon_T-\epsilon_0$. Because the derivative of $\epsilon^-$ is $\chi$ away from a measure-zero endpoint and the boundary energy is zero, linearizing (8) gives

\[
 (1-K^T\chi)\delta\epsilon
 =-b_TK^T\delta(\epsilon_0)+o(T^2).
 \tag{12}
\]

The corresponding free-energy change is

\[
 \delta f=\langle a,\chi\delta\epsilon\rangle
          -b_T\langle a,\delta(\epsilon_0)\rangle+o(T^2).
 \tag{13}
\]

Equation (9) gives $(1-\chi K)\rho=\chi a$. Using it as the adjoint identity in the first term of (13),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \langle\chi a,\delta\epsilon\rangle
 &=\langle\rho,(1-K^T\chi)\delta\epsilon\rangle\\
 &=-b_T\langle K\rho,\delta(\epsilon_0)\rangle+o(T^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore the bare density in (13) becomes the dressed density:

\[
 \delta f=-b_T\langle a+K\rho,\delta(\epsilon_0)\rangle
 =-\frac{\pi^2T^2}{6}\sum_q\frac{\rho^t(q)}{|\epsilon_0'(q)|}+o(T^2).
\]

This is (10). The dressing correction is necessary; using a bare velocity generally gives the wrong coefficient.

For completeness of the central-charge interpretation under T4, linearization at one endpoint gives the standard chiral density oscillators with energies $2\pi|v|n/L$, $n=1,2,\ldots$. Their oscillator partition function is

\[
 Z_{\rm osc}=\prod_{n\geq1}(1-e^{-2\pi\beta|v|n/L})^{-1}.
\]

Its thermal free-energy density is $-\pi T^2/(12|v|)$, the chiral $c=1$ coefficient. Dressing changes velocities and the allowed charge/winding lattice; it does not change this oscillator count under T3–T4. Pairing the two endpoints gives one nonchiral boson. Summing over $m$ intervals yields $c_T=m$. No further oscillator sector is allowed by T4. This finishes the proof.

The theorem can be extended to endpoints at infinite rapidity by a regular dressed-momentum coordinate with finite nonzero limiting velocity and a controlled expansion. Those conditions must be checked. Zero-field rational spin-chain seas often have such infinite-rapidity endpoints, so a finite-endpoint argument should not silently be applied to them. This note does not prove that limiting analysis for a particular microscopic model.

### 3.3 What this says about $gl(d)$

If a fundamental $gl(d)$-type NN chain has a complete regular description with $d-1$ nested density species, at most one occupied interval per species, and no further massless sector, Theorem 4 proves

\[
 c_T\leq d-1.
 \tag{14}
\]

This is an interacting theorem with explicit assumptions. The supplied ULS result $c=d-1$ is consistent with saturation. Here the detailed nested Bethe equations, their ground-state root pattern, and their infinite-endpoint limit have not been derived. Those model-specific inputs cannot be upgraded to theorems by calling the rapidities “dressed free fermions”.

There are three separate possible failures: more than one occupied interval for a species, massless auxiliary strings, and non-fermionic critical state counting. A rapidity sea is not necessarily a complete physical CFT mode.

The underlying algebraic Bethe ansatz may use polynomial $Q$-functions, but their degrees count extensive root numbers of order $L$. Their degrees are not bounded by the local dimension independently of chain length. Nested equations can also have indefinitely many string species. There is no counterpart here of the fixed-degree one-body determinant from the free proof.

### 3.4 Why bond-algebra rank does not supply (14)

For the fundamental swap,

\[
 P=\sum_{i,j=1}^d E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}.
 \tag{15}
\]

The two sets of $d^2$ matrices are linearly independent. Its operator Schmidt rank is therefore $d^2$, and that of $P-I\otimes I/d$ is $d^2-1$. The ordinary matrix rank of $P$ is also $d^2$. None of these ranks equals $d-1$.

The number $d-1$ is instead the Lie rank of $su(d)$. Identifying this rank with a complete number of massless modes uses fundamental-representation and level-one structure, not a general property of a two-site interaction. An irreducible $su(2)$ representation can generate the full associative algebra $\operatorname{End}(\mathbb C^d)$ while its Lie rank remains one. Neither interpretation repairs a universal rank count by itself.

### 3.5 Strings and the Takhtajan–Babujian test

The supplied spin-1 Takhtajan–Babujian endpoint is SU(2)$_2$, with

\[
 c=\frac{3\cdot2}{2+2}=\frac32.
\]

One nesting level cannot imply one boson and nothing else. More generally, the Cartan current of SU(2)$_k$ has a $U(1)$ Sugawara stress tensor with central charge one. Subtracting it from the WZW stress tensor gives a commuting coset with

\[
 c_{\rm neutral}=\frac{3k}{k+2}-1
               =\frac{2(k-1)}{k+2}.
 \tag{16}
\]

This is the parafermion sector. At $k=2$ its central charge is $1/2$, so the full $3/2$ consists of a boson plus a neutral Ising sector. The subtraction follows from the current algebra and the unitary coset framework; identifying the lattice endpoint as SU(2)$_2$ is the example input. The precise Bethe-string bookkeeping of this lattice Hamiltonian is not derived here.

TBA auxiliary-node contributions are generally dilogarithm weights, not one unit per node. As a useful finite check, consider the positive $A_{k-1}$-type plateau solution

\[
 \vartheta=\frac{\pi}{k+2},\quad
 x_a=\frac{\sin^2\vartheta}{\sin^2((a+1)\vartheta)},\quad
 Y_a=x_a^{-1}-1,\quad a=1,\ldots,k-1.
\]

It obeys $Y_a^2=(1+Y_{a-1})(1+Y_{a+1})$ with $Y_0=Y_k=0$, by the sine product identity. The code checks

\[
 \frac6{\pi^2}\sum_{a=1}^{k-1}\mathcal L(x_a)
 =\frac{2(k-1)}{k+2},\qquad
 \mathcal L(x)=\operatorname{Li}_2(x)+\frac12\log x\log(1-x),
 \tag{17}
\]

for $2\leq k\leq12$. Equation (17) for general $k$ is not proved here and is not used in Theorem 4 or the bound. Nor is this plateau system asserted to be the full finite-temperature TBA of every lattice SU(2) chain. It illustrates the noninteger weights in a proposed parafermion sector.

For $k=2$, there is one auxiliary value $x_1=1/2$. This case is analytic: differentiating the dilogarithm reflection formula gives a constant, fixing it at an endpoint gives $\mathcal L(x)+\mathcal L(1-x)=\pi^2/6$, and hence $\mathcal L(1/2)=\pi^2/12$. The single weight is $1/2$, as required by (16). Counting auxiliary nodes with weight one would already fail in this smallest case.

| Bethe/CFT example | Naive count | Full central charge | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fundamental SU($d$) ULS | $d-1$ nesting levels | $d-1$, given | Complete regular level-one description is consistent; root-pattern and endpoint control are still inputs to the TBA proof. |
| Spinful Hubbard with both sectors gapless | Two density modes | 2, given | Regular charge and spin sectors must both be included. |
| Half-filled Mott Hubbard | Still an algebra with charge and spin labels | 1, given | The charge sector is massive; nesting labels are not a count of massless modes. |
| Spin-1 Takhtajan–Babujian | One nesting level | $3/2$, given | One Cartan boson plus a neutral $c=1/2$ coset. T3–T4 fail if only the boson is retained. |
| A regular TBA with one species and two occupied intervals | One species | 2 under T1–T4 | It is intervals, not species, that count in Theorem 4. No microscopic NN realization is claimed by this row. |

## 4. Bounds from onsite symmetries

### 4.1 The representation-index bound

Theorem 5. Suppose the full infrared CFT is a unitary WZW theory $G_k$ for a compact simple group, with onsite representation $R$ on $\mathbb C^d$, and $k\leq I=\operatorname{ind}R$ in the convention of the supplied conformal embedding. Then

\[
 c(G_k)\leq d-1.
\]

For a semisimple current algebra with levels $k_i\leq I_i$, the same result holds provided the complete product embeds at levels $I_i$ in SU($d$)$_1$, and the full IR theory is the stated current-algebra theory, without extra stress-tensor sectors.

Proof. For a simple factor,

\[
 \frac{d}{dk}\frac{k\dim G}{k+h^\vee}
 =\frac{h^\vee\dim G}{(k+h^\vee)^2}>0.
\]

Thus $c(G_k)\leq c(G_I)$. The supplied unitary embedding $G_I\subset\mathrm{SU}(d)_1$ has a nonnegative coset central charge,

\[
 (d-1)-c(G_I)\geq0.
\]

Combining the two inequalities proves the result. For a product, sum the monotonicity inequalities and use nonnegativity of the single complementary coset in SU($d$)$_1$.

The full-theory hypothesis is necessary. In a theory containing $G_k$ with a commuting sector,

\[
 c_{\rm full}=c(G_k)+c_{\rm coset},\qquad c_{\rm coset}\geq0,
\]

a bound on $k$ alone gives no upper bound on $c_{\rm coset}$. An abelian current similarly contributes one regardless of its normalization, but does not preclude a large commuting CFT. This is the same neutral-sector problem seen in the Green-function construction.

Examples. SU(4) fundamental has $I=1$, so SU(4)$_1$ saturates $c=3=d-1$. For spin 1, the supplied index is $I=4$; the TB level $k=2$ obeys $c=3/2\leq c(\mathrm{SU}(2)_4)=2=d-1$. If the entire CFT is pure SU(2)$_k$ on spin $S\geq3/2$, then $c=3k/(k+2)<3\leq2S=d-1$ without any level bound. None of these observations bounds an unaccounted-for neutral factor.

### 4.2 A susceptibility-rank theorem for symmetry-complete Gaussian CFTs

This gives a quantity bounded by $d-1$ that equals $c$ in a substantial interacting class. Unlike electron winding, it measures independent conserved density modes. It is not claimed to be a topological invariant under arbitrary perturbations.

Theorem 6. Let a chain with local space $\mathbb C^d$ have mutually commuting Hermitian onsite charge matrices $q_\alpha$, with conserved global charges $Q_\alpha=\sum_j q_{\alpha,j}$. Assume:

S1. Its entire infrared CFT consists of $r$ compact bosons with a positive Gaussian Hamiltonian of the form (7), with no further sector of nonzero central charge.

S2. After a canonical choice of density coordinates, the uniform charge deviations are $\delta Q_\alpha/L=\sum_{a=1}^r B_{\alpha a}\rho_a$, where $\rho_a=-\partial_x\phi_a/\pi$ and the matrix $B$ has column rank $r$. Thus these onsite charges resolve every boson. This is not automatic for a liquid with several pockets or emergent neutral modes.

S3. The zero-temperature thermodynamic energy is twice differentiable in small chemical potentials within this phase, and the Gaussian zero-mode energy gives its quadratic response. Gapped charge directions remain in their plateaus. The thermodynamic limit is taken before differentiating the density with respect to chemical potential.

Then, for the uniform susceptibility matrix

\[
 \chi_{\alpha\beta}
 =-\left.\frac{\partial^2 e(\mu)}{\partial\mu_\alpha\partial\mu_\beta}
 \right|_{\mu=0},\qquad
 e(\mu)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac1L\min\operatorname{spec}
 \left(H-\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha Q_\alpha\right),
\]

one has

\[
 c_T=r=\operatorname{rank}\chi\leq d-1.
 \tag{18}
\]

Proof. Simultaneously diagonalize the $q_\alpha$. Modulo the identity they belong to a real vector space of dimension $d-1$. Any linear combination proportional to the identity shifts $e(\mu)$ only linearly, and hence gives a null vector of $\chi$. Consequently, even before using the continuum theory,

\[
 \operatorname{rank}\chi\leq
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_\alpha\bmod I\}\leq d-1.
 \tag{19}
\]

For the Gaussian uniform modes, setting the equilibrium current to zero in (7) gives the energy density

\[
 e(\rho,\mu)=e_0+\frac\pi2\rho^T V_\phi\rho
                         -\mu^T B\rho
                         -\mu^T\bar q.
\]

Here $\bar q$ is the background density, which does not affect the Hessian. Minimizing over the continuous thermodynamic densities gives

\[
 \rho_* =\frac1\pi V_\phi^{-1}B^T\mu,\qquad
 \chi=\frac1\pi B V_\phi^{-1}B^T.
 \tag{20}
\]

Positive definiteness implies $\operatorname{rank}\chi=\operatorname{rank}B=r$. Theorem 3 gives $c_T=r$, since S1 excludes additional stress tensors. Combining with (19) proves (18). Different velocities and Luttinger parameters change the nonzero eigenvalues of $\chi$, not its rank.

This proof does not use nearest-neighbor locality; its strong symmetry and completeness assumptions do the work. In particular it must not be promoted to a theorem for arbitrary Gaussian CFTs on $d$ states. A single onsite charge can act on several gapless seas, in which case $\operatorname{rank}B<r$. For example, a spinless fermion chain with longer-range hopping can have two seas but only one independent traceless diagonal onsite charge. This illustrates the missing S2 rather than violating (18).

It is essential to restrict to commuting onsite charges. The Hessian in fields coupled to all noncommuting SU($d$) generators can have rank $d^2-1$, so that rank would not obey (19). Non-onsite emergent symmetries also do not obey the onsite dimension argument.

| Endpoint | Commuting onsite charges to use | Active susceptibility rank in the stated IR description | Completeness and result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spinful LL with gapless charge and spin | $N,\ S^z$ | 2 | Two Gaussian modes, $c=2$. |
| Half-filled Mott Hubbard | $N,\ S^z$ | 1, since the charge direction is in a plateau | One Gaussian spin mode, $c=1$. The standard LL identification is assumed. |
| SU(4)$_1$ ULS on a spinful site | $n_\uparrow,\ n_\downarrow,\ n_\uparrow n_\downarrow$ modulo identity | 3 | These span its Cartan algebra; $c=3$. |
| Fundamental SU($d$)$_1$ ULS | $d-1$ independent traceless diagonal projectors | $d-1$ | Cartan currents exhaust the stress tensor at level one, so the bound is saturated. |
| Projected XX model (1) | $N,\ S^z$ | 1 | The neutral spin mode is visible to a conserved density, though invisible to physical electron propagation. |
| Spin-1 TB, SU(2)$_2$ | $S^z$ | 1 | The neutral $c=1/2$ coset violates S1 as a complete Gaussian description. Susceptibility rank is not the full $3/2$. |
| A parity-only Majorana theory | No nontrivial continuous onsite charge need exist | Can be 0 | A Majorana stress tensor is not a Gaussian charge mode. No equality is claimed. |

For the level-one SU($d$) rows, the Cartan current algebra has $d-1$ free-boson stress tensors. Its central charge equals that of SU($d$)$_1$, supplied as $d-1$. The difference is a unitary coset of central charge zero, so it carries no additional stress-tensor contribution. This justifies the completeness statement at the level of central charge without computing a physical electron Green function.

Equation (18) is the strongest $d-1$ bound obtained here directly from an onsite linear-algebra count. It does not reduce universally to the free hopping-rank bound. In a charge-complete complex-fermion liquid it equals the number of active complex channels and is therefore bounded by the free rank in the free NN case. A Majorana CFT can have fractional $c$ and no charge susceptibility, and even a multiband free chain need not have enough independent onsite conserved charges. Thus no universal interacting invariant with the requested free-rank reduction has been proved.

## 5. Ranked missing lemmas

The ranking gives priority to statements that would make a concrete route work. It distinguishes a feasible restricted target from a genuinely general missing input.

| Rank | Missing statement | What it would establish | Status and obstruction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Uniform weak-coupling continuum matching around a fixed linear-crossing free chain, with $v_*,\Delta_*>0$, control of irrelevant terms, and a complete stress tensor | An actual microscopic neighborhood for Theorem 2, subject to its velocity/Lorentz conditions | Not proved here. Perturbative power counting does not by itself exclude very-low-energy neutral sectors or dangerously irrelevant changes of endpoint. This is the most direct restricted extension of the free theorem. |
| 2 | A multivelocity monotonicity theorem for coupled unitary continuum flows, or a theorem establishing the required Lorentz structure throughout those flows | Removes RG3's major restriction without equating a Hamiltonian path with an RG trajectory | Not supplied by the ordinary relativistic $c$-theorem. The Gaussian case is proved separately in Theorem 3. |
| 3 | Fundamental-$gl(d)$ Bethe completeness plus a bound on the complete massless state counting, including infinite-rapidity limits and auxiliary strings | A broad integrable $c\leq d-1$ theorem using Theorem 4 or a full TBA dilogarithm budget | A node count is insufficient. One needs both the ground-state root pattern and a bound on all weighted neutral contributions. This does not yet apply to nonintegrable chains. |
| 4 | For WZW endpoints, the representation-index level bound together with control of the entire complementary stress tensor | Extends Theorem 5 beyond pure WZW theories | An affine symmetry subalgebra gives no upper bound on a neutral coset. A complete embedding or equivalent extra inequality is needed. |
| 5 | A general locality-to-neutral-sector budget: derive from a fixed two-site operator on $\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d$ an inequality on all emergent stress-tensor sectors whose total is at most $d-1$ | The full conjecture | Theorem 6 handles modes resolved by commuting onsite charges. Extra Gaussian modes and non-Gaussian neutral sectors remain uncontrolled. Merely assuming a UV parent of central charge $\leq d-1$ relocates the conjecture unless that parent is constructed from the bond Hamiltonian. |

There is also a candidate lemma that is false and should be removed from the program: “all gapless stress-tensor sectors are detected by isolated singularities of the physical one-electron Green function.” Theorem 1 disproves it. Enlarging to composite correlators can reveal the missing modes, but there are $d^2-1$ traceless onsite operators and many continuum harmonics; their number is not $d-1$, and they are not independent CFT sectors. The necessary reduction is the unknown part.

For an affine decomposition, a concrete target for the final budget would be

\[
 \sum_i\frac{k_i\dim G_i}{k_i+h_i^\vee}+c_{\rm neutral}\leq d-1.
\]

Theorem 5 controls the first sum in certain representations. The general problem is to connect the remaining term, or a theory with no useful affine decomposition at all, to the microscopic NN tensor product. No Green-function winding or nesting-rank argument in this note establishes that connection.

## 6. Checks and reproducibility

`python3 f2.py` needs numpy and scipy and reads no input files. It writes `f2_checks.json`. The checks are deliberately small local computations, not a large computation requiring a cluster.

1. Six-site projected XX and Ising models. The script diagonalizes the spin sector and the one-vacancy sector, constructs the physical electron addition/removal Lehmann measures, checks their normalization, and verifies $G(0)=0$ and odd Matsubara dependence. Momentum independence is proved by local charge conservation, not inferred from this finite-size calculation. The largest computed $|G(0)|$ was $6.94\times10^{-17}$.
2. A scalar nonzero-kernel TBA consistency model. With $K(\lambda,\mu)=0.35/(2\pi)$, bare density $1/(2\pi)$, and bare energy $-\cos\lambda+0.2$, the dressing equations reduce to a scalar self-consistency problem. At the smallest checked temperature, $T=0.005$, the central charge inferred using the derived dressed velocity was $1.000029904$. This checks the adjoint/Sommerfeld calculation. It is not presented as a Bethe solution of a microscopic NN chain.
3. The finite plateau dilogarithm identity (17) and its $Y$-recurrence are checked for $2\leq k\leq12$. These are numerical checks, not a proof of the identity for all $k$ or of any lattice-to-TBA matching.
4. Operator reshuffling verifies the swap Schmidt ranks $d^2$ and $d^2-1$ for $2\leq d\leq5$. The general ranks are proved by (15).
5. The exact thermodynamic XX energy in a field coupled to $S^z$ is

\[
 e(h)=-\frac{\sqrt{J^2-h^2}+h\arcsin(h/J)}{\pi},\qquad |h|<J.
\]

Differentiation gives $\chi_{S^z}=1/(\pi J)$ at zero field. The code checks this with thermodynamic finite differences and obtains rank one for the physical spin-resolved susceptibility matrix, while the total physical charge susceptibility is zero. This is the neutral mode that the electron Green function in Theorem 1 fails to count.

All assertions passed. The final computation reported about 0.011 seconds after imports. No thermodynamic central charge was fitted from small-system spectra. The Hubbard and SU(4) electron Green functions, model-specific Bethe root patterns, and a general interacting $c$-$d$ bound remain uncomputed or unproved as stated above.
