# Review of the transverse-DM modification of the qubit cusp

Date: 2026-11-09. AI-assisted independent review by the route-audit Codex
agent (GPT-6 family). Proposed microscopic perturbation: the parallel
`cusp_scattering` agent. No human review or new counterexample is claimed.

**Verdict: [derived, candidate route].** This is a genuine allowed
nearest-neighbour qubit perturbation. It supplies repulsion, and no
simple on-site gauge equivalence removes it. Its complete leading
effective Hamiltonian also contains a particle-number-changing term.
The latter has important virtual effects, including unequal valley
energy shifts. There is an available microscopic tuning of the valley
offset. The resulting route merits a complete-model calculation;
repulsion-only two-body results do not establish it.

## Exact algebra and second-order operator

Let \(n_j=(1-Z_j)/2\), with a particle corresponding to \(n_j=1\).
On a periodic chain,

\[
 F=\sum_j(n_jX_{j+1}-X_jn_{j+1})
  ={1\over2}\sum_j(X_jZ_{j+1}-Z_jX_{j+1})
  =\sum_j(n_{j-1}-n_{j+1})X_j.                  \tag{1}
\]

The one-site terms in the first expansion telescope. Thus \(\lambda F\)
is a transverse component of the DM interaction, with no longer-range
microscopic coupling. Take \(H_0=V\sum n_jn_{j+1}\), let \(P\) be its
zero-energy blockaded space, and take the original pairing operator to
have matrix element \(+\Delta\) for \(00\leftrightarrow11\).
Then \(PFP=0\).

The complete additional second-order terms are

\[
 H_{\lambda^2}={\lambda^2\over V}P\sum_j
 \left[b^\dagger_{j+1}b_j+\text{h.c.}-2n_j+2n_jn_{j+2}\right]P,
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

\[
 H_{\Delta\lambda}=-{\Delta\lambda\over V}P\sum_j
 (n_{j-2}-n_{j+2})X_jP.                         \tag{3}
\]

These identities were independently enumerated with exact fractions on
all blockaded periodic states at L=7,8,9,10: 275 basis states and 1771
nonzero checked entries. See `check_facilitated.py` and
`facilitated_checks.json`. They agree with the separately derived
[complete reduction](../cusp_validation_20261109/FACILITATED_REPAIR.md).

For (2), an empty site between two particles has zero facilitated flip
amplitude. Summing squared amplitudes gives
\(2N-2\sum n_jn_{j+2}\); the second-order minus sign gives its
diagonal part. A virtual path \(10\to11\to01\) has opposite signs on
its two flips, hence positive hopping. For (3), the two adjacent-pair
creation paths ending in a single newly created particle cancel unless
there is another particle two sites away; their difference is exactly
the displayed density difference.

Put \(r=\lambda/\Delta\) and measure energies in
\(|t_2|=\Delta^2/(2V)\). After canceling (2)'s real nearest hopping
and retuning the chemical potential with allowed bare couplings, the
additional terms are

\[
 4r^2\sum_j n_jn_{j+2}
 +2r\,P\sum_j(n_{j+2}-n_{j-2})X_jP.             \tag{4}
\]

The second term is not suppressed by another power of \(\Delta/V\).
At the naive repulsion-only binding threshold \(r=u/4\), its
dimensionless coefficient is \(u/2\). It must be retained.

## Gauge and symmetry checks

**[proved, restricted statement]** A uniform on-site spin rotation does
not transform (1) away to the original parity-class family. In vector
notation, the original strong field is along z and the new DM vector
has a nonzero y component. The invariant \(|\mathbf D\times\mathbf B|\)
is nonzero for \(\lambda B_z\ne0\), whereas it vanishes in the original
parity family. A site-dependent on-site rotation that leaves the field
uniform can differ between sites only by rotations about its axis;
those rotate the transverse DM components but cannot remove their
nonzero norm. This excludes that restricted on-site gauge route, not
every possible nonlocal duality.

The original parity \(\mathcal P=\prod_jZ_j\) maps
\(\lambda\mapsto-\lambda\). The old antiunitary \(IK\), inversion
times complex conjugation in the Z basis, also changes the sign of
\(\lambda\). Their product \(\mathcal PIK\) remains an exact symmetry.
It preserves momentum and does not exchange the two valleys.

The original valley-exchange operation, inversion followed by staggered
z rotations and a uniform z rotation by \(\pi/2\), is broken: it maps
the uniform transverse-DM term to a staggered term in a different
transverse direction. Consequently neither equal valley energies nor
\(k_a+k_b=\pi\) remain protected. They must not be assumed in a
calculation of the modified chain.

## Low-energy kinematics and a useful exact cancellation

At r=0, let \(k_a=-\arcsin(u/4)\), \(k_b=\pi-k_a\), with
\(0<u<4\). The leading atom-to-two-atom vertices generated by (3)
have bare momentum mismatches among

\[
 k_a,\quad k_b,\quad 2k_a-k_b,\quad2k_b-k_a.       \tag{5}
\]

None vanish at u=1. There is a cubic resonance at \(u=2\sqrt3\),
where \(k_a=-\pi/3\) and \(2k_a-k_b=-2\pi\). Finite-density
harmonics supply additional resonance conditions. Thus generic
translation kinematics can forbid a *resonant* charge-one vertex;
it does not make its finite virtual effects negligible. Those effects
are order r^2, the same order as the desired repulsion.

The number-changing term annihilates the vacuum. A one-particle Bloch
wave of momentum K is mapped to a separation-two pair with amplitude
\(4ir\sin K\) in the centre-coordinate convention. Conversely, a
separation-two pair can decay to one atom with the conjugate amplitude.
Therefore the original dangerous pair at total K=pi has no 2-to-1
matrix element from (3). Its 2-to-3 channels generally survive. This
cancellation is exact and is already incorporated in the parallel pair
self-energy calculation.

## Review of the proposed valley retuning

**[checked algebra; numerical coefficients reproduced for atoms].**
The root agent computes isolated-branch energy shifts
\(\Sigma_a r^2\), \(\Sigma_b r^2\), and \(\Sigma_p r^2\) for the
two atoms and the K=pi pair, at a fixed grand-canonical chemical
potential. An additional effective real nearest hopping \(q r^2\)
gives atom shifts \(2q r^2\cos k_i\). Hence

\[
 q=-{\Sigma_a-\Sigma_b\over4\cos k_a}             \tag{6}
\]

equalizes the two valley minima through order r^2. Their shifts sum
to \(\Sigma_a+\Sigma_b\), because
\(\cos k_b=-\cos k_a\). In the two-body K=pi sector the relative
nearest-hopping coefficient is proportional to \(\cos(K/2)=0\), so
this tuning has no first-order effect on the pair energy. The tuned
binding correction is therefore

\[
 \delta E_{\rm bind}
   =(\Sigma_a+\Sigma_b-\Sigma_p)r^2+O(r^4).       \tag{7}
\]

For \(\mu=-2.27\), u=1, independently rerunning the atom resolvent
at cutoff 512 gives

\[
 \Sigma_a=-0.82522487139594,\quad
 \Sigma_b=-2.66340005345982,\quad
 q=-0.47461479116968.
\]

The two finite-section resolvent gaps are 0.14329 and 0.10554.
The trimer denominator in the pair calculation was independently
checked at relative-gap cutoffs 40 and 80: its smallest eigenvalue is
0.10530 and 0.09511 respectively. These are finite-section checks,
not certified lower bounds on the infinite operator.

Using the parallel agent's pair coefficient about -1.16354, (7) gives
a formal zero near r=0.0916. That is a perturbative target for a
complete-model calculation, **not** an established unbinding threshold.
The branch's isolation and the vacuum stability against larger
clusters remain required. The chemical potential is held fixed in
these coefficients; changing it requires recalculating them.

The valley locations and optimum pair momentum generally shift by
O(r^2). Their effect on minimum energies begins at O(r^4) if the
unperturbed curvatures are positive. Thus they do not invalidate (6)--(7),
but the dressed momentum sum need not equal pi. Losing that equality
also removes the automatic momentum resonance of the old charge-four
vertex. The exact remaining antiunitary symmetry does not restore it.
