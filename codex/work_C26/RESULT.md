# C26. Sidon target and second-order infrared transfer

## 1. Result and scope

[proved] C19's reflection cell accepts the Sidon target without changing its
code, parity, pole count or second-order synthesis theorem. The only change
is the choice of hopping amplitudes, phases and chemical potentials. The
field is still generated by exchange, not inserted as a separate splitting
of the code. The formulas and checks below use C19's phase convention.

[numerical, n=1,2] The canonical fourth-order coefficient has a traceless
two-cell operator norm of \(1.23760530\rho^2\) and \(4.37656212\rho^2\),
respectively. The connected adjacent-bond, three-cell coefficients have
norms \(0.86221866\rho^2\) and \(1.61161974\rho^2\). These are the two types
of connected contributions to the fourth-order chain Hamiltonian. The
calculation includes the canonical normalization term, which contributes
at the same order as the irreducible excursion.

[proved] For this reflection family, the traceless logical effective
Hamiltonian is even in \(\rho\), vanishes at \(\rho=0\), and therefore has
an analytic factor \(\rho^2\) at every fixed perturbative order on every
finite cluster. The proof uses two symmetries, not parity alone. It does
not give a bound uniform in n or a thermodynamic convergence theorem.

[heuristic, conditional on the assumptions in Section 8] With
\(W=\lambda^2\rho^2/\Delta_{\rm src}\), a local massive-sector expansion
that preserves this structure gives a normalized payload correction
starting at \(O(\lambda/\Delta_{\rm src})\), rather than
\(O(\lambda/(\Delta_{\rm src}\rho^2))\). The fourth-order calculation
supports that expansion at n=1,2. The resulting Gaussian infrared
argument remains conditional on a complete spatial core, local
elimination, the dressed momentum margins and a positive stiffness
matrix. No thermodynamic central charge was computed.

[proved, scope of the remaining obstruction] No result here kills route
A. G5 is completed at the abstract-cell level. G6's requested finite
calculation and reformulation are completed, but the local integration
hypothesis remains open as a mathematical statement. These results do
not supply G1/G2's compiler, G3's wrapper composition, G4's many-body
central-charge numerics or G7's gluing data. An implementation that loses
the spectator property at \(\rho=0\) cannot inherit the improved bound
without an additional error estimate.

[proved, convention] Set \(N=2^n\), \(k=n+1\), and measure cell energies
in units of \(\Delta_{\rm src}\) until Section 8. C19 uses

\[
 H_* = \sum_{j,a}\left[-t_a e^{i\theta_a}c^\dagger_{j,a}c_{j+1,a}
       -t_a e^{-i\theta_a}c^\dagger_{j+1,a}c_{j,a}
       +\mu_a(c^\dagger_{j,a}c_{j,a}-1/2)\right],
\]

with dispersion \(e_a(p)=-2t_a\cos(p+\theta_a)+\mu_a\). For desired
roots \(p_{a,L},p_{a,R}\), the correct substitution is

\[
 t_a=1,\qquad k_a=(p_{a,R}-p_{a,L})/2,\qquad
 \theta_a=-(p_{a,R}+p_{a,L})/2,\qquad \mu_a=2\cos k_a.       \tag{1}
\]

[proved] Substituting either root gives \(e_a(p)=0\). Their derivatives
are \(-2\sin k_a\) and \(+2\sin k_a\). Thus the occupied interval is
between the roots and the velocity magnitude is \(2\sin k_a>0\).
The positive center angle in the ir_control script must be negated in
C19's hopping convention. Using it without this sign change would
reflect the Fermi points.

[proved, inherited construction] Number the \(2n\) points by
\(i=0,\ldots,2n-1\). Choose the first prime \(q\ge2n\), and put

\[
 A_i=2qi+(i^2\bmod q),\quad
 d_A=\min_i(A_{i+1}-A_i),\quad B=\lceil d_A/2\rceil,
 \quad T=2A_{2n-1}+4B+2,
\]
\[
 p_i={2\pi\over T}(A_i+B+1/6)+10^{-10}\sqrt{r_{i+1}},
 \qquad p_{a,L}=p_{a-1},\quad p_{a,R}=p_{n+a-1},            \tag{2}

where \(r_j\) is the j-th prime. For sufficiently large n the factor
\(10^{-10}\) is reduced by the margin rule given below. For n at least two the prime is odd

\(\delta_2\ge\pi/(6n)\), \(\delta_4\ge\pi/(576n^2)\) for n at least

any four-charge margin by at most \(4h\), with
\(h=10^{-10}\sqrt{r_{2n}}\). Claims about these scaffold bounds always

## 3. Why C19's exact checks survive

[proved, inherited identities] Let \(X_{ea}\) be C19's Jacobi filters,
\(C_{ef}=1/(e+f)\), and \(p_e=1/k\). Their defining identities are

\[
 \sum_eX_{ea}=0,\qquad X^TCX=I_n.                         \tag{3}
\]

They depend on n and the poles, not on the target momenta. In C19 they
follow by writing the filters as \(x(1-x)P_j^{(2,1)}(2x-1)\), integrating
against the Jacobi weight \(x(1-x)^2\), then setting \(x=e^{-t}\).
No new whitening or spectral fit is used here.

[proved] With local Majoranas \(\gamma_u\), parity \(P_\ell\), and
\(\eta_u=i\gamma_uP_\ell\), define

\[
 J_a=-\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}\sin\theta_a&\cos\theta_a\\
                    -\cos\theta_a&\sin\theta_a\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad F=-\tfrac12\sum_a\mu_a Z_a,
\]
\[
 K_{\rm hop}=\sum_{a,u,v}(J_a)_{uv}\eta_{2a-2+u}\otimes
                                      \gamma_{2a-2+v}.
\]

The rows of each J remain orthogonal with norm \(1/2\). Consequently
C19's cubic identity \(D_e^3=r_e^2D_e\) and its analytic square-root
formula are unchanged. In particular
\((r_e^R)^2=2\sum_aX_{ea}^2\),
\((r_e^L)^2=\sum_aX_{ea}^2/2\).

[proved] In syndrome order X,Y,Z, use

\[
 A_1=(|X\rangle\langle Z|+|Z\rangle\langle X|)/\sqrt2,
 \quad A_2=i(|X\rangle\langle Z|-|Z\rangle\langle X|)/\sqrt2,
\]
\[
 D_e^R=\sum_{a,u}X_{ea}A_u\otimes\eta_{2a-2+u},\qquad
 D_e^L=-\sum_{a,u,v}X_{ea}A_u^T\otimes(J_a)_{uv}\gamma_{2a-2+v}.
\]

Put \(d=(1,-1,0,\ldots)\), \(g=p^TCd=1/[2(k+2)]\). The weights are

\[
 M_e^R=p_eI+\rho D_e^R,\qquad
 M_e^L=p_eI+\rho D_e^L-{\rho^2\over g}d_e|Y\rangle\langle Y|\otimes F,
\]
\[
 0<\rho\le\min\left\{{1\over4kr_{\max}},
                         \sqrt{g\over4k\|F\|}\right\}.   \tag{4}
\]

The X,Z and Y blocks are separate. On X,Z the perturbation norm is
at most \(1/(4k)\), and on Y the field norm is at most \(1/(4k)\).
Thus every weight is at least \(3/(4k)\). Equation (3) and
\(\sum_ed_e=0\) give \(\sum_eM_e^s=I\).
For n=1,2 the first bound in (4) is active, giving
\(\rho^2=1/1536\) and \(1/172800\). The fields satisfy the other bound.

[proved] The code is the two Bell pairs, flag zero, and the entire
N-dimensional logical register. If S collects the six orthogonal
single-port sources and \(S_e\) embeds them at flag e, then

\[
 W_c=\sum_e S_e\sqrt{M_e^L\oplus M_e^R},\quad
 S^\dagger W_c=0,\quad W_c^\dagger W_c=I.
\]

The reflection

\[
 \mathcal R=I-SS^\dagger-W_cW_c^\dagger+SW_c^\dagger+W_cS^\dagger
\]

swaps S and \(W_c\), is the identity on their common orthogonal
complement, and fixes the code G. Hence \(\mathcal R^2=I\). Conjugate
C19's diagonal Hamiltonian, zero on G, e on flag e and k+1 on the
remaining flag-zero states, by \(\mathcal R\). This proves a complete
N-dimensional kernel, unit gap, and source measures
\(S^\dagger\Pi_eS=M_e^L\oplus M_e^R\). The full cell dimension remains
\(16(n+2)2^n\).

[proved] The physical involution
\(Q=Z_{p_L}Z_{s_L}Z_{p_R}Z_{s_R}P_\ell\) is unchanged. Each D is a
product of an odd syndrome operator and an odd logical operator; F is
even. Therefore the weights, their square roots and the cell commute
with Q. The port exchange \(v=\sum_{a=X,Y,Z}F_a^R\otimes F_a^L\)
commutes with \(Q\otimes Q\), and \(QG=GP_\ell\). This establishes
exact logical parity at every coupling, not only at second order.

[proved] The full second-order bond is the source contraction

\[
 K_2=-\sum_{e,f,a,b}C_{ef}M_e^R[a,b]\otimes M_f^L[a,b].     \tag{5}
\]

There is no conjugation of the second displayed entry. Its scalar
part is \(a_0I=-3p^TCp\,I\). Terms linear in D vanish because
\(\operatorname{tr}A_u=0\). The odd-odd contraction uses
\(\sum_{a,b}(A_u)_{ab}(A_v^T)_{ab}=\operatorname{tr}(A_uA_v)=\delta_{uv}\)
and (3), so it is \(\rho^2K_{\rm hop}\). The Y field cannot contract
with either A. Its only contribution is
\((\rho^2/g)(p^TCd)I\otimes F=\rho^2I\otimes F\).
There is no field-field term, since the right weights have no field.
Thus

\[
 K_2=a_0I+\rho^2(K_{\rm hop}+I\otimes F).                  \tag{6}
\]

[proved] On distinct bonds at second order, an outer cell is touched
once. Its energy is preserved by every unperturbed resolvent, so its
final compression contains \(G^\dagger F_aG=0\). These cross terms
vanish. On a ring every site therefore receives F once. On an open
chain the first site has no field, which is the boundary convention
used in every calculation here.

[numerical, exact rational and matrix checks] Exact rational filter
identities passed at n=1,2,4,8,12. Full-cell matrices were checked at
n=1,2, dimensions 96 and 256. The code dimensions were 2 and 4; the
measured gaps differ from one by less than \(10^{-14}\). First-order
compressions and physical parity commutators were zero in the stored
arithmetic. The largest source-measure error was \(4.0\times10^{-15}\),
and the largest reflection error was \(3.6\times10^{-15}\). Weight
minima were 0.375 and 0.25. The direct second-order bond errors were
below \(3.4\times10^{-15}\), measured in operator norm. Independent
Jordan-Wigner multiplication gave zero hopping-sign error. Root
substitution errors were below \(2.3\times10^{-16}\).

## 4. Canonical fourth order, with its subtraction derived

[proved] This section applies to any finite cluster with
\(H=H_0+\lambda V\), \(H_0\ge0\), code projector P, and \(PVP=0\).
Write \(Q_c=I-P\), \(R=Q_cH_0^{-1}Q_c\), and \(B=Q_cVP\). The symbol
\(Q_c\) denotes an excited-space projector, not physical parity.
For sufficiently small lambda the low spectral subspace is a graph
\(P+X\), where \(X=Q_cXP\). Its invariance equation and Bloch Hamiltonian
are

\[
 H_0X+\lambda B+\lambda Q_cVQ_cX=XH_B,
 \qquad H_B=\lambda B^\dagger X.                          \tag{7}
\]

[proved] Expand \(X=\lambda X_1+\lambda^2X_2+\lambda^3X_3+\cdots\).
Solving (7) order by order gives

\[
 X_1=-RB,\qquad X_2=RVRB,
\]
\[
 X_3=-RVRVRB+R^2B A,\qquad A=B^\dagger RB,
 \qquad T=B^\dagger R^2B.                                \tag{8}
\]

For the last line the order-three right-hand side is
\(X_1B^\dagger X_1=RB A\). Multiplying by R supplies the second term.
Consequently

\[
 H_B=-\lambda^2A+\lambda^3B^\dagger RVRB
   +\lambda^4[-B^\dagger RVRVRB+TA]+O(\lambda^5).
\]

[proved] The graph columns have metric
\(M=I+X^\dagger X=I+\lambda^2T+O(\lambda^3)\). Their canonical
orthonormalization is \((P+X)M^{-1/2}\), so the Hermitian effective
Hamiltonian is \(H_{\rm can}=M^{1/2}H_BM^{-1/2}\).
The commutator contribution at order four is
\([T,-A]/2\). Combining it with TA gives

\[
 K_2=-A,\qquad K_3=B^\dagger RVRB,
\]
\[
 K_4=-B^\dagger RVRVRB+\tfrac12(TA+AT).                  \tag{9}
\]

This fixes the effective-Hamiltonian gauge. A lambda-dependent unitary
within the code could change fourth-order matrix coefficients, so the
gauge must be specified when comparing them.

[proved] For numerical evaluation define \(w=RB\), \(y=Vw\). Then

\[
 K_3=w^\dagger y,\qquad
 K_4=-y^\dagger Ry+\tfrac12(TA+AT),\quad T=w^\dagger w.   \tag{10}
\]

The inverse in the first term has zero entries on the complete code.
One must not retain code intermediate states in that inverse, and one
must not omit the anticommutator. In units with general source gap,
the terms in \(H_{\rm can}\) are
\(\lambda^2K_2/\Delta_{\rm src}\),
\(\lambda^3K_3/\Delta_{\rm src}^2\) and
\(\lambda^4K_4/\Delta_{\rm src}^3\), with the dimensionless matrices
K used in the code.

[proved, finite computational reduction] On an endpoint cell touched
at only one port, the other port-partner Bell pair stays in its Bell
state. Both the reflection and the touched port preserve this
subspace: the unused-side source and weight embeddings are orthogonal
to it, while the used-side embeddings are contained in it. The reducing
cell dimension is \(4(n+2)2^n\), namely 24 or 64. This is an invariant
subspace restriction, not an excited-level truncation. Two-cell
calculations therefore have dimensions 576 and 4096. Full-cell
calculations, dimensions 9216 and 65536, independently verify the same
operators. No source pole or virtual state in the reducing subspace
is omitted. The exact known integer cell energies are used after numerical
diagonalization; the largest residual of this spectral representation is
below \(8.0\times10^{-15}\). Reported graph residuals are in that
representation, with this separate basis error recorded.

### Adjacent bonds

[proved] Let A denote bond 12 and B denote bond 23. At order four a
nonzero connected sequence cannot have a bond occurring just once at
an outer endpoint, by the same compression argument as at second
order. Thus a connected cluster has either one bond four times, or
two adjacent bonds each twice. A connected three-bond cluster would
have four cells and require at least two insertions on each of the
two outer bonds and at least one on the middle bond, hence at least
five insertions. Disjoint-bond terms cancel in the canonical Hamiltonian.
To see the cancellation without assuming it, give the disjoint bonds
independent couplings. The exact low subspace and the polar code
identification factorize over the disjoint systems, so their canonical
Hamiltonian is a sum. All mixed Taylor coefficients vanish.

[proved] Write \(B_A=V_AP\), \(B_B=V_BP\), \(w_s=RB_s\). For the
three-cell irreducible term the vector y in (10) is

\[
 y=V_Aw_A+V_Bw_B+V_Bw_A+V_Aw_B.                           \tag{11}
\]

The outer-cell compression kills \(B_A^\dagger R^mB_B\) for m=1,2.
Therefore the three-cell A and T in (9) are the sums of their two
embedded bond matrices. Evaluate (10) using (11), then subtract the
two embedded one-bond K4 operators. The remainder is the connected
adjacent-bond matrix \(K_{4,\rm adj}\).

[proved, evaluation formula] In cell energy bases, let the outer and
middle indices be i,j,k and the input code indices a,b,c. If
\(w_A(i,j;a,b)\) is the first-bond two-cell vector and
\(w_B(j,k;b,c)\) the second, the mixed pieces are

\[
 (V_Bw_A)_{ijk;abc}=\sum_{s,h}(F_s^R)_{jh}
                         w_A(i,h;a,b)(F_s^L)_{kc},
\]
\[
 (V_Aw_B)_{ijk;abc}=\sum_{s,h}(F_s^R)_{ia}
                         (F_s^L)_{jh}w_B(h,k;b,c).        \tag{12}
\]

The unmixed pieces are their two-cell y matrices tensored with the
untouched code embedding. Divide their sum by
\(E_i+E_j+E_k\) in the contraction, taking the inverse to be zero
when all three energies vanish. `three_cell.py` streams the middle
index in blocks of eight and sums these contractions. This includes
all middle-cell states and all endpoint states in the reducing spaces.

## 6. The rho-squared statement, proved at finite order

[proved] Treat rho as an independent real parameter in (4), holding
the target and pole positions fixed. The positive weights, their
square roots, the reflection and the full cell Hamiltonian are analytic
near rho=0. The radius can depend on n and the target. The field term
is quadratic in rho. At rho=0 every cell Hamiltonian and every port
operator acts as the identity on the logical register. This is the
spectator property.

[proved] Let \(\mathscr P\) act by bare logical parity on each cell and
trivially on all auxiliaries. Conjugation by it changes D to minus D
and leaves F unchanged. Thus on any fixed cluster

\[
 H(-\rho,\lambda)=\mathscr P H(\rho,\lambda)\mathscr P.    \tag{14}
\]

Let \(\mathscr Q\) be the product of the conserved physical cell
parities. For every rho,
\([H(\rho,\lambda),\mathscr Q]=0\). On the code both
\(\mathscr P\) and \(\mathscr Q\) induce the same
\(P_{\rm log}=\prod_jP_{\ell,j}\).

[proved] The canonical effective Hamiltonian is covariant under a
unitary preserving the unperturbed code. This follows directly from
(7) and the unique graph of the isolated low subspace: X, its metric
and its canonical orthonormalization conjugate together. Applying
that covariance to (14) and then to the conserved physical parity
shows

\[
 H_{\rm can}(-\rho)=P_{\rm log}H_{\rm can}(\rho)P_{\rm log}
                  =H_{\rm can}(\rho).                  \tag{15}
\]

The same identity holds coefficient by coefficient in lambda. This
is stronger than the statement that an effective operator is parity
even. Parity evenness by itself would not prohibit a coefficient
linear in rho multiplying a parity-even operator.

[proved] At rho=0 the auxiliary problem is independent of the logical
state, so every effective coefficient is scalar on the full logical
code. Subtracting its normalized trace, analyticity and (15) give

\[
 K_r^\circ(\rho)=\rho^2\widetilde K_r(\rho^2),\qquad r\ge2.\tag{16}
\]

Every \(\widetilde K_r\) is analytic and bounded on a sufficiently
small compact rho interval for that fixed cluster. The connected
coefficients inherit the identity by subtracting subclusters. This
proves the factor at arbitrary finite order. It does not bound
\(\widetilde K_r\) uniformly in r, n or cell length.

[proved, quantitative limitation] If \(K_r^\circ\) is analytic on a
complex rho disk of radius R and bounded by M there, Cauchy's bound
on its even Taylor coefficients gives, for \(|\rho|\le a<R\),

\[
 \|K_r^\circ(\rho)\|\le
 {M|\rho|^2\over R^2[1-(a/R)^2]} .                       \tag{17}
\]

The bound explicitly depends on the analyticity radius. That radius
may shrink with n because the filters grow. Therefore neither (16)
nor the n=1,2 numerics establishes coefficients of order one at all n.
The task's fourth-order order-one test passes at its two specified sizes.

## 7. What the first-order infrared audit has to change

[proved, algebraic translation of scales] The original audit uses a
first-order retained scale \(W_1=\lambda\eta\) and a second-order
error. Here the first-order payload vanishes, and the complete desired
payload is (6), so

\[
 W={\lambda^2\rho^2\over\Delta_{\rm src}},\qquad
 H_{\rm pay}/W=H_*+x\widetilde\Phi_3+x^2\widetilde\Phi_4+\cdots,
 \quad x={\lambda\over\Delta_{\rm src}}.                \tag{18}
\]

For a fixed cluster (18) is a proved expansion after removing scalar
energy shifts. To replace the audit's equation (5), its numerator must
start at order lambda cubed after subtracting the entire second-order
target. Merely substituting W into the old order-lambda-squared error
would leave an order-one error and would not establish transfer.

[proved, finite-order consequence] With constants referring to the
traceless payload and including the relevant support weights, the
finite-order replacement has the form

\[
 {\epsilon_{\rm pay}\over W}
 \le c_3|x|+c_4x^2+O_n(|x|^3)
 +{\epsilon_{\rm core}\Delta_{\rm src}\over\lambda^2\rho^2}
 +\epsilon_{\rm synth}.                                \tag{19}
\]

Here \(\epsilon_{\rm core}\) is an additive code splitting or an
uncontrolled first-order retained term in energy units, and
\(\epsilon_{\rm synth}\) is the error in the synthesized second-order
bond divided by W. The finite-cluster expansion proves the first
three terms at fixed size. The corresponding local-norm statement for
an infinite chain is the assumption in Section 8.

[proved, comparison of implementations] If a compiler preserves
neither (14) nor the spectator property, a generic cubic error has
size \(C\lambda^3/\Delta_{\rm src}^2\), giving
\(C|x|/\rho^2\) in (19). If it preserves them, (16) removes that
explicit loss at finite order. Separately, a fixed nonzero core
splitting becomes more important as lambda decreases because its
relative size is proportional to lambda to the minus second power.
It must vanish exactly, or be reduced together with the chosen
coupling. Relative weight errors, for example
\(\epsilon_{\rm synth}\le S(n)\epsilon_w\) with the assembly's
sensitivity S, are not improved by decreasing lambda.

[heuristic, conditional replacement for audit equation (6)] Let
\(\varepsilon_n>0\) be a tolerance lying inside the complete Gaussian
stability neighborhood. A sufficient hierarchy for a faithful cell is

\[
 c_3|x|+c_4x^2+\cdots\ll\varepsilon_n,\qquad
 \epsilon_{\rm core}\ll W\varepsilon_n,\qquad
 \epsilon_{\rm synth}\ll\varepsilon_n.                   \tag{20}
\]

There is no necessary lower bound on x. At every fixed constructed n,
all constants are finite and one can choose one fixed nonzero x before
taking the infinite-chain limit. Equation (20) is not a numerical
certification that x=0.02 or 0.1 is inside the phase. A local operator
norm is not automatically the BdG symbol norm or the normalized
bosonized coupling norm, and all such conversion constants must be
included.

[proved, audit equation (7)] The acceptance-pin lemma is unchanged
by using a second-order payload. Suppose P is the complete kernel projector
of H_init, P_good is a subprojector of P, a and gamma are positive, and B
bounds the norm of the positive operator A. If
\(H_{\rm init}\ge\gamma(I-P)\), \(0\le A\le B\),
\(AP_{\rm good}=0\), and
\(PAP\ge a(P-P_{\rm good})\), then

\[
 \operatorname{gap}(H_{\rm init}+A)\ge
                     {a\gamma\over a+B+\gamma}.          \tag{21}
\]

For a normalized vector orthogonal to \(P_{\rm good}\), write x=P psi and
y=(I-P)psi. The energy is at least
\(\gamma\|y\|^2+(\sqrt a\|x\|-\sqrt B\|y\|)_+^2\).
Where the bracket is positive, the two-by-two quadratic form has
determinant a gamma and trace a+B+gamma, so its smaller eigenvalue
is at least determinant divided by trace. Where it vanishes,
\(\|y\|^2\ge a/(a+B)\), giving the stronger lower bound
\(a\gamma/(a+B)\). The kernel is consequently the good code.
The compiler still has to meet the lemma's hypotheses.

## 8. The local transfer statement and its remaining assumptions

[assumed for the proposed spatial implementation, proved for the
abstract independent-cell base] The audit's equation (8) remains a
local defect-energy requirement. For fixed legal marker pattern,

\[
 H_{\rm base}-eL\ge
 \Delta_*\sum_{j\,\mathrm{selected}}Q_j
 +\gamma_{\rm len}\sum_{j\,\mathrm{wrong}}(1+|\ell_j-b|),
 \qquad \delta_* = \min(\Delta_*,\gamma_{\rm len})>0.     \tag{22}
\]

For the unwrapped reflection cells this follows by summing the proved
one-cell inequality \(H_j\ge\Delta_{\rm src}Q_j\), with no wrong-length
sector and \(\delta_*=\Delta_{\rm src}\). For the mobile model it is
the inherited C8 selector/core condition, conditional on a compatible
positive spatial core and the complete selected-cell spectrum. A
single global gap estimate is not a substitute for (22).

[proved, finite histories] During an unperturbed excursion carrying
m excited cells, (22) bounds propagation for time tau by
\(e^{-m\delta_*\tau}\). Each microscopic insertion acts on a bounded
number of neighboring cells. On any fixed finite ordered connected
history its time integrals therefore give inverse defect energies,
not inverse spacings between logical levels. An interval spent wholly
in the code must be treated as retained propagation. At second order
that propagation has scale W. Expanding it as if it were a high-energy
resolvent would use a false denominator.

[assumed, local expansion input replacing audit equation (9)] Suppose
the connected local construction can be organized in a symmetry-covariant
analytic gauge, preserves (14) and the rho=0 spectator property, and
has an exponential support bound. Let B bound the microscopic port
interaction and absorb fixed compensator norms. A sufficient form is

\[
 \|\Phi_r^\circ\|_{\rm loc}\le
 \rho^2 A_n{(B|\lambda|)^2\over\delta_*}
 \left({C_nB|\lambda|\over\delta_*}\right)^{r-2},
 \qquad r\ge2.                                         \tag{23}
\]

The constants may depend on the entire fixed cell and on the permitted
rho disk, but not on total chain length. The finite-order factor in
(23) is proved in Section 6; the all-order local bound is an assumption.
The r=2 term must be replaced by the actual target, not counted as an
error. For \(z=C_nB|\lambda|/\delta_*<1\), summing r at least three
and dividing by W gives the sufficient schematic replacement

\[
 {\epsilon_{\rm pay}\over W}\le
 A_nB^2{\Delta_{\rm src}\over\delta_*}{z\over1-z}
 +{\epsilon_{\rm core}\Delta_{\rm src}\over\lambda^2\rho^2}
 +\epsilon_{\rm synth}.                                \tag{24}
\]

This records the extra ratio if the actual smallest excluded gap is
below the source gap. Setting both gaps equal recovers an error that
starts linearly in \(|\lambda|/\Delta_{\rm src}\), with no explicit
inverse rho-squared factor.

[assumed, time-resolved replacement for audit equation (10)] After
separating the scalar auxiliary self-energy, a sufficient payload
kernel majorant is

\[
 \|\mathcal K^\circ(\tau)\|_{\rm loc}\le
 \rho^2 A_n(B\lambda)^2
       e^{-(\delta_*-C_nB|\lambda|)\tau}.                 \tag{25}
\]

One may subtract the known second-order payload kernel, whose integral
is W times the target; its higher-insertion remainder has an additional
factor of order z under (23). Integrating (25) gives a Hamiltonian
coefficient. Inserting an extra tau in the integral gives the frequency
derivative, so the expansion at a retained frequency omega is suppressed
by \(|\omega|/(\delta_*-C_nB|\lambda|)\). At the payload scale this
is of order \(W/\delta_*\), not of order one. Scalar kernel terms
need their own wavefunction normalization; equation (9) explicitly
includes the resulting finite-order effect.

[heuristic, mechanism for the assumed bound] The linked excursion
argument keeps the entire critical propagator inside the retained
theory. New spatial steps need extra insertions. In a state with m
active defects the number of local insertion positions is proportional
to m, while its energy cost is at least m times delta star. These are
the inputs to the usual connected massive-mode estimate (23).
For moving markers the bookkeeping, disconnected cancellations and
uniform support sum must still be constructed. The finite results
here do not prove that construction. The same limitation was present
in the first-order audit, and it is not removed by computing K4.

[assumed, Gaussian infrared input] The parity-only payload uses the
operator classification from ir_control, rather than the species-torus
selection rule of the sparse first-order route. In a small neighborhood
of the free point, the parity-even nonneutral charges of free dimension
at most two have squared norm two or four. They are
\(\pm e_i\pm e_j\), \(\pm2e_i\), and signed sums on four distinct
branches. These are protected by \(\delta_2\) and \(\delta_4\).
Neutral currents shift the roots, and neutral quadratic currents
deform the Gaussian stiffness. Other parity-even integer charges
have squared norm at least six and free dimension at least three.
If the dressed dimension form is bounded below by kappa times the
free one with \(\kappa>2/3\), those remaining nonneutral vertices
stay irrelevant. This is a statement using the bosonized dictionary,
not a microscopic CFT theorem.

[heuristic, sufficient dressed conditions] Include the matching
constants from the effective interaction to continuum couplings.
Quadratic corrections must be small in the symbol and its first
momentum derivative compared with \(v_{\min}\delta_2\). To preserve
both bare margins without retuning, a sufficient root-shift condition
is \(\max_i|\Delta p_i|<\min(\delta_2/4,\delta_4/8)\), omitting
the four-point restriction when it is absent. Then every two-charge
and four-charge mismatch remains at least half its original margin.
The root-shift bound is typically obtained from a perturbation bound
divided by the smallest velocity. Near-marginal oscillatory couplings
g must also obey \(g\log(1/\delta_4)\ll1\), with the dimensionless
cutoff ratio in the logarithm, or the corresponding more precise
\(g e^{x_+\ell_\delta}\ll1\) condition. The stiffness matrix must
remain positive. Under these conditions and (23), standard Gaussian
RG predicts c=n at fixed nonzero lambda.

[assumed, mobile extension] For the length fluid choose marker hopping
\(u=\kappa_0W/\chi\), where chi is its projected hopping factor and
\(\kappa_0>0\) fixes the ratio of the two retained bandwidths. Generic
mixed and marker-only errors bounded by
\(C_{mp}u|\lambda|B/\delta_*+C_{mm}u^2/\delta_*\) have normalized sizes

\[
 C_{mp}{\kappa_0B|\lambda|\over\chi\delta_*}
 +C_{mm}{\kappa_0^2W\over\chi^2\delta_*}.                \tag{26}
\]

Both can be made small at fixed cell resources. The mixed momentum
margins and stiffness conditions are the separate C13/C20 input.
No use is made here of a claimed rho-squared suppression of every
length-fluid interaction.

[proved, limitation important for G3] A term scalar on the payload can
still depend on the neighboring cell lengths. It is then an interaction
of the length fluid and cannot be removed as one global scalar. Such
a term can start at \(\lambda^2/\Delta_{\rm src}\), which is larger
than W by \(1/\rho^2\). It must be equalized, compensated or included
in a separately controlled length-fluid reference. Likewise an
unequal second-order payload bond produces a relative error independent
of lambda. The rho argument for fixed cells does not solve either
problem. They belong to the stated wrapper-composition hypotheses,
not to an extra obstruction to the Sidon cell itself.

[proved, order of limits] The retained manifold has extensive bandwidth
\(O(LW)\). For fixed nonzero lambda it generally overlaps machinery
excitation energies at sufficiently large L. A finite-cluster isolated
band and an error of order L times epsilon cannot resolve conformal
gaps of order \(1/L\). Thus no global-band Schrieffer-Wolff estimate
from this calculation establishes the thermodynamic infrared limit.
The conditional argument uses (22)-(25) for local correlations, with
all cell parameters and nonzero couplings fixed before L tends to
infinity.
