# Recovering RG endpoint powers from a finite microscopic degree

## 1. The precise endpoint issue

An analytic coefficient norm by itself only says that a degree-`l` kernel
is bounded by `C H^-l eta`. A useful stronger hierarchy is

    ||K_l|| <= C^l eta^(max(1,l/2-1)),        l even.          (1)

**[read]** In Antinucci, Mastropietro and Porta,
[arXiv:1708.08517v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.08517v4), equation (9.55)
contains the endpoint factor `C^(p_i)|lambda|^(p_i/2-1)`. Initial endpoints
can have arbitrary field degree; lower-scale endpoints are localized
quadratic/quartic terms. Remarks 9.1–9.2 give short-memory and continuity
estimates. Equation (9.72) separates trees touching the initial scale
from purely lower-scale trees before comparing the latter to the
relativistic theory. This is the source of the endpoint hierarchy tested
here.

**[read]** Mastropietro and Porta,
[arXiv:2108.13708v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.13708v3), Section 10,
uses that tree comparison for the multichannel model. Its Theorem 8.5
controls the reference beta function for a sufficiently small matrix of
forward couplings. The emergent chiral symmetry is a property of the
reference theory, not a required microscopic chiral symmetry.

## 2. A general order-degree lemma, including the narrow bandwidth

**[proved]** Let a physical-time residual action have a convergent
expansion

    K(lambda)=sum_(q>=3) lambda^q K^[q].                     (2)

Assume, in a fixed field-weighted exponential space/time norm,

    ||K^[q]||_H <= C A^q,
    K_l^[q]=0 whenever l>Dq,                                (3)

where `D>=2` is a fixed integer. Let the quadratic logical bandwidth be
`W=c lambda^2`, `c>0`. For definiteness take real `0<lambda`, and choose
it so `x=A lambda<=1/2` and `W<=1`.

Rescale time by `u=W t`, as in RG_EXTENSION (2). Each pinned kernel norm
picks up the common factor `W^-1`. Its temporal exponential decay rate
improves from `kappa` to `kappa/W`, so the original fixed rate `kappa`
is also available in the rescaled units.

At a fixed external degree `l`, the first possible perturbative order is

    q_l=max(3,ceil(l/D)).

Taking absolute values of the convergent expansion, using (3), and
applying the time rescaling term by term gives

    H^l ||K'_l(lambda)||
       <=(C/c) lambda^-2 sum_(q>=q_l)(A lambda)^q
       <=(2 C A^2/c) x^(q_l-2).                            (4)

Set

    epsilon=x^(1/(2D)),
    d_l=max(2,l/2-1).                                     (5)

Then

    x^(q_l-2) <= epsilon^d_l.                             (6)

To verify (6), recall `0<x<1`. It is enough to check
`q_l-2>=d_l/(2D)`. First, `q_l>=3` implies `q_l-2>=1>=1/D`.
Second, `l<=D q_l`, so

    (l/2-1)/(2D) <= q_l/4-1/(2D) <= q_l-2,

the last inequality holding for every `q_l>=3` and `D>=2`.
Combining (4) and (6),

    H^l ||K'_l(lambda)|| <= C_* epsilon^(max(2,l/2-1)).      (7)

This is stronger than (1): every extra residual endpoint, including
quadratic and quartic ones, is `O(epsilon^2)`. The estimate preserves
the exponential moment norm. Fixed conversions to Fermi-branch labels
and fixed Taylor moment losses can be absorbed by reducing the field
weight once, as in RG_EXTENSION.

The price is an extremely weak smallness variable: if `D` is enormous,
making `epsilon` small requires extraordinarily small microscopic
`lambda`. For an existence statement at a fixed cell dimension this is
permitted. It is not a numerically practical coupling schedule.

## 3. Why finite local microscopic rules give the degree hypothesis

**[proved sufficient mechanism]** Suppose the finite spectral embedding
uses `F` fermion modes per cell. Any operator on `r` cells can be written
as a normal-ordered polynomial of degree at most `2Fr`.

Assume the original perturbation is finite range and the exact finite-order
SW change of basis is generated by

    S(lambda)=lambda S_1+...+lambda^k S_k,

where `k` is fixed and each `S_i` is a bounded finite-range interaction.
Consider a coefficient of total order `q` in the exact conjugated
Hamiltonian. A nested commutator contributing at this order uses at most
`q` generator terms, since every generator insertion has order at least
one. Each nonzero commutator has overlapping supports. Its support
therefore has at most

    r_initial+q r_generator

cells, with fixed safe range bounds. After finite spectral embedding its
degree is at most

    2F(r_initial+q r_generator)<=D q,                       (8)

for `q>=1`, after increasing the fixed integer `D`.

The same argument applies to the block-diagonal reference terms and to
the exact residual. Conjugation analyticity in a sufficiently strong
local interaction norm gives a geometric coefficient bound by Cauchy's
estimate inside a smaller fixed disk. The bounded-range connected
commutator count in SINGLE_EXPONENTIAL provides one direct way to obtain
such a disk. The exponential support weight is chosen before the disk;
its deterioration changes constants, not finiteness.

When auxiliary fermions are integrated out, a coefficient of order `q`
is built from vertices of orders `q_1,...,q_s` with sum `q`. The total
number of fields before Wick contraction is at most
`D sum_i q_i=Dq`; contractions only remove fields. Thus the bound on
external degree survives every term of the convergent massive expansion.

For the finite-cell theorem, the exact auxiliary-vacuum cancellation of
the block-diagonal reference eliminates every contribution without a
residual insertion. If that residual starts at order three, the matched
correction starts at `q=3`. Analyticity in the coefficient norm then
supplies the other assumption in (3).

This is a sufficient mechanism, not a claim that every unspecified
quasi-local SW construction has the required support-order structure.
The chosen microscopic transform must have the finite generator form
above, or its own corresponding support-order bound. The exact finite
cell dimension and positive auxiliary gap are held fixed throughout.

## 4. A nonanalyticity caveat and an artificial analytic parameter

**[proved caveat]** Even if physical-time kernels are analytic in
`lambda`, rescaling time by `W=c lambda^2` need not give a Banach-analytic
family through `lambda=0`. A factor `exp(-g|t|)` becomes
`exp[-g|u|/(c lambda^2)]`; its zero-width limit can be an instantaneous
measure. Pointwise or norm analyticity cannot be inferred from (4).

This does not obstruct using (7) at a fixed microscopic `lambda`. If an
analytic auxiliary parameter is needed for an RG construction, freeze
that `lambda` and all of its kernels, and introduce

    K_l(z)=(z/epsilon)^d_l K'_l(lambda).                   (9)

Equation (7) gives `H^l ||K_l(z)||<=C_* |z|^d_l`. Since `d_l` grows
linearly with `l`, the degree sum converges for `|z|` in a fixed small
disk. Thus (9) defines an artificial analytic family whose value at
`z=epsilon` is the desired physical action. It preserves parity and
number conservation because every monomial is multiplied by a scalar.
Real/Hermitian kernel relations are preserved for real `z`.

This construction is a device for estimates; it does not claim that
all other `z` describe the original microscopic Hamiltonian. The fixed
quadratic covariance and its Fermi points can be kept unchanged while
this family is used.

## 5. What the proof comparison now does, and does not, require

**[derived proof-level reduction]** The degree obstruction can therefore
be removed for the finite-cell residual, provided its actual analytic
order and support bounds have been verified. One need not ask a generic
`O(r)` coefficient norm to imply (1).

At the RG starting scale extract the quadratic constant and linear terms
and the resonant quartic constants. Translation invariance, exact global
U(1), distinct Fermi points and the elastic-scattering margin leave only
the usual number-conserving quadratic terms and forward quartic terms
in this localized part. The rest has the extra scaling powers proved
in RG_EXTENSION. These initial extra endpoints may violate separate
conservation of each Fermi branch; they still live at the initial scale.

A tree containing one such endpoint necessarily reaches that initial
scale. If the renormalized GN estimate is established with the endpoint
weights (7), its usual path extraction gives the short-memory factor
`2^(theta h)` at root scale `h<0`. Pure lower-scale localized trees
contain only the forward vertices of the reference model. Its Ward
identities are therefore used on the same reference model, without
requiring the high-degree microscopic interactions to possess exact
chiral symmetries.

The remaining technical verification is now sharply delimited: restate
and prove the multichannel GN majorant with the initial norm and degree
weights (7), including the needed moment and counterterm-derivative
bounds. Then carry that majorant through the short-memory comparison
and the relevant-counterterm fixed point. The source papers carry this
out for kernels generated from their stated microscopic interactions;
they do not state an abstract theorem accepting (7) as replacement data.
The inspected comparison provides a clear path to such a theorem but
is not, by itself, a complete new proof of it.

In particular, the all-degree endpoint hierarchy fits a decisive
published tree bound. No new unsuppressed marginal operator is forced
by those degrees or by exponential retardation. A general microscopic
chiral Ward identity is not the missing hypothesis. Uniformity of the
extended GN induction and the counterterm solution are the remaining
proof tasks at this stage.

## 6. Why the stronger hypothesis matters

**[proved counterexample to a shortcut]** A bound of the form
`||K_l(lambda)||<=lambda exp(-l)` at every even degree can define a
perfectly analytic, coefficient-summable action. Nevertheless, there
need be no finite `a>0` and constants `C_l<=C^l` such that, uniformly as
`lambda->0`,

    lambda exp(-l) <= C_l (lambda^a)^(l/2-1).

Fix any even `l` with `a(l/2-1)>1` and send `lambda` to zero. The ratio
of the left side to the right side diverges. Thus arbitrary small
analytic kernel norms do not supply the necessary degree-order powers.
The finite microscopic degree and its linear growth with perturbative
order are substantive additional information.

The current result recovers that information and the endpoint hierarchy
for the stated finite-cell mechanism. It does not solve the unbounded
mobile-cell matching, prove a full CFT limit, or identify the pressure's
`T^2` coefficient. Those limitations remain exactly as recorded in
RG_EXTENSION.
