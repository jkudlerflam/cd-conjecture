# C75: The smallest route-A alphabet that violates c ≤ d − 1
Your folder is `../work_C75/`. Read `../../specs/_simplest_background.md` first. Task: shrink the 23-letter bond. For each group of letters (data L/R with bits, vacuum and excited tokens on the data track, the express track used to fold port order, charged species used for the conversions that separate Fermi-sea centres, markers) decide whether it is essential, and find the smallest alphabet a for which a member with cell length n + 1 has n distinct incommensurate Fermi-point pairs with positive pair-sum margins and an exact U(1), with c ≥ a (frozen) or c ≥ a after adding one vacancy (d = a + 1, c = n + 1 ≥ a + 1). Candidates to test include merging the express track into the data track with a different folding mechanism, a single charged species, cells with two tokens, or encodings where the data bit and token share a letter. For the best design give the explicit bond, the required n, the coupling hierarchy, and finite certificates (exact or high-precision band crossings and pair-sum margins) at that n, with second-order exactness checks by small exact diagonalization. A violation at d noticeably below 24 would be a significant simplification.
