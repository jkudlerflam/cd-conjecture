# Rigorous toolbox map for the C(d) target

2026-11-06. Literature map for the target of `specs/_Cd_target.md`: prove

    C(d) = sup_h sup_L L (E_{2L} - 2 E_L) / M < infinity

over one-site translation-invariant nearest-neighbour bonds h on C^d (x) C^d and vacuum-compatible ring lengths L, or one of the variants E_L >= e_0 L - K(d) M/L, R_L(beta) = log[Z(L,beta)^2/Z(2L,beta)] <= K(d) + kappa(d) M beta/L. Scope update from Jonah (same day): a finite bound at one fixed d, for example C(2) < infinity for every spin-1/2 nearest-neighbour chain, already counts. Section 8 maps the d = 2 literature.

Tags. [Lit, read: X] means I read statement X in the source (theorem text, or only the abstract when marked "abstract"). [Lit, recalled] means a standard result I did not reopen in this survey, stated only qualitatively. [Assessment] is my judgment. [Heuristic] is an unproved physical expectation. [Numerical] refers to `d2_sanity.py` in this folder. Lessons L1 to L5 are the five lessons of `specs/_Cd_target.md` (L1 crossed-channel data insufficient, L2 local energy functionals see near-critical massive modes, L3 unfiltered counting fails and crossovers are unbounded, L4 operator-order, Araki and extensive comparisons lose L or exp(exp(beta M)) or L beta, L5 Theta convexity is false in general).

## 0. Summary and ranking

[Assessment] No theorem in the surveyed literature gives L(E_{2L} - 2E_L) <= C M, or any O(1/L) Casimir-type lower bound on the ring ground energy, for a class of interacting chains defined by local dimension alone. Every general (h-uniform) tool I found is either a locality estimate whose constants depend on beta M exponentially or worse (L4), a statement about the thermodynamic limit at fixed beta, or an O(M)-per-cut energy comparison (L2, L4). The only rigorous O(1/L) or Casimir-level finite-size results are for (a) free fermions and free fields, (b) integrable chains via Bethe ansatz, at fixed parameters and not uniformly, and (c) small non-integrable perturbations of free fermions via constructive renormalization group. The promising directions are therefore class results that a covering argument could assemble, and at d = 2 the class structure is small enough that this is a realistic program.

## 1. Rigorous finite-size scaling and torus partition functions

Borgs–Kotecký, "A rigorous theory of finite-size scaling at first-order phase transitions", J. Stat. Phys. 61 (1990) 79 (no arXiv; preprint HUTMP 90/B259). [Lit, read: Theorem 4.1] For a classical lattice model with N ground states satisfying a Peierls condition with constant tau, contour activities |rho(Y)| <= e^{-(tau + e_0)|Y|}, there are tau_0, b_0 > 0 depending only on N and the dimension such that for tau > tau_0

    | Z_per(T) - sum_{q in Q} exp(-f'_q L^d) | <= exp(-f L^d - b_0 tau L),

with f'_q the metastable free energies and f = min_q f'_q. Companion: Borgs–Kotecký–Miracle-Solé, J. Stat. Phys. 62 (1991) 529, Potts models [Lit, read: abstract]. Quantum versions: Borgs–Kotecký–Ueltschi, CMP 181 (1996) 409, H = H^(0) + lambda V with H^(0) classical, finitely many ground states, Peierls condition, lambda small, mapped to a classical contour system in one more dimension [Lit, read: abstract]; Datta–Fernández–Fröhlich, J. Stat. Phys. 84 (1996) 455, same class [Lit, read: citation only]. Kotecký–Preiss, CMP 103 (1986) 491, the cluster expansion convergence criterion underlying these [Lit, recalled].

Application. [Assessment] In 1+1 dimensions on the L x beta torus these results give, for a bond close to a classical bond with q ground states and a Peierls gap, Z(L, beta) = sum_q exp(-beta e_q L) (1 + O(e^{-b L}) + O(e^{-b' beta})) uniformly, hence R_L <= log q + o(1) and D(L) exponentially small on lengths compatible with every ground state. This is the rigorous form of C127's observation that K(d) >= log d is forced by discrete zero-frequency sectors, and it fixes the constant for ordered points. It could cover the gapped ordered points of a d = 2 compactness argument (Section 8.5) near classical Ising-type bonds. Where it loses: it is perturbative in the quantum term and needs a Peierls condition, so it says nothing at or near critical points. No collision with L1 to L5; it simply does not reach the gapless regime.

## 2. Multivariate trace inequalities

Golden–Thompson, Tr e^{A+B} <= Tr e^A e^B [Lit, recalled]. Lieb 1973 triple-matrix inequality and Sutter–Berta–Tomamichel, "Multivariate trace inequalities", CMP 352 (2017) 37, arXiv:1604.03023 [Lit, read: Corollary 3.3, Eqs. (38) to (40)]: for Hermitian H_k and p >= 1,

    log || exp(sum_k H_k) ||_p <= integral dt beta_0(t) log || prod_k exp((1 + it) H_k) ||_p,

with beta_0 an explicit probability density, and for n = 3, p = 2 this is equivalent to Lieb's Tr e^{H1+H2+H3} <= integral_0^inf Tr e^{H1}(e^{-H2} + lambda)^{-1} e^{H3}(e^{-H2} + lambda)^{-1} dlambda (their Lemma 3.4). These are upper bounds only. Lower bounds: Peierls–Bogoliubov (Gibbs variational principle) [Lit, recalled], and Hiai–Petz, LAA 181 (1993) 153, as stated in Carlen–Lieb arXiv:1709.05450, Eq. (1.10) [Lit, read]: Tr[(e^{rH} #_t e^{rK})^{1/r}] <= Tr e^{(1-t)H + tK} for all self-adjoint H, K, r > 0, t in [0, 1].

Application. Write H_{2L} = H_0 + V with H_0 two decoupled L rings and V the four-bond reconnection, ||V|| <= 4M after gauge. [Assessment] A lower bound on Z_{2L} = Tr e^{-beta(H_0 + V)} is what the target needs. Peierls–Bogoliubov gives R_L <= beta <V>_{rho_L (x) rho_L}, and <V> is a correlation energy of order M at each seam, so R_L <= O(beta M), the factor L lost in C116 and C118. Hiai–Petz with H = -2 beta H_0, K = -2 beta V, t = 1/2 gives a geometric mean in which e^{-2 r beta V} has norm up to e^{8 r beta M}, again an O(beta M) loss. Golden–Thompson and SBT point the wrong way. No dimension-free trace inequality can know that the seam cost averages to M/L over the ring, since that is a statement about the spectrum of H_0 near its ground state. Collides with L4 (operator-order comparisons lose L). The information identity of C118 (R_L = beta<V> + I + D + D) is the sharp version of this family, and its marginal relative entropies are where the 1/L would have to come from.

## 3. One-dimensional thermal states

Araki, CMP 14 (1969) 120: analyticity of the free energy and exponential clustering of the infinite-chain Gibbs state at every beta for finite-range translation-invariant interactions, through "expansionals" E_n(s) [Lit, read: as restated in BCPH Proposition 3.2]. The constant G = G(beta, J, r) > 1 bounds ||E_n(s)||, ||E_n(s)^{-1}|| and the complex-time Lieb–Robinson tails G^n/(floor(n/r) + 1)! [Lit, read: BCPH Prop. 3.2]. BCPH do not make G explicit. C122 records a volume-independent explicit version G(b) = exp[6b + 2(e^{2b} - 1)], b = beta M, which is the doubly exponential growth of L4. Pérez-García–Pérez-Hernández, arXiv:2004.10516 (CMP 2023), extend Araki to exponentially decaying interactions on a strip [Lit, read: abstract].

Bluhm–Capel–Pérez-Hernández, Quantum 6 (2022) 650, arXiv:2104.04419 [Lit, read: Theorems I, II]: for finite-range translation-invariant non-commuting interactions on Z and every beta, uniform exponential clustering Corr(A:C) <= eps(|B|) on every finite interval ABC, and I(A:C) <= I_BS(A:C) <= delta_1(|B|) with delta_1 exponentially decaying. The beta dependence enters only through Araki's G.

Kato–Brandão, CMP 370 (2019) 117, arXiv:1609.06636 [Lit, read: Theorem 4, Corollary 5]: for 1D short-range H with ||h_i|| <= 1 there is a recovery map with ||rho_ABC - Lambda_{B->BC}(rho_AB)||_1 <= exp(-q(beta) sqrt(d(A,C))), with q(beta) = c e^{-c' beta} when the correlation length is e^{O(beta)}, and I(A:C|B) <= O(e^{-Theta(sqrt(d(A,C)))}).

Kuwahara, "Clustering of conditional mutual information and quantum Markov structure at arbitrary temperatures", arXiv:2407.05835 [Lit, read: main theorem, Eqs. (7) to (9), via the HTML]: for two-body interactions with ||h_{i,i'}|| <= J e^{-mu l}, partition Lambda = A B C of the whole lattice, Z_beta normalized to one, in D = 1

    I(A:C|B) <= exp( -c_3 R/beta + c_4 beta log(beta R) ),   R = d(A, C),

with c_3, c_4 of order one, independent of |A|, |C|. The rate is R/beta, the physically expected thermal length, but the bound only becomes nontrivial for R greater than about (c_4/c_3) beta^2 log(beta R).

Kuwahara–Alhambra–Anshu, PRX 11 (2021) 011047, arXiv:2007.11174 [Lit, read: abstract]: thermal area law of order beta^{2/3} up to logarithms (improving the O(beta) of Wolf–Verstraete–Hastings–Cirac), and 1D Gibbs states approximated by MPOs of bond dimension e^{sqrt(O~(beta log n))}. Wolf–Verstraete–Hastings–Cirac, PRL 100 (2008) 070502, arXiv:0704.3906 [Lit, read: Eq. (3)]: I(A:B) <= beta Tr H_bd (rho_A (x) rho_B - rho_AB) <= 2 beta ||h|| |bd A|. Kliesch–Gogolin–Kastoryano–Riera–Eisert, PRX 4 (2014) 031019, arXiv:1309.0816 [Lit, read: abstract]: clustering above a universal critical temperature. Brandão–Kastoryano, CMP 2019, arXiv:1609.07877 [Lit, read: abstract]: uniform clustering plus approximate Markov imply log-depth preparation, both hold in 1D.

Application. [Assessment] Two uses are conceivable. (i) Bound R_L(beta) <= K in the regime beta <= beta_*(L) by gluing L-ring Gibbs states with a recovery map at each seam and using the Gibbs variational principle. With Kuwahara's 1D bound the gluing error is small only when L >> beta^2 log beta, so this gives R_L <= K for beta << sqrt(L/log L). The target asks R_L <= K + kappa M beta/L at every beta, which in this regime is the same as R_L <= K, so this would be a genuine partial result, but only the easy half: as beta/L grows past L^{-1/2}, and in particular at beta of order L/M where the Casimir term lives, nothing survives. (ii) The thermal area laws bound the mutual-information term in the C118 identity by O(beta^{2/3}) per cut, uniformly in L, but the identity also contains beta<V>, which is O(beta M), so R_L is not controlled. Where it loses: the onset length beta^2 log beta (Kuwahara), e^{O(beta)} (Kato–Brandão), or the unspecified G(beta) (Araki, BCPH). Collides with L4. Note also that a finite-temperature bound at beta of order L does not give the zero-temperature D(L) (F <= E_0 <= F + S/beta loses the entropy of the 2L ring, which carries the reciprocal-velocity sum of C116).

## 4. Finite-size criteria, clustering and LSM

Knabe, J. Stat. Phys. 52 (1988) 627, and Gosset–Mozgunov, J. Math. Phys. 57 (2016), arXiv:1512.00088 [Lit, read: abstract]: for frustration-free translation-invariant chains, an open n-site gap above 6/(n(n+1)) implies a uniform ring gap (Knabe had 1/(n - 1)), and gapless frustration-free chains have open-chain gaps O(n^{-2}). Lemm–Mozgunov, arXiv:1801.08915 [Lit, read: abstract]: threshold of order n^{-3/2} with boundaries, 1D and 2D. Anshu, PRB 101 (2020), arXiv:1909.01516 [Lit, recalled]. Bravyi–Gosset, arXiv:1503.04035 [Lit, read: abstract]: complete gapped/gapless classification of frustration-free translation-invariant spin-1/2 nearest-neighbour chains, with gapless open-chain gaps at most 1/(n - 1).

Application. [Assessment] If h has zero-energy ground states on every ring (frustration-free on rings, after shifting h >= 0), then E_L = 0 = e_0 L for all L and C = 0. So these criteria cover only chains where the target is already trivial. The interesting case of a chain frustration-free on open chains but frustrated on some rings reduces to the definition of vacuum-compatible lengths. No collision, no content.

Hastings–Koma, CMP 265 (2006) 781, math-ph/0507008 [Lit, read: abstract], and Nachtergaele–Sims, CMP 265 (2006) 119, math-ph/0506030 [Lit, recalled]: a gap implies exponential clustering. [Assessment] Irrelevant at criticality, and the finite-size gaps of the ring are not bounded below in terms of d (L3).

LSM and twists. Lieb–Schultz–Mattis 1961 and Affleck–Lieb, LMP 12 (1986) 57 [Lit, recalled]: for half-integer spin with U(1) rotation, translation (and reflection in the original), the twisted state U_L Psi has energy at most C/L above the ground state and is orthogonal to it. Yamanaka–Oshikawa–Affleck and Koma, quoted in Tasaki, J. Stat. Phys. 170 (2018) 653, arXiv:1708.05186 [Lit, read: Theorem 0 and Corollary 1a]: for U(1)-symmetric translation-invariant systems with hopping range r, unit cell p, maximal hopping t_bar and non-integer filling nu, the local twist W_l obeys <W_l^dagger H W_l> - E_L^GS <= C/l with

    C = 8 pi^2 (p + 1)(r + 1)^2 nu t_bar / p^2,

independent of L. Ogata–Tasaki, CMP 2019, arXiv:1808.08740 [Lit, read: abstract]: half-odd-integer spin chains with time reversal or Z2 x Z2 symmetry cannot have a unique gapped ground state. Ogata–Tachikawa–Tasaki, CMP 2021, arXiv:2004.06458 [Lit, read: abstract]: general projective-representation no-go theorems, no energy estimates.

Application. [Assessment] Twist bounds are exactly the "stiffness" inputs of the repo (§9.75, Kohn budget sum D_alpha <= 4M). They give variational O(M/L) control of twisted sectors of a fixed ring, which is what DRAFT_C129 uses to remove the DM twist at d = 2. They do not map two L rings into one 2L ring. The replica version of that map is C126's permutation twist, which is obstructed on-site (disconnected stabilizer). Finite-group LSM (Ogata–Tasaki) has no energy estimate at all, as C126 already notes. Collides with the C126 obstruction, not with L1 to L5.

## 5. Lattice-to-continuum, lattice Virasoro, sine-square deformation

Koo–Saleur, hep-th/9312156 [Lit, read in lit.md: abstract]: lattice Virasoro generators from Temperley–Lieb, non-rigorous in general. Osborne–Stottmeister, CMP (2022), arXiv:2107.13834 [Lit, read: abstract]: convergence of Koo–Saleur Virasoro approximants in an operator-algebraic Wilson–Kadanoff framework, for free fermions and WZW models. Morinelli–Morsella–Stottmeister–Tanimoto, CMP 387 (2021) 299, arXiv:2010.11121 [Lit, read: abstract]: wavelet scaling limits of free lattice fields only. Zini–Wang, CMP (2018), arXiv:1706.08497 [Lit, read: abstract]: scaling limit proved for the Ising anyonic chain M(4,3), conjectural criteria otherwise.

Katsura, J. Phys. A 45 (2012) 115003, arXiv:1110.2459 [Lit, read: abstract]: for the XY chain and the critical transverse-field Ising chain the open SSD chain has the same ground state as the periodic chain; in CFT the vacuum is an exact eigenstate of the SSD Hamiltonian and an exact ground state for affine (Kac–Moody) CFTs. Katsura, J. Phys. A 44 (2011) 252001, arXiv:1104.1721 [Lit, recalled: XY case]. Kenyon–Sun–Wilson, PTRF 2016, arXiv:1310.2603 [Lit, read: abstract]: for weighted Z^2-periodic bipartite planar dimer models on tori, log Z = A f_0 + fsc + o(1) with fsc depending only on the conformal shape and parity data, universal within the class.

Application. [Assessment] None of these is uniform over a class defined by d. The rigorous SSD statement gives lambda_min(H_SSD) = E_L/2 exactly for XY and critical Ising, which makes the identity H_L = (2/L) sum_x H_SSD^(x) of `_Cd_target.md` an equality on that class, but for interacting chains lambda_min(H_SSD) is a local energy functional (L2), as the target file already says. Kenyon–Sun–Wilson is the cleanest rigorous universal finite-size statement on tori, but it is a free-fermion theorem. These results belong in the free-fermion leg of the program (rank 3), where a uniform finite-L bound is provable by direct computation anyway.

## 6. Loop, random-current and height-function methods

Aizenman–Nachtergaele, CMP 164 (1994) 17, cond-mat/9310009 [Lit, read: abstract]: loop representation of SU(2S+1)-invariant chains with interaction -P^(0) (singlet projector), relation to Potts/FK clusters in one more dimension, and an Affleck–Lieb-type dichotomy (slow decay of correlations or translation symmetry breaking). Tóth, LMP 28 (1993) 75 [Lit, recalled]. Ueltschi, J. Math. Phys. 54 (2013), arXiv:1301.0811 [Lit, read: abstract]: random loop representation for spin-1/2 Heisenberg models with possibly anisotropic interactions and some SU(2)-invariant spin-1 models. Aizenman–Duminil-Copin–Warzel, AHP 21 (2020) 2737, arXiv:2002.02543 [Lit, read: abstract]: dimerization of the spin-S projector chain for all S > 1/2 and Néel order of spin-1/2 XXZ for Delta = cosh(lambda) > 1, through a shared loop representation with sqrt(Q) = 2S + 1 = 2 cosh(lambda). Duminil-Copin et al. on six-vertex: delocalization for a = b = 1, 1 <= c <= 2 and localization for c > 2 (as summarized in arXiv:2012.11675, Section 1.2) [Lit, read]. Björnberg–Ueltschi and Björnberg et al. on O(n)/SU(n) dimerization [Lit, recalled].

Application. [Assessment] These are structural (order, dimerization, delocalization) and contain no finite-size energy estimate. For R_L they give a positive representation of Z(L, beta) on stoquastic SU(2)-type chains. Z_{2L} versus Z_L^2 then differs by reconnecting loops at the two seam bonds, and the number of loops changes by at most the number of seam events, which is Poisson with mean of order beta M. That reproduces C121's per-event cost and the equivalence of REPORT §9.86, so this family hits L4, and the positivity it supplies is the positive transfer structure of C125 (L1). Their value for the target is at d = 2 phase identification (which spin-1/2 points are gapped, Section 8.4).

## 7. Rigorous O(1/L) and Casimir-type results; velocities

Constructive RG. Giuliani–Mastropietro, CMP 324 (2013) 179, arXiv:1211.3030 [Lit, read: abstract and the summary in Greenblatt arXiv:1409.1852]: for the 2D ferromagnetic Ising model with nearest-neighbour plus weak finite-range interactions of strength 0 < lambda < lambda_0, at criticality the finite-size correction to the free energy on the torus is universal, the central charge defined by the Affleck/Blöte–Cardy–Nightingale coefficient equals 1/2 for all such lambda. Greenblatt and Antinucci–Giuliani–Greenblatt extend to cylinders [Lit, read: search abstracts]. Benfatto–Mastropietro, J. Stat. Phys. 138 (2010) 1084, arXiv:0907.2837 [Lit, read: Eq. (1), Theorem 1.1]: for H = -sum[J_1 S^1 S^1 + J_2 S^2 S^2] - h sum S^3 + lambda sum v(x - y) S^3_x S^3_y, |v| <= C e^{-kappa|x-y|}, lambda small, J_1 = J_2, the Haldane relations X_+ = K, X_- = 1/K and 2 eta = K + 1/K - 2 hold with K analytic in lambda; the sound velocity v_s = v_F + O(lambda). [Lit, read] For v nearest-neighbour and J_1 = J_2 this model is XXZ in a field (integrable), so their non-integrable spin chains are beyond nearest neighbour at d = 2. Benfatto–Falco–Mastropietro, PRL 104 (2010) 075701, and Benfatto–Mastropietro on Drude weights [Lit, recalled].

Application. [Assessment] Constructive RG is the only rigorous method I found that produces the Casimir coefficient for non-integrable interacting systems. It works in a ball |lambda| < lambda_0(h_0) around a nondegenerate free critical model h_0 and controls all scales from the lattice to L, so inside the ball there are no unbounded crossovers and L3 does not bite. The quantum-chain ring version (zero temperature, finite L, the 1/L term with a uniform remainder) appears not to be stated in print for spin chains, but the cylinder and torus technology exists. Where it loses: lambda_0 depends on the Fermi velocities and on nondegeneracy of h_0, so the ball shrinks near slow or multicritical free models, and nothing is said far from free points. See `DRAFT_C130_constructive_rg_casimir.md`.

Bethe ansatz finite size. See Section 8.3. [Lit, read] DKKMT arXiv:2012.11675 Theorem 2 controls the sector free energy f_N^(n) of six-vertex to O(1/N) per site (O(1) in log Lambda), and Section 8 of that paper improves this only to O(1/(zeta (N - 2n) N)). So it does not reach the Casimir term. Kozlowski arXiv:1508.05741 proves an all-order asymptotic expansion of the counting function at fixed Delta > -1 and fixed density D in ]0, 1/2], from which the 1/L energy term should follow by standard Euler–Maclaurin analysis [Assessment: not stated there as a theorem about E_L].

Velocities. Lieb–Robinson, CMP 28 (1972) 251; Bravyi–Hastings–Verstraete, PRL 97 (2006) 050401, quant-ph/0603121 [Lit, read: abstract]; Damanik–Lukic–Yessen, CMP, arXiv:1407.5067 [Lit, read: search abstract], explicit positive lower bounds on the Lieb–Robinson velocity of periodic XY chains; Damanik–Lemm–Lukic–Yessen, anomalous bounds for quasi-periodic XY. [Assessment] The repo already has v_a <= 4eM (C109). None of these bounds the number of sectors with velocity in a shell, which is the missing multiplicity statement of the heuristic orientation in `_Cd_target.md`.

Complexity. Bausch–Cubitt–Lucia–Pérez-García, PRX 2020, arXiv:1810.01858 [Lit, read: abstract]: translation-invariant nearest-neighbour chains whose spectral gap is undecidable, gapped with a classical ground state for all sizes up to an uncomputable threshold and gapless beyond. Gottesman–Irani arXiv:0905.2419 (QMA_EXP for TI nearest-neighbour ground energies, in lit.md). [Assessment] At large fixed d this makes crossover lengths uncomputable, a strong form of L3. It does not contradict C(d) < infinity, but it means a proof must not rely on any computable bound on the length at which the infrared sets in, and "vacuum-compatible" must be defined so that lengths below a hidden vacuum period are excluded without computing that period. These constructions need large d and do not affect d = 2.

Kapustin–Spodyneiko, PRL 123 (2019) 060601 (in lit.md): no equilibrium energy currents, so c_L = c_R. [Assessment] Fixes the meaning of c, no bound.

## 8. The d = 2 map

### 8.1 Normal form of the general spin-1/2 bond

[Assessment, elementary] Write h = c 1 + sum_a (f_a sigma^a (x) 1 + g_a 1 (x) sigma^a) + sum_{ab} J_ab sigma^a (x) sigma^b. The gauge h -> h + o (x) 1 - 1 (x) o shifts f by o and g by -o, so only the uniform field b = f + g survives on the ring. Split J = S + A into symmetric and antisymmetric parts, A_ab = epsilon_abc D_c (Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya vector D). An on-site rotation U (x) U acts as R in SO(3) on (S, D, b), so S can be diagonalized. The general bond is therefore XYZ (J_x, J_y, J_z) plus DM vector D plus uniform field b, nine real parameters, eight after fixing the scale M = 1, modulo axis permutations and signs. Sublattice rotations by pi preserve the spectrum on even rings only when they map D and b to uniform vectors.

Jordan–Wigner with one mode per site maps the bond to a local fermion bond exactly when h commutes with a global parity prod sigma^z in some frame, which requires b and D along a principal axis z of S and S block-diagonal in (xy, z). Inside that class, J_z = 0 gives a quadratic fermion chain (XY + DM_z + b_z, free), and J_z != 0 adds a nearest-neighbour density–density term. Outside it the fermion bond contains strings and JW gives nothing local. Chapman–Flammia, Quantum 4 (2020) 278, arXiv:2003.05465 [Lit, read: abstract] reduce free-fermion solvability of Pauli-sum spin models to recognizing line graphs, which settles which of these bonds are free in some generalized JW frame.

### 8.2 Integrable and stoquastic subclasses

de Leeuw–Pribytok–Ryan, J. Phys. A 52 (2019) 505201, arXiv:1904.12005 [Lit, read: abstract]: all integrable spin-1/2 nearest-neighbour chains from regular difference-form R-matrices, 14 independent solutions, 8 of eight-vertex type and 6 new (some non-diagonalizable). [Lit, recalled] The Hermitian ones include XYZ (Baxter) and XXZ + DM_z + b_z, with XY + b (Lieb–Schultz–Mattis) free. Klassen–Terhal, Quantum 3 (2019) 139, arXiv:1806.05405 [Lit, read: abstract]: an efficient algorithm deciding whether an XYZ Hamiltonian is stoquastic under single-qubit unitaries, and examples needing non-Clifford rotations. [Assessment] The U(1) class XXZ + DM_z + b_z is integrable for every parameter, and its critical points are exactly those of twisted XXZ in a field. Non-integrable d = 2 critical chains therefore all lie off the U(1) class, typically on Ising-type surfaces (XYZ in a field, or DM off-axis), with c = 1/2 expected [Heuristic].

### 8.3 Rigorous spin-1/2 results relevant to finite size

Yang–Yang, Phys. Rev. 150 (1966) 321 [Lit, read: abstract and Kozlowski's restatement]: Bethe's hypothesis holds for the ground state of the periodic XXZ ring in every fixed number of down spins, by a Perron–Frobenius argument, and the ground state of each sector is unique. Yang–Yang II, Phys. Rev. 150 (1966) 327 [Lit, read: abstract]: properties of f(Delta, y) in the thermodynamic limit. Kozlowski, arXiv:1508.05741 [Lit, read: introduction and stated theorem]: for H = J sum (sigma^x sigma^x + sigma^y sigma^y + Delta sigma^z sigma^z) and every Delta > -1, the ground-state Bethe roots in sector N with N/L -> D in ]0, 1/2] condense with density rho(lambda|q), and the counting function has an all-order asymptotic expansion (Theorems 3.4, 3.5), also for particle-hole excitations. Earlier partial results: Dorlas–Samsonov for -1 < Delta <= 0 and Delta > Delta_0 > 1, Gusev (incomplete) [Lit, read: Kozlowski's review]. DKKMT arXiv:2012.11675 [Lit, read: Theorems 1, 2, 22]: six-vertex free energy for Delta < 1, and sector free energies to O(1/N) per site. Bravyi–Gosset–Liu–Wong arXiv:2607.14401 (in lit.md): gap at least twice the smallest longitudinal field for Suzuki–Fisher Hamiltonians.

### 8.4 Which spin-1/2 chains are gapless (rigorous statements)

[Lit] Gapless or degenerate: Affleck–Lieb (U(1) plus translation, half-integer spin) [recalled]; Ogata–Tasaki arXiv:1808.08740 (time reversal or Z2 x Z2, half-odd-integer spin, no unique gapped ground state) [read: abstract], which covers XYZ at zero field and any DM-free bond with b = 0. Gapless: XY family exactly (Lieb–Schultz–Mattis 1961) [recalled]; XXZ with -1 < Delta < 1 in the sense that Kozlowski's particle-hole excitations have energies condensing to zero [Assessment from read statement]; six-vertex height delocalization for Delta in [-1, 1/2] [read: DKKMT summary]. Gapped with two Néel ground states: XXZ with Delta > 1 (ADW arXiv:2002.02543) [read: abstract]. Frustration-free spin-1/2 chains: complete classification (Bravyi–Gosset) [read: abstract]. [Assessment] I found no rigorous classification of the gapless locus of the general nine-parameter bond, and no rigorous proof that the generic point of the complement is gapped. Bonds with a generic field have no LSM obstruction (the product state is allowed), consistent with generic gaps.

### 8.5 A d = 2 program

[Assessment] C107 shows the gauge quotient sphere is compact. For each fixed L, Q_L(h) = L(E_{2L} - 2E_L)/M is continuous on it, so C(2) < infinity follows if every bond h_0 has a neighbourhood U and a constant C_0 with Q_L(h) <= C_0 for all h in U and all even L. Points then fall into: (a) the U(1) class and its neighbourhood (DRAFT_C129 handles the class itself; a neighbourhood needs C130), (b) free chains and their neighbourhoods (C130 with the free-fermion leg), (c) gapped points (need an exponential finite-size estimate uniform in a neighbourhood; Borgs–Kotecký gives it near classical bonds with a Peierls condition, and I found no general statement for frustrated gapped chains), (d) non-integrable critical surfaces far from free points, where no rigorous tool exists, and (e) multicritical points (Heisenberg with its marginal operator, the ferromagnetic point Delta = -1 with z = 2, Lifshitz points where Ising lines meet the U(1) line). DRAFT_C131 states this reduction precisely and asks for the first two missing local lemmas.

[Numerical, `d2_sanity.py`, output in `d2_sanity_output.txt`] On 36 points of the U(1) class (Delta in {-0.95, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5}, D in {0, 0.3}, B in {0, 0.4, 0.8}) and 12 random Gaussian bonds, Q_L for even L <= 8 (rings to 16 sites, gauge-optimized M) never exceeds the Heisenberg value Q_2 = 4, which reproduces the known C(2) >= 4. Q_L oscillates and can be strongly negative with DM (the twist L arctan(D/J) moves the ring ground state through flux sectors) and with fields (sector switching), and decays rapidly for the random bonds, which are gapped. Small sizes only; this is a consistency check, not evidence about large L. [Numerical, `twist_check.py`] The twist lemma of DRAFT_C129 Step 3, E_L^(n)(Phi) - min_Phi E_L^(n) <= J pi^2/(4L), holds with largest ratio 0.671 over L = 4 to 8, all sectors n <= L/2, Delta in {-0.9, 0, 0.5, 1, 2} and 121 twists.

## 9. What a proof would need that the literature does not supply

[Assessment] Every general tool above is either local in space at fixed beta (Sections 2, 3), perturbative around free or classical points (Sections 1, 7), or exact on integrable or free classes (Sections 5, 8). The target needs a scale-L comparison that is uniform in the crossover, which the literature does not attempt for interacting chains. The nearest rigorous technology to that statement is constructive RG, which controls all scales but only in a perturbative ball. For d = 2, the parameter space is eight-dimensional and the hard part is concentrated on non-integrable critical surfaces and a few multicritical points, which makes C(2) the natural first target, as Jonah suggested.
