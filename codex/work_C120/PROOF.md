# C120. Dimension-bounded divergences and the reconnection ratio

2026-10-29. Natural logarithms. Every theorem below is finite dimensional unless an infrared assumption is stated. The numerical tests do not supply a general lattice theorem.

## 1. Setting and conclusion

[Assumed] Use the gauge-optimized bond scale M and vacuum-compatible lengths in the background specification. On the common Hilbert space of two L-rings, set

    H0 = H_L tensor I + I tensor H_L,
    H1 = H0 + V,
    X = exp(-beta H1),       Y = exp(-beta H0),
    Z1 = Tr X = Z(2L,beta),  Z0 = Tr Y = Z(L,beta)^2,
    rho = X/Z1,             sigma = Y/Z0,
    R = log Z0 - log Z1.

V removes two closing bonds and inserts two crossed bonds. In an optimized representative its norm is at most 4M. No continuous replica symmetry is assumed.

[Proved, inherited from C116/C118] The identities

    R = beta Tr(rho V) + D(rho || sigma),
    R = beta Tr(sigma V) - D(sigma || rho)

and the bound |R| <= 4 beta M hold. The proposed all-temperature bound forces

    L [E0(H1)-E0(H0)] <= k M f(d).

Under the vacuum Casimir expansion, this implies A <= (4k/pi) M f(d). These settled results are used without a new derivation.

[Open] None of the replacements investigated below proves a universal K(d), k, f(d). No counterexample to that target, or to its consequence for A, is established. What is proved is a set of exact replacement identities, explicit failures of the required dimension bounds, and the remaining normalization estimate in each identity.

## 2. Which quantities are dimension bounded

[Proved] For faithful states,

    Dmax(rho || sigma) = log ||sigma^(-1/2) rho sigma^(-1/2)||,
    D0(rho || sigma) = -log Tr(P_rho sigma),
    D_alpha(rho || sigma) = log Tr(rho^alpha sigma^(1-alpha))/(alpha-1),
    Dtilde_alpha(rho || sigma)
      = log Tr[(sigma^((1-alpha)/(2alpha)) rho
                 sigma^((1-alpha)/(2alpha)))^alpha]/(alpha-1).

Here 0 < alpha < 1 and P_rho is the support projector. D0 is the support definition of min-relative entropy. Some conventions instead call -2 log F a min-relative entropy. Those are different quantities. F here always denotes root fidelity, ||sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma)||_1. These conventions follow the primary definitions in [Datta](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.2770) and [Muller-Lennert et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.3142v2).

[Proved counterexample] On a qubit let

    epsilon = 1/(1+exp(t)),
    rho = diag(1-epsilon, epsilon),
    sigma = diag(epsilon, 1-epsilon),       t > 0.

Both states are faithful. Direct substitution gives

    Dmax = t,
    D0 = 0,
    Tr rho^alpha sigma^(1-alpha)
      = (1-epsilon)^alpha epsilon^(1-alpha)
         + epsilon^alpha (1-epsilon)^(1-alpha),
    D_alpha = Dtilde_alpha
      = [min(alpha,1-alpha)/(1-alpha)] t + O(1),
    F = 2 sqrt(epsilon(1-epsilon)) = sech(t/2),
    T = ||rho-sigma||_1/2 = tanh(t/2).

The asymptotic expression follows by factoring out the larger of the two exponential terms. Every fixed order alpha in (0,1) is unbounded with t, as are Dmax and -2 log F. The dimension is two throughout. These states are Gibbs states of opposite one-qubit Hamiltonians of fixed spectral width, with inverse temperature t. Thus faithfulness and bounded local energy width do not repair the dimension premise.

[Proved support distinction] With rho = |0><0| and sigma = diag(epsilon,1-epsilon), D0 = -log epsilon, also unbounded in dimension two. For every finite beta in the ring problem, both Gibbs states have full support and D0 = 0. Sending alpha to zero at fixed beta therefore removes the information in D0. Sending beta to infinity first can change the support. The two limits cannot be exchanged without justification.

[Proved] Entropies relative to the maximally mixed state are a different case. For dimension q, Dmax(rho || I/q) <= log q. To replace I/q by a prescribed faithful state gamma, the comparison costs

    Dmax(I/q || gamma) = -log q - log lambda_min(gamma).

This grows for a cold Gibbs state. Choosing a seam of q = d^w does not remove this cost.

[Proved scope] The qubit constructions disprove bounds based on dimension alone. They do not exclude a stronger theorem restricted to the particular Gibbs pairs generated by ring reconnection. Such a theorem would require additional structure beyond the listed dimensions. The direct seam-capacity ceilings are also tested on the prescribed ring pairs below.

## 3. Max-relative entropy and the exact order interval

[Proved] Define the positive operator

    B = Y^(-1/2) X Y^(-1/2),
    bminus = lambda_min(B),    bplus = lambda_max(B).

Normalization gives sigma^(-1/2)rho sigma^(-1/2) = exp(R) B. Hence

    Dmax(rho || sigma) = R + log bplus,
    Dmax(sigma || rho) = -R - log bminus,
    R = Dmax(rho || sigma) - log bplus.                         (1)

For the reverse formula, the generalized eigenvalues for (Y,X) are reciprocals of those for (X,Y). No commutation is required. Alternatively use the equivalence of X <= bplus Y and Y >= X/bplus.

[Proved] Taking traces of bminus Y <= X <= bplus Y gives

    -log bplus <= R <= -log bminus.                            (2)

The upper bound in (2) requires a lower bound on an unnormalized relative eigenvalue, not just a bound on the dimension of B. B acts on the full ring Hilbert space, despite V having four-site support. Exponentiation with H0 spreads that support in imaginary time. The assertion that exp(-beta H1) is ordered by exp(-beta H0) solely from H1-H0 in operator order would incorrectly use operator monotonicity of the exponential.

[Proved] The legitimate substitution in the C118 identity is

    R <= beta Tr(rho V) + Dmax(rho || sigma).                  (3)

Indeed rho <= exp(Dmax) sigma implies log rho <= Dmax I + log sigma by operator monotonicity of log. Taking the rho expectation proves D <= Dmax. Equation (3) is valid but Dmax has no dimension-only upper bound.

[Numerical] At beta = 4, Dmax is 10.926100 on XX and 15.654739 on Heisenberg, with L = 4. At beta = 2 it is 15.017146 on SU(3), with L = 3. These exceed the generous four-site capacities 4 log 2 = 2.772589 and 4 log 3 = 4.394449. The exact Umegaki values also provide stable lower bounds on Dmax when direct inverse Gibbs powers become poorly conditioned. No large-beta Dmax value obtained from numerical rank truncation is used.

[Proved additional ring-pair criterion] If H0 and H1 have distinct unique ground states, the H1 ground state has expectation of H0 strictly above E0. The Gibbs logarithm gives

    D(rho||sigma) = -S(rho)+beta Tr(rho H0)+log Z0
      = beta[<psi1|H0|psi1>-E0]+o(1).

The positive coefficient and Dmax >= D prove that Dmax is unbounded with beta even on this fixed Hilbert space. Thus distinct unique ground states already prevent any temperature-independent Dmax ceiling for that pair, without varying its dimension.

## 4. Conditional min-entropy gives an exact identity with a new normalization

[Proved] Let C be any chosen region, including a fixed seam region of w sites, with q = dim C = d^w, and D its complement. For a nonnegative, not necessarily normalized operator W define

    mu(W) = inf {Tr Omega_D : Omega_D >= 0,
                              W <= I_C tensor Omega_D}.

For normalized omega, Hmin(C|D)_omega = -log mu(omega). Homogeneity gives mu(aW) = a mu(W) for a >= 0.

[Proved] The bounds

    Tr W/q <= mu(W) <= q Tr W                                 (4)

hold for every W. For the lower bound take the trace of the defining constraint. For the upper bound use a basis of C and its q diagonal pinching projectors P_i. Cauchy-Schwarz for the q vectors W^(1/2)P_i v gives

    <v,Wv> <= q sum_i <v,P_i W P_i v>.

Thus W <= q sum_i P_i W P_i. Write W_ii for its diagonal blocks and W_D = sum_i W_ii. Since each W_ii <= W_D,

    W <= q I_C tensor W_D.

Choosing Omega_D = q W_D proves the upper bound in (4). Consequently

    -log q <= Hmin(C|D)_omega <= log q.

This is a true dimension bound, obtained by optimizing the reference operator on D.

[Proved exact replacement] Apply homogeneity separately to X and Y:

    log Z1 = log mu(X) + Hmin(C|D)_rho,
    log Z0 = log mu(Y) + Hmin(C|D)_sigma.

Subtracting gives

    R = Hmin(C|D)_sigma - Hmin(C|D)_rho
         + log[mu(Y)/mu(X)],                                  (5)
    R <= 2 log q + log[mu(Y)/mu(X)].                           (6)

The desired finite capacity is present. The remaining estimate would be a bound on log[mu(Y)/mu(X)] of order beta M/L, up to a dimension constant. Equation (4) instead bounds this term within 2 log q of R itself, so it gives no independent improvement.

[Proved zero-temperature obstruction to dropping that term] At fixed L let E_i and P_i be the ground energy and ground projector of H_i. Then

    exp(-beta H_i) = exp(-beta E_i)[P_i + O(exp(-beta gap_i))].

The functional mu is monotone, homogeneous and subadditive. Bounding the positive excited remainder above by its norm times I proves mu(P_i + remainder) -> mu(P_i), which is strictly positive by (4). Therefore

    log[mu(Y)/mu(X)]
       = beta(E1-E0) + log[mu(P0)/mu(P1)] + o(1).              (7)

All of the doubling-energy slope remains in this term. A fixed seam dimension cannot absorb it. This statement holds with degenerate ground spaces as well.

[Numerical scope] Finite-temperature tests check the constructive fixed-reference witness W <= q I_C tensor W_D for one site. They do not solve the conditional min-entropy optimization on a large complement. At beta = 0 the optimized value is log q. For a pure ground state with Schmidt coefficients s_j, the optimized value is

    Hmin(C|D) = -2 log sum_j s_j.                              (8)

[Proved derivation of (8)] The rank-one constraint |psi><psi| <= I tensor Omega is equivalent on the support to <psi|I tensor Omega^(-1)|psi> <= 1. This is Tr(rho_D Omega^(-1)) <= 1. Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy-Schwarz gives

    (Tr sqrt(rho_D))^2 <= Tr Omega * Tr(rho_D Omega^(-1)).

The lower bound on Tr Omega is attained by Omega = (Tr sqrt(rho_D)) sqrt(rho_D), with support restriction if needed. Thus mu = (sum_j s_j)^2, proving (8). In the three small-ring ground-state tests the one-site marginal is maximally mixed. Both split and joined Hmin values are -log d. Their difference is zero, while E1-E0 is positive.

## 5. Petz and sandwiched Renyi trace identities

[Proved] Define the unnormalized mixed traces

    Q_alpha = Tr(X^alpha Y^(1-alpha)),
    Qtilde_alpha
      = Tr[(Y^((1-alpha)/(2alpha)) X
             Y^((1-alpha)/(2alpha)))^alpha].

Both are positive. Scalar homogeneity gives, respectively,

    log Tr(rho^alpha sigma^(1-alpha))
        = log Q_alpha - alpha log Z1 - (1-alpha) log Z0,

and the identical normalization formula with Qtilde. Solving for R gives

    R = D_alpha(rho || sigma)
           + [log Q_alpha-log Z1]/(1-alpha),                 (9)
    R = Dtilde_alpha(rho || sigma)
           + [log Qtilde_alpha-log Z1]/(1-alpha).            (10)

There is an exact identity, but neither term on the right has the proposed dimension ceiling. The mixed trace is not a partition function of the reconnected Hamiltonian alone. In the Petz case it is Tr exp(-alpha beta H1) exp(-(1-alpha) beta H0). Replacing it by exp of the sum is an additional noncommuting inequality, not an identity.

[Proved direction check for Petz] In eigenbases of rho and sigma, log Tr(rho^alpha sigma^(1-alpha)) is a log-sum-exponential of affine functions of alpha, hence convex. Its value at alpha = 1 is zero and its derivative there is D(rho||sigma). The tangent inequality, divided by alpha-1 < 0, gives D_alpha <= D. Therefore

    beta Tr(rho V) + D_alpha(rho||sigma) <= R,
    R <= beta Tr(sigma V) - D_alpha(sigma||rho).              (11)

The forward replacement is a lower bound. The reverse replacement is an upper bound with less subtraction than the Umegaki identity. For sandwiched divergences, the published order monotonicity gives Dtilde_alpha <= D, so the same directional conclusion holds. The checks evaluate that ordering only where the matrix powers are numerically conditioned. See [Muller-Lennert et al., limits and order monotonicity](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.3142v2).

[Proved alpha-zero limit] For faithful X,Y, Q_0 = Tr Y = Z0 and D_0 = 0. Equation (9) becomes R = R. The support limit has retained no new inequality. In particular, a vanishing divergence does not imply a vanishing mixed-trace term.

[Proved low-temperature behavior] Suppose for illustration that both ground states are unique and have nonzero overlap c = |<psi1|psi0>|. At fixed L and fixed 0 < alpha < 1,

    Q_alpha = exp[-beta(alpha E1+(1-alpha)E0)] [c^2+o(1)],
    D_alpha(rho||sigma) -> -log(c^2)/(1-alpha),
    [log Q_alpha-log Z1]/(1-alpha)
         = beta(E1-E0) + log(c^2)/(1-alpha) + o(1).             (12)

For the sandwiched trace the pure-state limit gives

    Dtilde_alpha -> -alpha log(c^2)/(1-alpha).

Thus even when these divergences stay bounded as beta increases, their companion terms carry the full desired slope. The full-rank qubit example in Section 2 demonstrates that boundedness is not a general theorem. No bound on c follows from the seam dimension.

[Proved failure of the reverse fixed-order bound to recover 1/L on XX] In the unique split XX ground state every one-site marginal is I/2. A crossed bond therefore has zero expectation. Each original closing bond has expectation E_L/L by translation invariance. Hence

    lim_(beta -> infinity) Tr(sigma V) = -2 E_L/L
                                     = 2 csc(pi/L)/L.

The two XX ground states have nonzero overlap. An alternating on-site rotation makes every hopping matrix element nonpositive on both even graphs. The hopping graph is connected in each fixed-number sector, so Perron-Frobenius gives a strictly positive joined ground vector. The split ground vector is nonnegative with nonempty support in that sector. Their overlap is positive. The reverse fixed-order Petz or sandwiched divergence therefore stays finite at fixed L. Dividing the reverse bound in (11) by beta therefore gives the ceiling 2 csc(pi/L)/L, which tends to 2/pi. The actual doubling defect in (20) is asymptotic to pi/(4L). Thus this bound loses a factor proportional to L even on XX. Keeping a fixed-order reverse divergence, without bounding it by dimension at all, does not repair the gluing scale. Choosing alpha to approach one with beta may recover the Umegaki cancellation, but then its uniform boundedness is lost and a new quantitative estimate is required.

## 6. Fidelity and trace distance

[Proved] Write G(X,Y) = ||sqrt(X)sqrt(Y)||_1. Homogeneity yields

    F(rho,sigma) = G(X,Y)/sqrt(Z1 Z0),
    R = 2 log[G(X,Y)/Z1] - 2 log F(rho,sigma).                (13)

This is (10) at alpha = 1/2. Root fidelity lies between zero and one, but its negative logarithm has no upper bound from dimension. In the nonorthogonal unique-ground-state case,

    F -> c,
    2 log[G(X,Y)/Z1] = beta(E1-E0)+2 log c+o(1).              (14)

The normalized overlap can remain far from zero while R grows without bound.

[Assumed published inequality, numerically checked] The Fuchs-van de Graaf inequalities in the root-fidelity convention are

    1-F <= T <= sqrt(1-F^2),       T = ||rho-sigma||_1/2.

The primary reference is [Fuchs and van de Graaf](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9712042). They imply

    -log(1-T^2) <= -2 log F <= -2 log(1-T).                   (15)

[Proved consequence] A constant ceiling T <= 1 does not bound the right side of (15). To use it quantitatively one needs T bounded away from one, and even such a bound leaves the mixed trace in (13). Substituting F >= 1-T in (13) gives the valid bound

    R <= 2 log[G(X,Y)/Z1] - 2 log(1-T),                      (16)

not R <= a universal constant. Scalar multiplication X -> aX leaves every normalized-state distinguishability measure unchanged and shifts R by -log a. This observation explains why trace normalization must occur somewhere in an exact identity. It is an algebraic obstruction to state-only identities, not a claim that arbitrary independent scalar shifts are allowed microscopic ring reconnections.

## 7. Positive maps and Hilbert's projective metric

[Proved] On the cone of positive matrices, Hilbert's projective metric is

    h(rho,sigma) = Dmax(rho||sigma) + Dmax(sigma||rho)
                = log(bplus/bminus).

The normalization R cancels. This metric is not bounded by dimension, as the qubit example gives h = 2t.

[Assumed published theorem] For a cone-preserving map Phi of finite projective diameter Delta(Phi), the Birkhoff-Hopf contraction coefficient is tanh(Delta(Phi)/4). This requires cone positivity, not complete positivity, and does not assume a dimension-only diameter bound. The statement and spectral consequence are [Reeb, Kastoryano and Wolf, Theorems 4 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.5170v2).

[Proved counterexample to quantum trace Cauchy-Schwarz from map positivity] On qubit matrices take

    Phi(W) = a U W U^dagger + (1-a) Tr(W) I/2,
    U = exp(-i theta Z/2),           0 < a < 1.

This map is completely positive and sends every nonzero positive W to a positive definite matrix. In the identity/Pauli basis its eigenvalues are 1, a, a exp(i theta), a exp(-i theta). The trace below is the superoperator trace on a four-dimensional vector space. Thus

    Tr Phi^n = 1 + a^n [1 + 2 cos(n theta)] > 0.

Fix m and choose theta = pi/(2m). Then

    Tr Phi^m = 1+a^m,
    Tr Phi^(2m) = 1-a^(2m),
    log[(Tr Phi^m)^2/Tr Phi^(2m)]
        = log[(1+a^m)/(1-a^m)] -> infinity as a -> 1.          (17)

In particular, the ceiling log 4 fails even for strictly positive, completely positive maps. This refutes the proposed abstract trace inequality from cone positivity. It is not a construction of a local quantum chain violating the target.

[Proved diameter and contraction] The image of the qubit state space is the Bloch ball of radius a, rotated by U. For any two image states, their maximal relative eigenvalue is at most (1+a)/(1-a). Antipodal pure input states attain this in both directions. Consequently

    Delta(Phi) = 2 log[(1+a)/(1-a)],
    tanh(Delta(Phi)/4) = a.

Every a < 1 gives strict contraction, but its coefficient tends to one at fixed dimension. Strict contraction by itself provides no uniform budget for (17).

[Proved conditional spectral estimate] Suppose a D-dimensional transfer matrix has one eigenvalue lambda0 > 0 and all other eigenvalues satisfy |lambda_j/lambda0| <= eta < 1. Algebraic multiplicities are counted. Tr T^m = lambda0^m(1+s_m), with |s_m| <= (D-1)eta^m, even for Jordan blocks since nilpotent parts have zero trace. If the physical traces are positive and (D-1)eta^(2m) < 1, then

    log[(Tr T^m)^2/Tr T^(2m)]
      <= 2 log[1+(D-1)eta^m] - log[1-(D-1)eta^(2m)].           (18)

A Birkhoff theorem can supply such an eta under its hypotheses. It does not control D, eta, or their combination in the Trotter limit. Equation (18) makes those additional requirements explicit.

[Proved distinction between different positivity statements] If G = exp(-delta h), its spatial realignment S_(a,c),(b,d) = G_(a,b),(c,d) is the supermatrix of a completely positive map with Choi matrix G. A negative eigenvalue of S therefore does not disprove positivity as a map. Conversely, (17) shows why complete positivity of this map does not prove the classical trace inequality. Entrywise nonnegativity in a fixed basis, positive semidefiniteness as a matrix, and preservation of a matrix cone are three distinct properties. Gluing an entire time history also changes the transfer space. No positivity property of one gate establishes a temperature-independent history dimension.

## 8. Actual Trotter histories and an explicit sign penalty

[Proved construction] For a p-layer product formula with n repetitions, assign a spin history a = (a_0,...,a_(pn-1)) to each spatial column, cyclic in time. The layer color of bond i is i modulo p. A bond of color c contributes G_(a_(t+1),b_(t+1);a_t,b_t) at t modulo p = c. For times when the left site participates in neither the outgoing color c nor the incoming color c-1, insert delta_(a_(t+1),a_t). Multiplying these factors defines W_c(a,b).

All intermediate spins are summed by matrix multiplication. Therefore, with

    T_n = W_0 W_1 ... W_(p-1),      D_n = d^(pn),
    Z_N^(n) = Tr T_n^(N/p),

the spatial contraction is identical to the temporal product formula. Use p = 2 for even XX/Heisenberg rings and p = 3 for SU(3) lengths divisible by three, including the vacuum-compatible pair 3 and 6. As n -> infinity the temporal formula converges to exp(-beta H_N). A fixed small n is not an exact quantum partition function.

[Proved real-matrix inequality] Let B = T_n^(L/p), assume the two traces are positive, and define

    S = sum_i B_ii^2,
    Nminus = sum_(i != j) max(0,-B_ij B_ji).

Diagonal Cauchy-Schwarz and expansion of the trace give

    (Tr B)^2 <= D_n S,
    Tr B^2 = S + sum_(i != j) B_ij B_ji >= S-Nminus.

It follows that

    R_L^(n) <= log D_n + log[1+Nminus/Tr B^2].                (19)

For complex B, a basis-independent weaker version is

    |Tr B|^2 <= D_n Tr(B^dagger B).

When Tr B^2 is real and positive, its additional penalty is log[Tr(B^dagger B)/Tr B^2]. These formulas expose a cancellation cost without assuming positivity of the transfer matrix.

[Proved limitation] Even setting Nminus = 0 leaves log D_n = pn log d, which diverges in the Trotter limit. A bound on signs of order beta M/L would therefore not complete this proof. A further effective-dimension or spectral-distribution bound at spatial separation L is necessary. C116 already rules out replacing D_n by d^2 in an exact representation with the correct pressure.

[Numerical] All sampled XX and Heisenberg blocked transfer matrices have nonnegative entries and zero penalty in (19). Nonetheless their ratios exceed log(d^2) at depth n = 2 or 3 and beta = 8. SU(3) has negative entries and a nonzero pair penalty at n = 2, for example 0.081873 at beta = 2 and 0.508770 at beta = 8. The tested Trotter depths do not establish the asymptotics of this penalty in n, L or beta. In particular no order-beta M/L estimate is claimed. The zero-penalty XX/Heisenberg tests isolate the history-dimension obstruction independently of signs.

## 9. Free-fermion checks and scope of the conclusions

[Proved computational formulas] The full finite-temperature XX trace uses both Jordan-Wigner parity sectors, as established in C118. For stable large-L evaluation, subtract each grid's filled-sea energy before performing the even/odd excitation recursion. On even lengths the two sea energies are -csc(pi/L) and -cot(pi/L), whose difference is tan(pi/(2L)). Thus log Z_L = -beta E_L + log W_L with E_L = -csc(pi/L). Evaluate the doubling defect without subtracting large reciprocals:

    E_(2L)-2E_L
      = 2 sin^2(pi/(4L)) / [sin(pi/(2L)) cos(pi/(2L))].         (20)

Then R = beta(E_(2L)-2E_L)+2 log W_L-log W_(2L). This avoids cancellation of bulk terms of order beta L. The trace remains a full spin trace, not a ground-state approximation.

[Proved ground-overlap formula] At zero temperature, build the occupied one-particle plane waves for the joined 2L-ring and the two separate L-rings in their physical parity sectors. Denote the two orthonormal occupied-mode matrices by U1 and U0, both of size 2L by L. Their Slater overlap is

    c = |det(U1^dagger U0)|.

The ordered Fock basis agrees with the spin tensor-product basis up to an irrelevant overall phase for these fixed occupation sectors. This determinant therefore gives the physical ground-state fidelity. For distinct pure states with c > 0,

    T = sqrt(1-c^2),
    D0 = -log c^2,
    D_alpha = -log c^2/(1-alpha),
    Dtilde_alpha = -alpha log c^2/(1-alpha),
    Dmax = infinity.

The last statement follows from the unequal one-dimensional supports. A finite-temperature support divergence is still zero, not this pure-state value. For a fixed small region C the eigenvalues nu_j of its restricted correlation matrix give the pure-state conditional min-entropy

    Hmin(C|D) = -2 sum_j log[sqrt(nu_j)+sqrt(1-nu_j)].           (21)

[Numerical] Ground determinants through L = 512 agree with the small-ring ED fidelity at L = 4 to below 1e-12. At L = 512, c = 0.432262 and D0 = 1.677448. Full finite-temperature ratios extend to L = 8192 with paired length 16384. At beta/L = 64 the ratio is 50.265484, far above any of the tested fixed seam capacities, while satisfying the established C118 bound. No finite-temperature large-ring fidelity or conditional min-entropy optimization is claimed. The large-ring tests evaluate the scalar consequences of those capacity replacements, and the state distinguishability measures at the zero-temperature endpoint via the determinant formulas.

[Open] A successful estimate still needs one of the following genuinely new inputs: control of the unnormalized conditional-min-entropy functional in (6), control of a mixed trace in (9), (10) or (13), or a bound on the effective spatial transfer dimension together with its cancellation cost. Each must supply beta M/L rather than beta M. The present results exclude the direct dimension-only replacements and the abstract positive-map Cauchy-Schwarz premise. They do not exclude another use of these tools with additional microscopic information.
