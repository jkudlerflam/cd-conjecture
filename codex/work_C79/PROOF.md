# C79. Paying coefficient moments with unused scale gains

## 1. Statement and boundaries

[proved below, source-free theorem] A fixed Gevrey cutoff gives a general-tree alternative to the bounded-edge-load construction. Moment orders on an original endpoint edge or a BBF tree line need not be bounded individually. Their full constants, their routing sum, and the localization scale factors nevertheless obey C60 (17), with a constant exponential in the total initial field number and independent of infrared depth. The proof permits arbitrary branching, arbitrary even endpoint degree, and singular retarded coefficient measures with the exponential terminal-tree norm. It retains the BBF determinants.

[assumed, analytic inputs] Use normalized shell covariances, at fixed infrared cutoff and after removing the volume and temperature regulators, with

    integral |X|^b |partial^a g_s(X)| dX
        <= A^(b+1) (b!)^2 E_s^(a-b-1),
    a <= 4, b >= 0, E_s=2^s,                              (1)

and the usual fixed-order derivative Gram bounds. The lattice version is uniform in real offsets. Section 8 derives (1) for a fixed Gevrey-order-two cutoff and the analytic free lattice dispersion, including the rational normalized covariances. This is a choice of cutoff, not an assumption that every smooth cutoff has quantitatively controlled derivatives of all orders.

[assumed, endpoint input] A degree-p initial coefficient is a translation-invariant measure, with the degree-factorial convention of C61, satisfying

    H^p sup_anchor integral exp[T(P)] d|K_p|
        <= A epsilon^max(2,p/2-1).                       (2)

T is the terminal minimum-spanning-tree length in the fixed positive spacetime metric. Constants below can depend on that metric. Local forward and local constant quadratic endpoints have their separately prescribed amplitudes. No derivative is taken of an endpoint measure.

[definition, quadratic convention] Sections 2-8 first treat derivative-free interaction endpoints, apart from localization derivatives generated by the remainders. Both localized quadratic derivative coordinates may be absorbed into the covariance using the fixed-cutoff rational formula in MARKED_COVARIANCE, Section 1. Initial nonlocal quadratic remainders remain among the measures in (2). Section 9 also proves the dimensional version with explicit engineering-derivative endpoints. Engineering derivatives are not silently counted a second time.

[proved below, scope of (17)] In this convention the theorem proves the general source-free localization estimate, including its hidden moment constants. It does not prove C61's N-comparison or N-source. In particular, corrected Lemma N as a whole is not concluded. Those statements require additional signed reference identities and Gaussian-subtracted source estimates. No branching obstruction to the endpoint norm is exhibited.

[definition, root norm] This is the derivative-decorated estimate in C60 (17). After pinning a common anchor, each retained root field is tested with derivatives of order a<=2 bounded by C E_h^a. It is not an untested kernel total-variation estimate that vanishes with an independently chosen root scale. All field derivatives use deviation momenta in the fixed branch charts. Taylor subtraction is in the resonant infrared sectors of C61, where the conservation hyperplane passes through zero.

[definition] The tree is rooted toward the infrared. A nontrivial vertex v has scale h_v, and a child has larger scale. Initial endpoints are at zero; running local endpoints have their prescribed scales. Remove repetitions with no intervening contraction. For a remainder vertex put

    m_v=2 for p_v=2, m_v=1 for p_v=4, m_v=0 otherwise.

A local running endpoint has no remainder demand. Its edge is not assigned an irrelevant-endpoint gain. Write ell_v=h_v-h_parent(v). The retained root fields are tested at scale h below the root vertex.

[proved, finite-tree legitimacy] One may first truncate each endpoint measure at terminal-tree length R, work at fixed infrared cutoff, and use compactly supported smooth external test fields. All identities below then involve finite-order operations on a finite tree. The shell bounds give integrable majorants for every finite polynomial moment. The final bound is independent of R, so total-variation domination removes the endpoint truncations. The bound is also independent of the number of infrared scales. No all-order endpoint derivative assumption is used.

## 2. An exact induction that leaves all moments visible

[proved, representation invariant] At every vertex the coefficient can be represented as a finite sum of integrals with the following factors.

* Original endpoint measures and local endpoint amplitudes.
* The chosen BBF tree covariances and residual determinants, with derivatives attached to original field labels.
* One scalar displacement component for every localization derivative that has been created.
* External fields, each evaluated at a convex affine combination of original terminal positions in its own current cluster, and with at most two localization derivatives on its original label.

The scalar coefficients of the affine maps depend on Taylor parameters, not on the terminal coordinates. A displacement created at v joins two such convex combinations inside v. All previously created displacement factors remain inside the coefficient. They are not replaced by independent Taylor kernels whose derivatives would have to be estimated afresh.

Proof. The assertion holds for the initial measures before localization. At a merge, contract the selected fields. An inherited momentum monomial on surviving field labels factors outside the Gaussian expectation. A factor on a contracted label differentiates its selected covariance or its determinant row or column. Each field contracts only once. The remaining coefficient is still an integral of the displayed kind.

Let P_a be the monomial on the surviving fields, with total localization degree a, and let F be the complete remaining coefficient, including all descendant moments. Homogeneity on the momentum-conservation hyperplane gives

    R_m(P_a F)=P_a R_r F,       r=max(0,m-a).              (3)

Choose one external field as pivot and use the other external deviation momenta as independent variables q. For r>0, use the single radial formula

    R_r F(q)=sum_(|gamma|=r) (r/gamma!) q^gamma
          integral_0^1 (1-t)^(r-1) D^gamma F(tq) dt.       (4)

There are at most six scalar independent components. Here r<=2. In the existing position representation of F, differentiation in q multiplies by the corresponding component of Y_f-Y_pivot. Evaluation at tq replaces each external position by

    Y_f -> Y_pivot+t(Y_f-Y_pivot).                        (5)

It leaves all internal covariance arguments and all existing coefficient weights untouched. Equations (4)-(5) are Fourier identities for the entire coefficient, before any estimate. In particular, differentiating F does not mean opening and then differentiating each previously chosen divided-difference coefficient.

If r>0, the total exposed degree after (4) is a+r=m<=2. If r=0, no field degree changes. Thus each original field has at most two derivatives. All positions in (5) remain convex combinations of terminal positions in v. The new displacement factors have the stated form. This closes the induction.

[proved, Gaussian loads] A selected tree covariance has at most four derivatives, two from each endpoint. Each residual determinant row or column has at most two. Multilinearity attaches these derivatives to its Gram vectors; it does not expand the determinant into pairings. There can be many coefficient moments, but these moments have not been put on determinant entries.

[proved, numerical coefficient mass before routing] The Taylor coefficient mass including integration is d^r/r!, with d<=6 and r<=2. Every created derivative stays on its original field until that field contracts or reaches the root. The construction never consumes a derivative after recording it. Since each original field carries at most two, the total number M of created displacement factors obeys M<=2P, where P=sum_i p_i. Every operation with r>0 creates at least one of these derivatives, and an operation with r=0 introduces no Taylor coefficient. Thus the total numerical coefficient mass before routing is C^P, even if the tree is left with arbitrarily many trivial unary scale vertices. Removing those vertices changes neither the representation nor the scale spans.

[proved, product-rule completeness] In an ancestor-first transposition, a child derivative can act on an ancestor Taylor field or consume an ancestor coordinate weight. Those terms cannot be dropped. Here Gaussian contraction is performed before the ancestor remainder is expanded. Surviving child momenta are retained in P_a, and the exact identity (3) combines their full contribution before absolute values. The cut record is constructed directly for this new representation. It is not obtained by erasing consumed paths from a previously expanded representation. The equality in (3), rather than an independence assumption about exterior fields, justifies this order of operations.

[proved, why this avoids the C72 obstruction] This construction does not bound D^b Q_s K while requiring its old surviving factor to remain explicit. It keeps the whole coefficient F in (3)-(4), and then retains every resulting coordinate factor. Its moment orders are allowed to grow. The estimate of that growth is Sections 5-6, rather than a claimed fixed two-moment closure.

## 3. Routing through actual shifted arguments

[definition] Equip each initial endpoint with a deterministic minimum spanning tree of its original terminal positions. Degenerate choices can be resolved measurably; zero-length edges cause no problem. Adjoin every selected BBF tree edge at every merger. An edge of the first kind has its original coordinate displacement and source scale zero. A BBF edge has its actual interpolated covariance argument X_e and source scale t_e equal to the contraction scale. Denote this collection by E. Local endpoints need no terminal edges. Its size N satisfies N<=P+n, with an inessential fixed enlargement if conversion legs are retained.

[proved, geometric routing lemma] Let x and y be any two convex combinations of terminal positions inside v. At fixed Taylor parameters,

    x-y=sum_(e in E_v) theta_e X_e,       |theta_e|<=1,    (6)

where E_v contains only edges in the subtree of v. The coefficients can be chosen independently of the coordinate values once endpoint-tree topologies and interpolation parameters are fixed. In particular t_e>=h_v for every eligible edge.

Proof at an endpoint. For two terminals, use their oriented path in the chosen tree; each edge occurs at most once. For two convex combinations, write their difference as the weighted sum of all terminal-pair differences, with weights u_i v_j, u_i,v_j>=0 and sum u_i=sum v_j=1. The sum of these weights is one. Each resulting edge coefficient therefore has absolute value at most one.

Inductive step. First take two terminals in distinct children of v. Follow the unique path between their children in the BBF child tree. At each connection add its actual line argument, with the appropriate sign. Within each visited child, connect the entering and exiting positions; these can be convex combinations of its terminals. Use the induction hypothesis for that difference. A child occurs at most once on the path. The edge sets of different children are disjoint. Consequently each edge coefficient still has absolute value at most one. If both terminals lie in the same child, only that child is needed. Finally average over terminal pairs to treat arbitrary convex combinations, as at an endpoint. This proves (6).

[proved, offset control] Formula (6) includes offsets produced by relocating a covariance endpoint. For example x-y=X+t(x-p) is obtained by one passage through the child containing x and p. The second term is routed inside that child. No bound identifies x-y with X alone, and no interpolation parameter is inverted.

[proved, exact routing expansion] Apply (6) separately to every displacement factor generated by (4). A routing A selects one eligible edge e(j) for each moment j. Its scalar coefficient is a product of theta components, of absolute value at most one. Several moments may choose the same edge. If b_e is their number, then sum_e b_e=M. This is an exact finite expansion, not only a diameter estimate. Coordinate-direction and metric-conversion constants cost C^M.

## 4. The cut record before moments are estimated

[definition] Each derivative created in (4) has its original field label, birth vertex v(j), birth scale u_j=h_v(j), and sink scale s_j. The sink is its field's first contraction, or h for a retained root field. Its paired coordinate factor is routed to e(j), of scale t_e(j)>=u_j. The full path splits into

    source edge to birth, followed by birth to sink.       (7)

Define D(A)=sum_j(t_e(j)-u_j)>=0. The scale exponent is

    sum_j(s_j-t_e(j))
        =sum_j(s_j-u_j)-D(A).                            (8)

[proved, birth-to-sink coverage] Let G_w be the number of birth-to-sink paths crossing a remainder edge w. Then G_w is the exposed localization degree of the external fields of w, immediately after its remainder. Consequently

    G_w>=m_w,
    sum_j(s_j-u_j)=-sum_w G_w ell_w
                   <=-sum_w m_w ell_w.                  (9)

The first statement follows because a derivative follows its original field until that field contracts. Equation (3) gives external degree a+max(0,m_w-a)>=m_w. Concatenating the scale intervals along each field path gives the equality in (9), which is C61's cut identity. Multiple children do not alter this argument. Edges with no remainder have demand zero.

[proved, meaning of the surplus] The entire factor 2^(-D(A)) in (8) is additional to the required localization gain. It arises when a moment born at v is paid by a shorter-distance line deeper inside v. The proof below spends this factor, not any part of the required gain in (9).

## 5. The hidden moment constants

[proved, endpoint payment at arbitrary load] On an endpoint tree with nonnegative metric edge lengths rho_e,

    product_e rho_e^b_e <= product_e b_e! exp(sum_e rho_e). (10)

This follows from rho^b/b!<=exp(rho) on each edge. Integrating against the original measure pays the exponential by (2). The remaining cost is product b_e!, without any dependence on p in the exponent of the terminal-tree length. Higher-degree retarded endpoints are covered by this same formula.

[proved, line payment at arbitrary load] Equation (1) pays a tree-line factor X_e^b_e partial^a g_t(X_e), relative to its unweighted line dimension, by

    A^(b_e+1) (b_e!)^2 2^[t(a-b_e)].                     (11)

The derivative Gram bound supplies 2^(s_j) for derivatives ending in a determinant. Root test derivatives supply 2^(s_j) at the retained root scale. Thus, after ordinary line dimensions and endpoint norms are factored out, the total extra moment constant is bounded by

    C^(N+M) product_e (b_e!)^2,                          (12)

and the scale factor is the left side of (8). Only selected tree lines receive coordinate moments. No position moment of a determinant entry has been used.

[proved, anchor integration] Every contracted field at a merge v has been relocated only inside its child w of v. With y_w its anchor, its position is y_w+rho, where rho depends on internal relative coordinates and parameters. Hence every actual selected line argument is y_w-y_w'+rho_e. For fixed internal variables the reduced child-tree incidence matrix has determinant of modulus one. Integrating the outer anchors first makes the complete sequence triangular. The lattice sums use unimodularity for the integer anchor differences and the uniform real-offset bound in (1). Singular endpoint measures are integrated by their pinned total variation. All routed powers in (11) are powers of these actual arguments.

[proved, load-surplus lemma] For a fixed edge e, all moments assigned to it were born at ancestor vertices of that edge. Those vertices have distinct integer scales and emit at most two moments each. If b=b_e, the smallest possible list of offsets t_e-u_j is

    0,0,1,1,2,2,...

truncated after b entries. Therefore

    sum_(j:e(j)=e)(t_e-u_j) >= q(b),
    q(b)=floor((b-1)^2/4) for b>=1, q(0)=0.              (13)

For b=2k the sum is k(k-1); for b=2k+1 it is k^2. This proves (13) for every depth. Multiple siblings cannot create extra births at the same scale for this edge, since only its ancestor chain can assign it a moment.

[proved, absorption of factorials] There is a fixed C_* such that

    (b!)^2 2^(-q(b)/2) <= C_*^b,       b>=0.             (14)

Indeed log(b!)<=b log b while q(b)>=b^2/4-b/2. The logarithm of the left side divided by b is bounded above and tends to minus infinity as b grows. Its supremum over positive integers is finite. Summing (13) over edges now gives

    product_e(b_e!)^2 2^(-D(A)/2) <= C_*^M.              (15)

A fixed factorial power other than two works by the same proof. No estimate of an unbounded moment by a fixed-order moment is being asserted.

## 6. Summing all routes without losing a field radius at each scale

[definition] Put rho=2^(-1/2), and for every birth vertex v define

    S_v=sum_(e in E_v) rho^(t_e-h_v).                    (16)

If r_v moments are born at v, r_v<=2. An empty edge set gives a zero displacement and its positive-order Taylor term vanishes.

[proved, route sum] After (15), the sum over the remaining routing factors is

    sum_A 2^(-D(A)/2)=product_v S_v^r_v.                 (17)

The equality allows all eligible edges independently and ignores theta coefficients of absolute value at most one, so it is an upper bound for the actual weighted expansion. For each fixed source edge e, its eligible birth vertices form an ancestor chain with distinct integer scales. Thus

    sum_v S_v
      =sum_e sum_(v ancestor of e) rho^(t_e-h_v)
      <= N/(1-rho).                                     (18)

Using r_v<=2 and log x<=x for x>0,

    product_v S_v^r_v <= exp[2N/(1-rho)].                 (19)

For S_v<1 its logarithm is negative, which only improves this upper bound. This estimate covers high arity as well as deep chains. It does not replace the number of descendants by a uniform constant.

[proved, general moment-majorant theorem] Combine (8)-(9), (12), (15), and (19). Since N+M=O(P+n),

    sum_A |c_A| product_e(b_e!)^2
           2^[sum_j(s_j-t_e(j))]
      <= C^(P+n) product_w 2^(-m_w ell_w).              (20)

Here c_A includes the bounded Taylor coefficients, affine-routing coefficients, and the fixed exponential moment constants, but not the displayed factorials. Absorbing those factorials into the absolute coefficients gives C60 (17) in the stated derivative-free endpoint convention. In particular it controls the coefficient moments hidden in every descendant kernel, rather than only the derivatives of the current kernel.

[proved, independence of branching and endpoint degree] The proof used only two facts about the local Taylor operation: it emits at most two derivatives, and its output external degree covers its remainder demand. Endpoint degree enters only N and P. The geometric induction admits any child tree. The load-surplus lemma uses the unique ancestor chain of a selected source edge, irrespective of the number of its siblings. Therefore neither several quartic children nor higher-degree retarded endpoints create an exception to (20).

## 7. Determinants, field labels, and marks

[proved, anchored-tree count] At a merger with s>=2 children and q_i fields contracted from child i, put Q=sum q_i. For fixed child-tree degrees d_i, the abstract tree count is (s-2)!/product(d_i-1)!, and incident edges can be assigned at most product(q_i)_(d_i) distinct field labels. Including 1/s! and summing degrees gives

    [product q_i/(s(s-1))] binom(Q-s,s-2) <= 4^Q.         (21)

This is the coefficient extraction from product q_i(1+z)^(q_i-1). If some q_i=0 there is no connected merger. Orientation constants are exponential in Q. Each original field contracts once, so sum_v Q_v<=P. Residual determinants cost C^Q by Gram, not Q! by a Wick expansion. This proves an overall C^P count for these choices.

[proved, consequences conditional on the other supplied RG inputs] The source-free dimensional factor following (20) is the same one as in C60,

    d_R(p)=p/2-2+m(p),
    d_R(2)=d_R(4)=1, d_R(p)>=p/6.                        (22)

The surplus spent in Sections 5-6 is additional to (22). Thus the previously stated field-label summation, normalization-ratio reserve, and short-memory implications retain their full dimensional budgets. They are not weakened to a smaller localization order.

[proved conditional on marked Gevrey symbol bounds] One or two specified history marks have the same arbitrary-moment estimate (1) multiplied by their supplied scale/history factors. Marking a residual determinant uses the fixed cofactor/minor bounds from MARKED_COVARIANCE. Its polynomial loss is attached only to the specified marks and can be bounded by C^P. The geometric and load-surplus arguments are unchanged. This establishes the moment part of the corresponding marked source-free estimate; it does not prove a reference cancellation or a source theorem.

## 8. Why a permitted cutoff supplies the moment hypothesis

[definition] Choose a fixed compact Gevrey-order-two bump on the real momentum chart, with derivative bounds C^(k+1)(k!)^2. It can be constructed from the function exp(-1/x) for x>0, extended by zero for x<=0, then translated, multiplied and normalized to form a plateau and a compact support. Choose the plateau/support radii so chi_j=1 on supp chi_(j-1). The cutoff does not depend on running velocities.

[proved, quantitative bump bound] For x>0, use a complex disk of radius c x around x, with c small and fixed. Throughout that disk Re(1/z)>=c'/x. Cauchy's estimate gives

    |d^k exp(-1/x)/dx^k| <= k! (c x)^(-k) exp(-c'/x).

Maximizing x^(-k)exp(-c'/x) gives at most C^k k!, hence C^k(k!)^2. The function and all derivatives approach zero at the left boundary. Finite products preserve the bound by Leibniz's formula. Reciprocals of functions bounded away from zero preserve it by differentiating f(1/f)=1 inductively. To see the quantitative step in one dimension, assume |f^(k)|<=A B^k(k!)^2 and |f|>=delta. In the recurrence for the nth derivative of 1/f, division by C^n(n!)^2 leaves a sum bounded by

    (A/delta) sum_(k=1)^n (B/C)^k / binom(n,k).

Choose C>B sufficiently large that the geometric upper bound for this sum is at most one. The zeroth reciprocal norm supplies the fixed prefactor. This closes the induction. Multi-indices introduce only fixed-dimensional exponential constants. The plateau normalization therefore has the stated Gevrey bound. Tensor-product cutoffs on the two real components suffice.

[proved, shell symbols] On the rescaled shell p=E_s k, the analytic lattice dispersion has uniformly bounded analytic derivatives on the fixed compact annulus after division by E_s. Its denominator is bounded away from zero there on the prescribed small history domain. Multiplication by the fixed Gevrey cutoff and inversion of this denominator give, uniformly in s,

    |D_p^beta ghat_s(p)|
       <= C^(|beta|+1)(|beta|!)^2 E_s^(-1-|beta|).        (23)

The rational absorption factors in MARKED_COVARIANCE obey the same estimate. Their finitely many history derivatives preserve the Gevrey order and carry the scale factors already displayed in that note. The uniformly small rescaled curvature term and its analytic derivatives are covered by the same constants.

[proved, position moments] For a<=4, multiplication of the symbol by p^a preserves (23), with an extra E_s^a and fixed polynomial factors in |beta| that can be absorbed in C^(|beta|+1). Fourier integration by parts N times in coordinate directions gives

    |partial^a g_s(X)|
      <= C^(N+1)(N!)^2 E_s^(1+a)
                              (1+E_s|X|)^(-N).

Take N=b+4, integrate in two dimensions, and rescale X. Since ((b+4)!)^2 <= C^(b+1)(b!)^2, the result is (1). The same decay bound summed on the spatial lattice is uniform in a real offset, with a fixed enlargement of the constant. No analyticity of the compact cutoff is asserted.

[proved, conversion-leg qualification] Fixed band-conversion legs constructed with this cutoff and an analytic isolated-band eigenvector have moments C^(b+1)(b!)^2. Adjoin each leg as an additional scale-zero edge in Section 3 and apply the same argument. This costs O(P) extra edges. An arbitrary preassigned conversion leg known only to have moments through order four does not satisfy this hypothesis merely by being smooth. The latter decorated-endpoint class is not covered by the present proof without additional input.

## 9. What this does and does not establish about Lemma N

[proved, general-tree conclusion in a fixed admissible scheme] For exponential endpoint measures, fixed Gevrey cutoff covariances, and no explicit engineering-derivative interaction endpoints, (20) proves the hidden-moment estimate and the source-free version of (17) for general GN trees. Both branching and arbitrary endpoint degree are included. This is a different proof mechanism from uniform edge loads. Loads can grow, and their constants are paid by the separately identified source-to-birth gain.

[proved, explicit engineering-derivative bookkeeping] An explicit local spatial-derivative quadratic endpoint has one engineering derivative, which is retained on its original field label. Let e_w be the number of such derivatives on the surviving fields at w, and a_w the number of inherited localization derivatives before its remainder. Factor both types of momentum monomial for the exact algebra, but record their dimensions separately. Equation (3) then uses

    r_w=max(0,m_w-e_w-a_w).                              (24)

The total derivative degree on each field is still at most two. If r_w>0, the total output degree is m_w<=2; if r_w=0, it does not increase. Consequently the same covariance bound with a<=4 applies. The moment births still obey r_w<=2, and every argument in Sections 3, 5, and 6 is unchanged.

The localization birth-to-sink coverage alone is now

    G_w^loc >= max(0,m_w-e_w).                           (25)

An engineering derivative born at a local endpoint of scale k_i and ending at contraction or root scale s_i has factor E_(s_i). Extract its endpoint engineering dimension E_(k_i) once. The remaining factor is E_(s_i)/E_(k_i). Its path crosses exactly those cuts on which that engineering derivative survives. Hence

    sum_i(s_i-k_i)=-sum_w e_w ell_w.                     (26)

Combining (25)-(26) with the localization paths gives coverage at least m_w. The full version of (20), with endpoint engineering dimensions extracted once, is

    sum_A |c_A| product_e(b_e!)^2
      2^[sum_localization j(s_j-t_e(j))
                         +sum_engineering i(s_i-k_i)]
       <= C^(P+n) product_w 2^(-m_w ell_w).              (27)

This is proved by the same surplus argument. No new coordinate moment is attached to an engineering derivative. Its factor in (26) is its actual transported engineering dimension, not an additional localization gain.

[proved, equivalent dimensional form] If the engineering factors are instead kept in the canonical dimension on each cut, that dimension is p_w/2-2+e_w. The localization gain furnished by (25) gives

    p_w/2-2+e_w+max(0,m_w-e_w)
       >= p_w/2-2+m_w.                                  (28)

This proves the required source-free dimensional bound also in C60's explicit spatial-derivative convention. It avoids a change-of-scheme argument. In particular a surviving engineering derivative can lower the newly required Taylor order only while its engineering scale contribution is retained.

[proved, qualification on the literal display (17)] When e_w=0, (27) is the localization-only display C60 (17). With explicit engineering endpoints, (27), or equivalently (25) together with (28), is the statement proved here. Dropping the engineering factor in (27) while keeping the full demand m_w would be a stronger, different inequality. This note does not assert that stronger localization-only inequality. This distinction makes the engineering convention explicit rather than using it to hide a missing gain.

[assumed, corrected Lemma N remains incomplete] C61 separates N-action, N-comparison, and N-source. The present theorem supplies the missing general-tree moment estimate for N-action in the stated scheme, with the qualifications about conversion legs and the explicit engineering bookkeeping above. It does not establish the signed constant-history reference cancellation, the scheme comparison needed for N-comparison, or the one- and two-source estimates. In the source statement the interaction-prefactored difference must remain

    D_O^int(u)=D_O(u)-D_O(0).

The full free lattice-to-reference descendant D_O(0) remains separate. It is not removed by the source-free tree theorem. Accordingly this run does not complete the requested full corrected Lemma N and does not produce its alternative counterexample.

## 10. Checks and provenance

[proved, limiting checks] If no moment is created, D=M=0 and the routing estimate is trivial. A moment paid at its birth scale has no surplus; at most two moments per scale can do this on one edge. A load b=1 or b=2 has q(b)=0 and costs only a fixed constant. For b=3 the minimum surplus is one, and for b=4 it is two. At large b the quadratic growth in (13) dominates the logarithmic factorial growth per moment. All estimates use dimensionless scale differences. Vanishing endpoint displacements kill their positive-order terms, as required by contact localization.

[proved, ladder check] For a ladder route with one endpoint moment, b=1 and the full birth-to-root path gives the usual 2^h gain. The new proof allows extra routes whose factorial costs are bounded by the surplus argument. It does not need multiplication in a single conserved pair momentum. Thus the geometric and scale argument, rather than the pair structure, is what extends to branching.

[record, primary-source context] Benfatto and Mastropietro keep weighted propagators in their tree bound and state paired localization scales in Sections 3.10-3.15 of their primary paper, https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0004226. Those passages were checked through the browser. The surplus estimate (13)-(20) and the convex-routing induction are derived here; that paper is not invoked as an arbitrary-endpoint theorem.
